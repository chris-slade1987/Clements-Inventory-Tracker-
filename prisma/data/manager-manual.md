## 1. Manager Expectations

Manager Expectations & Responsibilities

At Clements Pest Control, our managers are far more than just supervisors—they are the driving force behind the strength, consistency, and culture of our teams. As a manager, you are the bridge between field operations and leadership, and your ability to lead with clarity, accountability, and integrity directly impacts the performance and morale of your branch.

We expect our managers to lead by example—upholding our standards, modeling professionalism, and holding their teams accountable. Whether you're meeting with technicians to review performance, checking in with the operations team, or preparing for an all-hands meeting, your presence sets the tone. Being organized, communicative, and responsive is just as important as being knowledgeable about pest control services or operational procedures.

Leadership at Clements involves both proactive and reactive responsibilities. On any given day, a manager may need to pivot between resolving a customer concern, supporting a technician in the field, organizing inventory, or responding to a cancellation in a way that protects the relationship. Managers are expected to keep a pulse on the data—reviewing KPIs regularly, preparing for leadership meetings with insights and trends, and ensuring records and systems are kept up to date.

The role also comes with a responsibility to develop and maintain the operational health of your branch. That includes staying on top of equipment maintenance, ensuring workplace safety, maintaining a clean and organized environment, and preparing the team for everything from hurricane season to quarterly audits. Managers are expected to understand and enforce company policies and maintain compliance with industry regulations, including FDACS documentation and safety requirements.

Perhaps most importantly, Clements managers are people-first leaders. That means being present during the hiring and onboarding process, supporting team development through regular coaching and training, and handling discipline or conflict with fairness and professionalism. You're expected to represent the company both internally and externally, with a consistent voice and a high level of customer service. Communication—both with your team and with customers—should always be clear, respectful, and timely.

Ultimately, being a manager at Clements Pest Control means taking ownership—not just of your branch’s performance, but of its culture, its reputation, and its growth. It means showing up each day with the mindset of a leader: thoughtful, thorough, and committed to building something stronger with every interaction.

## Hiring / Onboarding

> **Portal (v1).** Hiring and onboarding now run in the portal. **Hiring / ATS** lets HR post jobs, move candidates through the pipeline (applied → screening → interviewing → offer → onboarding → hired), and assign interviews with required digital **scorecards**. Every open job now has a **public application link** (a "front door"): each job detail page shows two copyable links — one for the **Indeed** ad and one for the **company website/careers page** — that lead to a clean, branded, no-login apply page for that role. When someone applies, a candidate is created automatically in that job's pipeline (stage **Applied**), their résumé is stored, their **source** is tracked by channel (Indeed vs. Company Website), the applicant gets an automatic confirmation email, and HR plus the job's branch supervisors are notified. A public **careers page** at `/careers` lists all open roles for the company website to link to. When a candidate reaches the offer/onboarding stage, the **online onboarding** portal takes over — the new hire completes paperwork through a personal link, including a typed-signature **acknowledgment of the Employee Handbook**. New hires also receive **30- and 60-day reviews** in the portal, with assigned reviewers and e-signatures. The roadmap and email templates below remain the reference for the human steps.

> **Portal (v7) — the applicant pipeline.** The job container (**People / HR → Hiring / Jobs → open a job**) now groups every candidate under the stage they're in — **Applied · Screening · Interview · Ranked · Selected · Pre-hire** — with a retained **Excluded** section beneath. HR drives the pipeline across all branches; the supervisor HR assigns to a job works only inside that job.
>
> - **Shortlist & screen.** On an applicant, HR clicks **Shortlist → Screening**. **Request screening call** emails the candidate your Google **Appointment Schedule** booking link (set it once on the Hiring / Jobs page — in Google Calendar, *Create → Appointment schedule*, then paste the public link; no third-party scheduler). HR records **screening notes** and marks the call **complete** on the candidate, then **Advance → Interview**.
> - **Interview handoff.** On the job page, HR picks the **interviewing supervisor** and an **interview deadline**. Every candidate in the Interview stage is handed to that supervisor (who gets container access), and the supervisor is notified with the candidate list and the deadline. The supervisor **logs the confirmed interview time** on each candidate and completes the **standardized interview questionnaire** (the same scorecard: eight competencies, a recommendation, and a summary). If the deadline passes with interviews unlogged, the job is flagged.
> - **No-show / exclude.** Excluding a candidate is **never a delete** — it's a retained, reason-tagged archive. The exclude control shows only the reasons that fit the current stage (application vs. screening vs. interview; the interview set includes **No-show**). "Other" needs a note. Everything cut lands in the job's **Excluded** section and the global **Excluded archive** (Hiring / Jobs → Excluded archive), with the reason, the stage it was cut at, the date, and who did it. HR/admin can **Reactivate** anyone back into a chosen stage if a pick falls through.
> - **Ranking → selection.** After interviews, the supervisor submits a **forced ranking** (top 3 required; no-shows must be excluded first). Submitting notifies HR, the CEO, and the supervisor, and opens a **48-hour selection window**. HR then **selects the finalist** — the other ranked candidates are automatically excluded ("Not selected"), kept **warm**, and sent a warm rejection email inviting them to reconnect about future roles. HR reaches out to the finalist directly, then clicks **Move to pre-hire**, which starts the existing online-onboarding packet (the pre-hire boundary; onboarding paperwork continues as described above).

> **Portal (v8) — the editable Hiring Template Library.** The interview questionnaire and the HR screening-call script are no longer hard-coded — HR/admin build and edit them in the **Hiring Template Library** (**People / HR → Hiring / Jobs → Template Library**). Templates come in two kinds — **Interview** and **Screening call** — and are assignable by role/worker type (Pest Technician, Sales/Service Advisor, Service/Branch Manager, CSR, or a general default).
>
> - **Starts stocked.** The library ships with off-the-shelf, best-practice templates for each role plus a general **default** of each kind, and a categorized **question bank** to browse. These are the starting point — HR edits, adds, duplicates, or deactivates freely (nothing is ever hard-deleted; a retired template just goes **inactive**).
> - **Build a template three ways.** In the template editor, add a question **(1) from the off-the-shelf bank** (browse by category/role and insert), **(2) custom** (type your own; choose the response type — 1–5 rating, yes/no, free text, or a basics Yes/No/Unsure check), or **(3) with the AI assistant** — *Draft with AI* opens a helper where you describe the role and what you want to assess (or paste a rough question) and it returns polished, role-grounded, legally-mindful suggestions to insert and edit. Reorder questions, edit inline, and delete. **AI drafting needs the Anthropic key configured (in Vercel); the off-the-shelf bank and custom questions always work without it.**
> - **Assign to a job.** On the job page, **Hiring templates** lets HR pick the interview + screening template for that job (or leave on **Auto** to use the role-matched template, then the default). The supervisor **interview questionnaire** and the HR **screening call** then render the assigned template's questions. Already-completed scorecards keep rendering exactly as saved.
> - **Who can edit.** Only HR/admin manage templates and use AI drafting. The interviewing supervisor still only **fills** the assigned questionnaire inside their job container — they never edit templates.
> - **Legally mindful.** The AI assistant is instructed to keep questions job-related and behavioral and to **avoid protected-class / ADA-risky wording** (no age, disability/health, family status, etc.); it assesses job requirements by condition ("this role requires lifting ~50 lb… with or without a reasonable accommodation") rather than probing health. HR still reviews every question before saving.

## Technician Uniform Protocols

As part of the onboarding process, all technicians are given five shirts, five pairs of pants, a belt, a jacket, and your choice between two styles of hats. Each year, every branch is given a designated month where they can place orders for replacing worn out items. Please see your supervisor to find out which month is designated for your branch.

During the annual “ordering period” for your branch, technicians are allowed to replace the following:

- Every year - Up to three shirts, a hat, and a pair of shoes (shoes for lawn techs only)
- Every two years - Belts and up to three pairs of pants
- Every three years - jackets

Wearing a Company Issued Hat

Wearing a hat with our company logo helps to identify you as a member of our team and promotes our brand. If you choose to wear a hat while working, Clements provides you with a choice between a moisture wicking, floppy brim hat that helps protect your skin from the sun, or we also provide trucker style ball caps with netting ventilation. As with all other uniform items, it is not acceptable to wear any other type of hat unless approved by your supervisor. Exceptions may include larger, straw hats that protect from the sun or beanies during unusually low temperatures. That being said, any deviations from company issued attire must be approved by your supervisor.

Tuck in Your Shirt

Your uniform shirt should always be tucked in, regardless of the type of pants you're wearing. You should also wear a belt to hold your shirt and pants in place.

Wearing a Company Issued Belt

Your uniform belt should match the color of your shoes and be the correct size.Once you are issued a gray woven belt by the company, it's important to wear it as it can help to identify you as a member of our team.

Wearing a Company Issued Jacket

In addition to the uniform, a pest control technician may be issued a company jacket for colder weather. It is important to wear the jacket as instructed by the company policy. The jacket should be clean and in good condition. It should also have the company logo or name clearly visible. The jacket should be worn over the uniform, zipped up, and with the hood tucked away when not in use.

Wearing Black Boots/ Sneakers

Footwear is an important part of your uniform. Your shoes should be comfortable, sturdy, and appropriate for the job. Black work boots or black sneakers are acceptable as they are versatile, provide ankle support and are easy to maintain. It is the technicians responsibility to obtain footwear that is approved by the Director of Field Operations.

Non-Company Approved Apparel

It is important to remember that only company-approved apparel should be worn while on the job. Personal clothing items should not be visible while performing pest control services. Wearing non-company-approved apparel may give the impression of an unprofessional image, which could negatively impact the company's reputation. It is important to adhere to the dress code policy and represent the company in a professional manner at all times.

## Rain Day Policy

PurposeTo provide clear guidance on how to manage and reschedule service appointments impacted by inclement weather, ensuring that no services are missed or left unaccounted for.

Policy Overview

When rain or weather conditions prevent you from completing scheduled services, it is your responsibility to promptly reschedule and complete those services in a timely and organized manner.

Rescheduling Guidelines

- Immediate Action
  - Use PestPac Mobile to move affected service orders forward to a future date.
  - Do not leave any jobs on past dates—every missed service must be rescheduled.
- Completion Window
  - All missed services due to weather must be completed within 7 to 10 business days of the originally scheduled date.
- Customer Communication
  - If a customer requires confirmation prior to service, you must contact them directly before rescheduling.
  - If no confirmation is required, reschedule for the next available day you’ll be servicing their area.
- Catch-Up Opportunities You may use end-of-month “extra days” to catch up on missed stops in cases of:
  - Extended rain events
  - Return from Paid Time Off (PTO)
  - Major service disruptions (e.g., hurricanes or other natural events)
- Weekend Work
  - While Saturday work is not required, there may be occasions when you’re asked to work a weekend to help stay on schedule—especially if significantly behind due to weather or other delays.
  - Your flexibility is greatly appreciated in these cases and helps support the team and customers.

### Key Expectations

- No service orders should remain on a past calendar date.
- All missed services should be rescheduled and completed within the same calendar month when possible.
- Maintain clear communication with both customers and supervisors as needed.
- If you need assistance adjusting your route or rescheduling stops, contact your supervisor for support.

Why It Matters

Proactive rescheduling ensures we stay accountable to our customers, prevents service lapses, and helps maintain trust and reliability across all routes.

## Callback Policy

Purpose To ensure timely, consistent, and professional handling of all callbacks in order to maintain high levels of customer satisfaction and service reliability.

### Policy Overview

All callbacks—regardless of whether they are received through the office or directly from a customer—must be responded to by the end of the same business day.

### Procedure for Responding to Callbacks

- Initial Response
  - Attempt to call the customer first.
  - If the customer does not answer, leave a voicemail, send a text message, and follow up with an email.
  - This creates a clear and documented trail of communication efforts.
- Contact Made
  - Once you reach the customer, schedule the callback for a mutually convenient time.

## Company Vehicle Cleaning Procedures

To maintain this high standard, all technicians are required to clean and wash their vehicle every Friday afternoon upon returning to their local office from the field. Supervisors conduct monthly inspections of each vehicle to ensure that you maintain your service vehicle and equipment to the highest standards, as well as checking to make sure you are storing chemicals and equipment properly. Provided below is a detailed checklist of how we expect you to clean your vehicle each week.

The checklist below provides a detailed guide to maintaining the standard that we expect from you each week.

Vehicle Exterior Cleaning:

- Start by rinsing the vehicle with a hose to remove any loose dirt.
- Wash the vehicle with soap and water, making sure to clean all exposed (painted) areas, including the hood, roof, sides, bumpers, and lips of fender wells.
- Wash the windshield, side windows, rear window, and mirrors, including the backside of mirrors where bugs collect.
- Remove grease and brake dust from the hubcaps and lips of fender wells.
- Rinse all soapy areas with a hose to prevent streaking.
- Inspect the headlights and front bumper to ensure all bug splatters are removed.
- Dry the wet exterior with a towel, paying special attention to spots like the roof line just above the windows that could spill excess water later when you move the vehicle.

Toolbox and Bed of Vehicle:

- Organize all aerosol cans.
- Clean the B&G with a damp cloth if necessary, ensuring that there are no leakages in the bottom of the toolbox.
- Check the lock to make sure it closes and locks properly, and ensure that all chemicals are properly labeled and legible.
- Mark all water containers with "H2O," and ensure that MSDS and spill kits are stored and complete.
- Remove any empty cans, containers, or waste from the toolbox and bed of the vehicle.
- Make one final lap around the vehicle to inspect the exterior for missed areas, and open both doors to inspect the interior for any missed areas.

Vehicle Interior Cleaning:

- Remove all trash from inside the vehicle, including papers or materials on the dashboard or in the visors.
- Sweep or vacuum the floorboard.
- Clean the dash, doors and handles, vinyl surfaces, and plastic trim with a damp cloth, ensuring that all grease and oils are removed from the steering wheel and dash buttons.
- Wipe down cup holders and remove any dry liquid stains, and wipe down the center console if applicable.
- Wipe down the white door jams and ensure no dirt is visible.
- Clean the floor mats, if applicable, by shaking them and wiping them down with a damp cloth.
- Move the seats all the way forward and then all the way back to get the nose of the vacuum down in the cracks. Check for any trash behind the seat and tidy any stored equipment while the seats are in the forward position.
- Wipe down the floorboard and hard-to-reach areas under the seat with a damp cloth.
- Do not place hats or cloth materials on the dashboard where they will fade due to sun damage.

## Reporting a Workplace Accident

In the event that you injure yourself while on the job, it is important to report the injury to your supervisor or manager as soon as possible. Here are the steps to follow:

Evaluate Yourself:

If the injury is serious, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room. If the injury is minor, contact your supervisor and seek treatment from a first aid kit at your office, or seek treatment from a designated medical professional (as directed by management). In the event that the injury is minor, an urgent care facility is recommended for treatment. Never ignore an injury, as it can lead to further complications.

Notify Your Supervisor or Manager:

Fill Out a Workplace Accident Report:

Your employer should provide you with a form to report workplace injuries. Complete the form as soon as possible and provide as much detail as possible. Be sure to include any medical documentation, such as doctor's notes or medical bills. These can be found at every Clement office location. In the event we need to file a workers compensation claim on your behalf, you may need to provide additional information to the insurance company. Following the injury, an insurance adjuster will be assigned to your claim and provide you with further details, including medical options, workplace duty limitations, results of your drug test, etc.

In conclusion, workplace injuries can have serious consequences. By prioritizing safety and reporting injuries promptly, you can help protect yourself and your colleagues from harm. Remember to follow all safety guidelines and take the necessary precautions to ensure a safe working environment.

## Inventory Process & Procedure

> **Portal update (v1).** Inventory is now tracked in the Clements Command & Control portal — not in a spreadsheet or the PestPac Inventory Module. The portal is the system of record for on-hand stock at every branch. This replaces the previous PestPac-based weekly process described in earlier versions of this manual.

### How stock is tracked

Every stock change is a **movement**. On-hand for a product at a warehouse is the running **sum of its movements** — check-ins add, check-outs subtract, and reconciliation adjustments go either way. On-hand is never edited directly, and movements are never deleted; corrections are made with offsetting **adjustments and reversals** so the full audit trail stays intact.

### Check-IN (receiving from a distributor)

- Go to **Check-In** and upload the distributor invoice.
- The **AI invoice reader** extracts the line items (product, quantity, unit, price).
- Review the extracted lines and **confirm**. Confirmed lines post as positive stock movements into the receiving warehouse.
- Any new or unrecognized product lands in a **confirm queue** (Manage → Products / Confirm). Unconfirmed products are **blocked from check-out** until an admin reviews and confirms them, so a mis-read or brand-new item can never be dispensed before it has been vetted.

### Check-OUT (dispensing to a technician)

- Go to **Check-Out** and select the **warehouse**, the **technician**, and the **products**.
- Quantities use a **canonical unit-of-measure dropdown — there is no free-text unit**. Every quantity is recorded in a governed unit, which is what makes on-hand totals reliable across branches. This replaces the old step of hand-keying chemical handout data into a spreadsheet.
- Confirmed check-out lines post as negative stock movements.

### Over-dispense hard stop

If a check-out would drive on-hand **negative**, the portal **blocks it** with a pop-up. **No one can override the block by dispensing into the negative.** The pop-up explains the next steps: recount the shelf, re-log what was actually taken, or reconcile the discrepancy. From the block you can start a **one-click escalation group chat** to senior management — the **Chief of Staff** (Julie Glanville), the **Director of Field Operations** (Graham Foster), and the **COO** (Chris Slade) — so a real shortage is surfaced immediately instead of being papered over.

### Month-end physical reconciliation

Physical counts are reconciled on the **Reconcile** screen. You enter the counted quantity for each product and the portal writes the **adjustment (or reversal)** needed to bring recorded on-hand in line with the physical count. Nothing is overwritten — every reconciliation entry is an attributed movement with a **"logged by"** note showing who made it, so the audit trail is complete. As the weekly/monthly process states: if something looks wrong, investigate before adjusting.

A full **company-wide physical count** is trued up the same way: on-hand for every product at every branch is set to the counted quantity by writing dated **adjustment** movements (counted − recorded), so nothing is edited directly and the audit trail is preserved. The first such count (dated **July 27, 2026**, all four branches) was loaded this way; future dated counts are applied identically.

### Approved-product catalog, units & divisions

The portal maintains the CEO's **approved-product catalog** with governed units and case **pack sizes**. Every product is classified by **line-of-service division** — **GHP** (General Household Pest), **L&O** (Lawn & Ornamental), **Mosquito**, **Termite**, **Rodent**, or **Other** — so purchasing and usage can be read by division.

### Alerts

The **Alerts** screen watches your inventory automatically (checks run on each check-in and on demand via "Run checks now"):

- **Low stock / reorder** — learns each product's usage rate and reorder cadence and flags items at or below their reorder point (critical when already out).
- **Price increase** — flags a distributor's unit price rising above its trailing average by more than the configurable threshold (default 10%).
- **Duplicate invoice** — catches a repeated distributor invoice number, or a matching date + total.
- **Negative stock** — flags any product showing below zero on hand.
- **Quantity spike** — flags a check-out far larger than a product's norm.
- **Cost-saving opportunity** — surfaces a cheaper equivalent product or the same product cheaper from another distributor.

Acknowledge, dismiss, or **Discuss** (open a thread) any alert. This is how purchasing patterns are spotted and how reordering is driven day to day.

## Paid Time Off (PTO)

> **Portal (v1).** PTO is requested and approved in the portal.

Time off is handled through the portal rather than by email or spreadsheet:

- **Requesting.** An employee submits a date range and type from their **Time Off** screen. The request goes to their branch supervisor for approval.
