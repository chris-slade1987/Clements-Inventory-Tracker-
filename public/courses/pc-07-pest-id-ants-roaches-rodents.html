Why this course matters

Wrong ID, wrong treatment. Every time.

A German roach breeds in the kitchen; a Florida woods roach wandered in from the mulch. Treat them the same and one customer calls back angry.

"Correct identification is the whole job. Name the species first, then the treatment plan almost writes itself."CLEMENTS ROACH IDENTIFICATION · AUG 2026

The wrong product can make it worse

A repellent spray on pharaoh or ghost ants can split one colony into several. Spray over roach bait and they stop eating it. Misidentification doesn't just fail; it spreads the problem.

The label and the law expect it

Labels list target pests and sites. Applying "just in case" to the wrong pest wastes product, adds exposure and isn't IPM. Florida's GHP category (Chapter 482) assumes you know your pests.

It's what the customer is paying for

Fire ant stings, roach allergens, rodent-chewed wiring. Naming the species and explaining it in plain words is what separates a Clements technician from a can of bug spray.

01 · The four-step ID method

Same four steps on every account

1

Confirm the group

Ant, termite, roach, beetle, rodent? Elbowed antennae and a pinched waist mean ant. Long antennae, flat oval body and no jumping legs mean roach.

2

Look closely

Use a 10X or stronger hand lens. Ants: count the nodes, check color, spines and worker sizes. Roaches: read the shield behind the head and the wings.

3

Read place and behavior

Where it nests, when it forages and how it moves. A tidy trail, an erratic swarm, a high cabinet or a drain all narrow the list.

4

Confirm, then record

Not sure? Collect a specimen or photo and ask your supervisor before treating. Put the species, not "bugs," in your notes.

Hand lens 10X or stronger on your keyring, every day.
Flashlight for cracks, voids and the dark corner you'd rather skip.
Photo close up, with a coin or ruler for scale.
Specimen in a vial, or on clear tape stuck to a card.

02 · Peridomestic roaches

Big roaches: they live outside and wander in

"Palmetto bug" is a nickname for any big roach, not a species. The Oriental roach on our Pest ID chart is uncommon in Florida; a big, dark roach with short wing pads here is almost always a Florida woods roach.

03 · What it means for the customer

Same word, "roaches." Two very different conversations.

Indoor breeders · German, brown-banded

"They're living and breeding in the home."

  • Signs inside: droppings like pepper, egg cases, shed skins, nymphs of all sizes
  • Came in on groceries, boxes, used appliances or furniture; not a sign of a dirty home
  • Interior bait in harborage, monitors, sanitation partnership; expect follow-up visits as eggs hatch
  • Ask them not to spray store products over our bait. Heavy cases go to a cleanout (PC-08)
Peridomestic · American, smokybrown, Australian, Florida woods, Asian

"They live outside. Some get in by accident."

  • Seen at night, after rain or cold snaps; usually adults, no egg cases inside
  • Coming from mulch, palms, tree holes, gutters, drains and sewers
  • Exterior treatment, door sweeps, gaps, moisture, lighting and roofline checks
  • One big roach indoors is a visitor. Egg cases or nymphs inside means look for an indoor source: drains, attic, garage
"This is a smokybrown roach. They live in the trees and gutters and come in at the roofline at night. It isn't breeding in your kitchen. I've treated the eaves and entry points, and if you can clear those gutters it will cut down how many find their way in."

04 · From ID to service

The species tells you where to look and what to offer

Roof rat

Inspect up: roofline, soffits, gable vents, A/C chase, palms and limbs touching the roof, attic insulation runways.

Season: the fall push-in. Attic noise in October is the classic call.

Norway rat

Inspect low: foundation, slab cracks, burrows under sheds and decks, garage door corners, dumpster areas, utility penetrations near the ground.

Season: cooler months; year-round near food.

House mouse

Inspect close: within 10–30 feet of the droppings. Appliances, cabinets, drawers, wall voids behind the kitchen, garage storage.

Season: fall push-in; year-round indoors.

Seal before you bait. Activity inside means inspection, exclusion and trapping through Rodent Shield. Baiting with entry points open can leave a rodent dying in a wall. Tiers and warranty: CW-05. Pricing goes to the branch manager.
Measure every gap. Rats fit through about ½ inch, mice about ¼ inch. Photograph each entry point with a scale. Foam alone is not exclusion; rodents chew through it.
