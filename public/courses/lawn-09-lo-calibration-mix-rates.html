L&O Calibration & Mix Rates

How to calibrate the hose reel, backpack and spreader, do the math for a 500-gallon tank, mix it in the right order, and prove every lawn got the label rate.

Course objectives

By the end of this course you will be able to:

Read a turf label's rate per 1,000 sq ft, carrier volume and maximums, and check them against the Clements program sheet
Calibrate a hose-reel gun on a timed 1,000 sq ft test plot and set your walking pace to hit 3.5 gallons per 1,000 sq ft
Work out product per tank, per 100 gallons and per 1,000 sq ft for a 500-gallon reel, the SG52 and a backpack
Mix a tank in the correct order (W-A-L-E-S or the label's order) and run a jar test before a new tank mix
Calibrate a rotary spreader and convert a fertilizer analysis into pounds of nitrogen per 1,000 sq ft
Record what you actually applied, and keep a calibration log for every piece of equipment
Why this course matters

A 500-gallon mistake covers three acres.

A lawn rig carries enough mix for dozens of yards. One wrong number at the fill station follows you to every stop that day.

Over-application

A FIFRA and Chapter 482 violation. Burned St. Augustine, nutrient runoff into Florida canals and lagoons, and a lawn we now have to repair.

Under-application

Chinch bugs and weeds survive, the customer sees it from the window, and we pay for a second trip. Weak rates also help pests and weeds build tolerance.

The certified operator's license

Florida holds the certified operator in charge responsible for correct concentrations and formulations (482.152). Your calibration and your math protect that license.

01 · Four numbers on a turf label

Rate, carrier, mix and maximum

Rate per area

Product on the ground

Fl oz or lb of product per 1,000 sq ft or per acre. This is the legal number.

Carrier volume

Water that delivers it

Gallons of finished spray per 1,000 sq ft. Clements programs use 3.5.

Mix

Product per tank

Fl oz per 100 gallons or per gallon. Set by the rate and the carrier together.

Maximums

Lines you can't cross

Top rate per application, per year, days between, wind, water buffers.

Product per 1,000 sq ft = mix per gallon × gallons applied per 1,000 sq ft. The tank mix only means something at the carrier volume it was written for. If your rig puts out more water than that, every lawn gets more product than the sheet intends.

01 · Same tank, different dose

The tank was right. The lawn wasn't.

02 · The hose-reel method

Gallons per minute, then gallons per 1,000 sq ft

  1. 1Prep. Clean water in the tank. Check the screen, gun tip and hose. Run the pump at the pressure you spray at on the route.
  2. 2Catch test. Spray into a marked bucket for 30 seconds. Fl oz × 2 = fl oz per minute; ÷ 128 = gallons per minute (GPM). Repeat three times and average.
  3. 3Mark a 1,000 sq ft test plot on turf, for example 20 ft × 50 ft.
  4. 4Time yourself covering it evenly at your normal pace and pattern, with your normal overlap.
  5. 5Calculate: GPM × minutes = gallons per 1,000 sq ft. Compare it with 3.5.
  6. 6Adjust and repeat. Pace first, then tip or pressure. Log the date, gun, tip, pressure and GPM.
The math
GPM = fl oz in 30 s × 2 ÷ 128
gal per 1,000 = GPM × minutes per 1,000 sq ft
target time = 3.5 ÷ GPM (minutes)
Worked example

96 fl oz in 30 s → 192 fl oz per min ÷ 128 = 1.5 GPM.
The plot took 2 min 20 s = 2.33 min.
1.5 × 2.33 = 3.5 gal per 1,000 sq ft. On target.

04 · Backpack and SG52

Same idea, different tools

Backpack, broadcast

Catch 30 s and double it for fl oz per minute. Time a 250 sq ft plot. 40 fl oz per min × 1 min on 250 sq ft = 160 fl oz per 1,000 = 1.25 gal. Mix for that volume, not the rig's 3.5.

Backpack, spot

Spot labels give a mix per gallon and say to wet the weed or the target evenly, without runoff. You still need a calibrated pace so small spots don't get drenched.

Steel Green SG52

Twin 30-gallon tanks (60 gal), a 52-inch boom and its own hose reel. Calibrate the boom at the ground speed and pressure you use: time a measured run, catch every nozzle, average.

One backpack mix, two volumes. A product at 0.15 fl oz per 1,000 sq ft is 0.15 ÷ 1.25 = 0.12 fl oz per gallon at 1.25 gal, but 0.043 per gallon at 3.5.
Herbicide in the backpack? Never in a sprayer that also sprays shrubs. Label it, rinse it, and keep it for herbicide only (LAWN-06).
05 · What changes your output

Know what moves the number

Walking pace

Output per minute is fixed. Walk slower and every square foot gets more.

Pressure

More pressure, more output and finer drops that drift. Spray at the pressure you calibrated at.

Tip and wear

Worn tips spray more and spray unevenly. Replace a tip that reads 10% over a new one.

Hose and screens

Kinks, a longer hose run or a clogged screen cut output. Check them on every fill.

Recalibrate at the start of each season, after changing a gun, tip, hose or pump, after any repair, when you change pressure, and whenever the tank runs out early or late.
Coarse drops, low to the turf. Keep the gun pointed down. Bifen XTS and most turf labels prohibit spraying in wind over 10 mph or toward water.

01 · Units and conversions

Measure it. Never eyeball it.

Graduated cylinder or pitcher marked in fl oz and mL, used only for pesticide. Read it level, at eye level.
Syringe for small amounts, such as a backpack dose or a jar test.
Know the tank level. Use the sight gauge or marks, not a guess. Gallons in the tank drive every number.
Never use kitchen tools, or mix on pavement or near a drain. Rinse the measure into the tank.

02 · Three formulas

From the label rate to the tank

Area a tank covers
sq ft = tank gal ÷ carrier × 1,000

500 gal ÷ 3.5 × 1,000 = 142,857 sq ft. The SG52's 60 gal covers 17,143 sq ft.

Product for the tank
product = sq ft ÷ 1,000 × rate

Bifen XTS at 0.14 fl oz per 1,000: 142.857 × 0.14 = 20 fl oz for a full 500 gal.

Product per 100 gallons
per 100 gal = rate × 100 ÷ carrier

0.14 × 100 ÷ 3.5 = 4 fl oz per 100 gal, the top of the program sheet.

Partial tanks. 120 gal of mix left and you top up to 500? Add product only for the 380 new gallons: 3.8 × 4 = 15.2 fl oz.
One lawn. 8,500 sq ft at 0.75 oz per 1,000 = 8.5 × 0.75 = 6.38 oz in 8.5 × 3.5 = 29.75 gal of spray.

05 · Mixing order · select the steps in order

Fill a hot tank, step by step

W · A · L · E · S

Wettable powders, dry flowables, water-dispersible granules · Agitate (and any defoamer or buffer the label calls for) · Liquid flowables, suspensions and solutions · Emulsifiable concentrates · Surfactants last

The label's order wins. The Bifen XTS label lists: wettable powders, agitate, liquids and flowables, agitate, then emulsifiable concentrates. The formulation code is on each label: WP, DF or WDG, SC or F, EC, SL.
Keep agitation running from the first product until the tank is empty. Mix what you'll use today; many labels say not to let a tank mix stand overnight.
Fill with an air gap or check valve so mix can't siphon back into the water supply. Goggles and label PPE when pouring concentrate (FC-03). Spill: the 3 Cs and your supervisor (FC-05).
06 · The jar test

Find a bad mix in a quart jar, not a 500-gallon tank

  1. 1Label PPE. A clear quart jar with a lid, used only for this.
  2. 2Fill it 1/2 to 2/3 with the same water you fill the tank with.
  3. 3Add each product in proportion, in mixing order. Shake after each one.
  4. 4Let it stand 5 to 15 minutes, then look and feel.
  5. 5Empty it into the spray tank, never down a drain.
Jar math · a quart is 1/400 of 100 gallons

0-0-25 at 42 fl oz per 100 gal: 42 ÷ 400 = 0.105 fl oz = 3.1 mL.
Bifen XTS at 4 fl oz per 100 gal: 4 ÷ 400 = 0.01 fl oz = 0.3 mL. Use a syringe.

07 · Adjuvants and surfactants

Only what the label calls for, at the label rate

Surfactants

Help spray wet waxy or hairy leaves like dollarweed and doveweed. Many post-emergent labels require a non-ionic surfactant (NIS), usually written as a % v/v.

Stickers

Help product stay on the leaf through irrigation and rain. Our program sheets add a sticker to most lawn and shrub tanks. Use the amount on the sheet and the sticker's label.

When not to add

Some products already contain surfactant, and some labels prohibit adding one. Extra surfactant in summer heat can burn St. Augustine and ornamentals.

Percent v/v math. 0.25% v/v = 0.25 ÷ 100 × 128 = 0.32 fl oz per gallon, or 32 fl oz per 100 gallons.
Example from a label: Bifen XTS recommends an NIS or silicone surfactant at 0.25% v/v when the carrier is below 2 gal per 1,000 sq ft. At our 3.5 gal, it is not required.

01 · Spreader calibration and nitrogen math

Pounds of product, then pounds of N

  1. 1Find the effective swath. Catch pans across the throw, or watch the pattern on a dark tarp. Space passes so the edges just overlap.
  2. 2Set the gate to the label or spreader-chart starting setting.
  3. 3Weigh the product, then spread a measured strip, for example 10 ft swath × 150 ft = 1,500 sq ft, at route speed.
  4. 4Weigh what's left. lb used ÷ sq ft × 1,000 = lb product per 1,000.
  5. 5Convert: lb product × N% ÷ 100 = lb N per 1,000. Adjust the gate and repeat until you're within 10%.
Worked example

15-0-15, target 0.5 lb N per 1,000.
0.5 ÷ 0.15 = 3.33 lb product per 1,000 sq ft.
A 6,500 sq ft lawn needs 6.5 × 3.33 = 21.7 lb. A 25 lb bag covers 25 ÷ 3.33 = 7,500 sq ft.

Per-application limits (UF/IFAS SL21, LAWN-05): no more than 0.7 lb of quick-release N per 1,000 sq ft; up to 2 lb N in spring or summer only with at least 65% slow-release; no more than 1 lb in fall. County rules can be stricter and ban N in summer.
Granules off walks and drives. Blow them back onto turf, and close the gate on turns and near water.

03 · Records and the daily routine

If it isn't recorded, it didn't happen

The service record

Product, amount actually used, rate, area treated, target pest or weed. Florida requires it within 2 working days and kept 2 years (5E-14.142). Enter it in PestPac Mobile at the stop.

The calibration log

Date, equipment, gun or nozzle tip, pressure, GPM, gal per 1,000 sq ft, spreader setting. Keep it with the equipment and redo it on schedule.

Notice of application

Post the filled-in notice in a conspicuous spot at the time of application (482.2265(2), 5E-14.147), and give the product name and active ingredient on request (482.2265).

Every new jug

Labels change. Check the rate on each new container against the program sheet before you mix from it.

Leftover mix

Apply it to a labeled site at or below the label rate. Never in a drain, on pavement or on the ground at the shop. Plan the last fill so little is left.

Unsure? Ask

Rates and products belong to the certified operator. Call your supervisor before you guess.
