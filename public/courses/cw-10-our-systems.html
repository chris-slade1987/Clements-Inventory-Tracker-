Customers trust us with more than pests

Our records hold names, addresses, phone numbers, gate and lockbox notes, pets, schedules and billing details. In the wrong hands that tells someone when a home is empty and how to get in.

The law expects us to protect it

Florida's Information Protection Act (s. 501.171, F.S.) requires businesses to take reasonable measures to protect customers' personal information and to notify them within 30 days of a breach.

The record is the proof

What gets posted in PestPac is how we show what was done, where and when. Late, wrong or missing entries mean missed services, billing errors and work we can't back up.

04 · Reading schedule codes

A schedule code tells you when a customer gets serviced

BJAN4THUR
CycleHow often
MonthThe month the cycle starts
WeekWhich week of the month
DayWhich weekday
BJAN4THUR = bi-monthly (every other month) starting in January, on the 4th Thursday: January, March, May, July, September and November.
CycleMeansExample
MMonthly, every monthStarts with M format to confirm
BBi-monthly, every other month (6 a year)BJAN4THUR
QQuarterly, every third month (4 a year)QJAN2TUES
AAnnual, once a yearA-FEB2TUES

Weekdays are written MON, TUES, WED, THUR, FRI. Week 4 means the 4th of that weekday in the month, not the last.

01 · Email and calendar · Google Workspace

Your @clementspestcontrol.com account

Gmail: write it like letterhead

  • Company email is company property, for company business. Messages are company records and may be reviewed. Don't expect privacy in it.
  • With customers and vendors: proper grammar and punctuation. No ALL CAPS, slang or emoji.
  • Use the company standard signature block. HR has the format.
  • Reply to office email during the business day, and clear anything left by end of day.
  • Never write anything you wouldn't want read aloud. Email can be forwarded, misdirected or used in a lawsuit.

Google Calendar

Meetings, trainings and appointments live here. Accept or decline invitations so the organizer knows, keep your own calendar current, and check it at the start of each day.

Technician notes for the office go to the Customer Service Team at technotes@clementspestcontrol.com. Urgent? Call the tech line, 772-469-2566.

01 · Passwords and 2-Step Verification

Long, unique, never shared, backed by a second step

Long beats clever. Use a passphrase of at least 15 characters, like four unrelated words. Federal guidance (NIST SP 800-63B) favors length over symbol swaps.
One account, one password. Never reuse your PestPac, Google, Paychex or CanopyOS password anywhere else. A breach on one site gets tried everywhere.
Never share a login, not with a coworker, a supervisor or anyone who calls or emails. Everything done under your login is recorded as you.
Turn on 2-Step Verification wherever it's offered. A stolen password alone won't get in. Approve only prompts you started.

04 · Devices and customer data

Lock it, guard it, share only what's needed

Lock every device

  • Screen lock on your phone, tablet and computer, with a PIN or biometric
  • Lock your computer when you step away (Windows key + L)
  • Lock the truck, and never leave a phone or laptop in view
  • Install updates when prompted

Customer information stays in our systems

  • Look up only the accounts your work requires
  • Never give one customer's details to another person, even a neighbor or "family member"
  • No photos of customers' homes on social media, per the handbook
  • No customer lists in personal email or personal apps

Payment details

  • Never write down, text, email or chat a full card number
  • Take payment only through the approved company process
  • Billing questions go to Finance & Accounting
  • Never read card details aloud where others can hear
Company devices and accounts are company property and may be monitored. Personal phones used for work follow the same professional standards. Lost or stolen device: tell your supervisor immediately.
05 · When something goes wrong

Clicked a bad link? Lost a phone? Speed matters more than blame.

Criminals move within minutes of getting a password. The person who reports a mistake right away is protecting the company. The one who hides it is giving the attacker time.

Never try to "fix it quietly" first. Report, then follow instructions.
  1. 1Stop. Don't enter anything else, don't reply, don't forward the message to coworkers.
  2. 2Tell your supervisor now, by phone or in person. Say what you clicked, entered or lost, and when.
  3. 3Change the password for that account from a device you trust, and anywhere you reused it.
  4. 4Report the message in Gmail (the three-dot menu, then Report phishing) so it's flagged.
  5. 5Watch for follow-up: unexpected 2-Step prompts, password reset emails, or changes in Paychex Flex. Report them too.
