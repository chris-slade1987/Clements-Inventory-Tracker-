The Standard L&O Visit

One lawn visit, step by step: walk the property, choose the right application, treat turf and shrubs by the label, post the notice, and leave a customer who knows exactly what we did.

Course objectives

By the end of this course you will be able to:

Walk a property and read turf color and density, weeds, insect and disease signs, irrigation problems and shrub health
Decide whether the truck's tank mix fits what you found, and spot-mix only from the current label
Recognize the conditions that mean hold, not apply: wind, rain, heat, stressed turf, people and pets
Apply to turf and ornamentals with an even pattern and overlap, keeping product off hard surfaces, water and beds it isn't labeled for
Post the lawn notice Florida requires and explain how the pesticide-sensitive registry works
Leave clear watering and mowing recommendations, flags, a door hanger and PestPac Mobile notes the customer can use
Why this course matters

A lawn that improves, plus communication, keeps a customer.

Customers don't read labels or see dilution rates. They see whether the lawn gets better, whether we looked professional and whether they understand what was done. We are not just lawn techs. We are property care professionals.

The weeds nobody explained

You spot-treat weeds on an insect visit but don't flag them, leave a note or document it. A week later the weeds are still standing. The CSR sees no mention of weeds. A callback is booked, or the customer cancels. Almost always preventable.

The law is in the yard

The label is federal law. Florida requires a posted notice on every lawn and exterior foliage application, and at least 24 hours' notice to nearby people on the pesticide-sensitive registry.

Everything drains somewhere

Drift, runoff and granules on the driveway end up in canals, ponds and groundwater. FDACS can test a property after a drift complaint.

The FC-11 arrival, with lawn questions

  1. 1Park in front of the house, in park, ignition off, seatbelt off. Never on the lawn or blocking the driveway.
  2. 2Start the order in PestPac Mobile before you walk up, so the service time is accurate.
  3. 3Knock and introduce yourself, unless the account says Do Not Knock or the customer asked for no contact.

FDACS ID card on you. Show it on request (s. 482.091). Give the product name, active ingredient and label safety information on request (s. 482.2265).
Ask before you walk
  • "Have you noticed anything in the lawn or plants?" Start your walk there.
  • "When does the irrigation run?" It explains wet, fungus-prone turf or dry strips, and decides your watering advice.
  • "When was it last mowed, and at what height?" Scalping looks like disease.
  • "Any pets or children using the yard today?" They stay off treated areas until the label allows.
  • "Any new plantings, edibles or a garden?" No spray unless the label lists them.

02 · What to inspect

Six things to read on every walk

Turf color and density

Even green and thick, or thin, off-color, patchy? Note where: sun, shade, along pavement.

Weeds

Broadleaf, grassy or sedge, and how much. Weeds in beds are a different job than weeds in turf (LAWN-06).

Insect signs

Damage at the edge of the green, chewed blades, frass, moths at dusk. Part the grass and look (LAWN-07).

Disease signs

Rings, spots, leaf lesions, blades that pull loose. Note the season, shade and moisture (LAWN-08).

Irrigation

Broken or tilted heads, dry strips, puddles, water on pavement, runs during rain.

Shrubs and beds

Black sooty coating, whiteflies, scale, leaf spots, chewed leaves, mulch piled on stems.

And read the site for safety: ponds, canals, seawalls and drains; pools and play areas; edible gardens; pets, bowls and toys; wind direction. These decide where you won't spray.

Part two

Choose and mix the right application

Why it matters: the right product at the wrong rate, on the wrong grass or in the wrong weather can burn a lawn, miss the pest or break the law. The label decides, every time, and some days the right call is to hold.

  1. 01Does the tank mix fit?
  2. 02Mix by the label

01 · Does the tank mix fit?

Match what you found to what's in the truck

1 · What did you find?

Insects, disease, weeds, nutrition or a water problem. Where, and how much.

2 · Is it in this service?

Check the order and the customer's program (LAWN-02). Not covered: note it and route it.

3 · Does the tank mix cover it?

Labeled for this site, this turf species and this pest, in today's conditions? Then apply.

4 · If not, spot-mix

A backpack or small tank, mixed from the current label and the approved mix sheet, for the problem area only.

Treat the property, not just the work order. Weeds, fungus or thin turf on an insect visit? When it's in their program and you have a labeled product: treat it, flag it, communicate it, document it.
Never improvise a rate. No "a little extra." Over-application is off-label, burns turf and drives resistance. If the label and mix sheet disagree, use the label and tell your supervisor.

Mixing safely

  • Label PPE on before you open a concentrate. Gloves at minimum; eye protection when the label says.
  • Fill and mix away from storm drains, water and the customer's hard surfaces.
  • Keep the fill hose above the water line, and never walk away from a filling tank.
  • Spill? The 3 Cs: Control, Contain, Clean up, and tell your supervisor (FC-05).

01 · The turf pattern

Border first, then even passes with a slight overlap

  1. 1Post the notice as you start, so it's up while product goes down.
  2. 2Border pass: spray a strip around the edges, directed in toward the lawn and away from pavement, beds and water.
  3. 3Fill in with straight passes, each slightly overlapping the last. Steady speed and a steady fan; no gaps, no double strips.
  4. 4Shut off on turns and at the end of each pass. Stopping with the nozzle open leaves a burned spot.
  5. 5Work toward your exit so you never walk back through wet product. Then spot-treat and wind the hose, gloves on.
Boom: lower booms before moving, steady speed, slight overlap between passes.
Hose: keep the nozzle moving at an even height and walking pace.

01 · The lawn notice and the registry · checked October 2026

Every lawn application gets a posted notice

The law · 482.2265(2), 5E-14.147

  • Posted in a conspicuous location at the time of application
  • At least 4 x 5 inches, rigid, durable, weatherproof
  • Contrasting background and lettering, wording and symbol per the FDACS example
  • Our business name clearly shown

The Clements standard

  • Notice of Application sign fully completed with the correct product information
  • Placed where it's easy to see from the street, at the driveway
  • Area of Concern flags where you spot-treated or found a problem, with a short note
  • Left in place when you go

The registry · 482.2267

  • FDACS lists people whose physician certifies they need prior notice
  • Lawn, bed or foliage work near a registrant (adjacent by default, up to half a mile): notice at least 24 hours ahead, with location and pesticide type
  • FDACS sends the list quarterly. The office tracks it and flags the order
Order flagged for registry notice and you can't confirm it was given? Hold the broadcast and call the office. The statute drops only the pesticide-type detail for a small amount on an infestation or disease found on site; it does not clearly waive the 24-hour notice. No confirmed notice, no application.

04 · Watering and mowing advice · UF/IFAS guidance

Most lawn problems start with water or the mower

Watering

  • Water when about a third of the lawn shows need: blades folding, footprints that stay, a blue-gray cast
  • About ½ to ¾ inch per watering, early in the morning
  • Only on the days the local watering rules allow. Turn it off when it rains
  • Daily watering keeps turf wet and invites fungus and weak roots
After today's treatment, tell them what the label says: water it in, or keep it dry until it's dried on the leaf. Never guess.
