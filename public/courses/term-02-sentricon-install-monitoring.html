01 · What you're up against

A colony works as one animal, underground

Workers forage

Blind, soft-bodied workers tunnel through soil looking for cellulose. They do the damage, and they're the ones that find a station.

They share food

Workers feed the queen, king, soldiers and young mouth to mouth (trophallaxis). Whatever they eat, the colony eats.

They molt to grow

Like all insects they shed the old exoskeleton and grow a new one built from chitin. That's the weak point Sentricon targets.

They replace losses

Lose the queen and secondary reproductives take over. Killing the foragers you can see doesn't end the colony.

Florida pressure: native subterraneans statewide, plus invasive Formosan in most coastal counties and Asian subterranean termites as far north as Brevard County (UF/IFAS 2026). TERM-01 covers identification.
The idea: a liquid treatment stops termites at a treated zone. Sentricon uses the colony's own foraging and food sharing to eliminate it.
02 · How noviflumuron works

Feed, share, fail to molt, collapse

1 · Feed

Foragers find Recruit HD bait in the station. Corteva's studies show termites prefer it to wood, and it stays palatable in the soil for a long time.

2 · Share

Workers carry it home and pass it through the colony, including termites that never leave the nest.

3 · Molt fails

Noviflumuron is a chitin synthesis inhibitor, a type of insect growth regulator. When a termite tries to molt it can't form a new exoskeleton, and it dies.

4 · Colony collapses

Workers die off, no one feeds the queen and brood, and the colony is eliminated. The stations stay baited for the next one.

Slow on purpose. Termites don't die at the station, so nothing warns the rest of the colony and feeding keeps spreading. That's why elimination takes time, and why you never promise a date.
Label facts: Recruit HD termite bait, 0.5% noviflumuron, EPA Reg. No. 62719-608. For use only with service by a state-licensed pest control professional. The label is the law (FC-02).

03 · Obstacles and special cases

Real yards aren't diagrams

Irrigation

Find the heads, valve boxes and line runs before you auger; shift a station a foot rather than hit a line. Soil by sprinkler heads stays moist, so add stations near them, never on them.

Pavers, patios, drives

Never under pavers or loose stone. Place stations in soil at both edges and map the hardscape. If a run passes 20 ft with no soil, the label allows stations set through cored concrete or asphalt: CSS and branch manager decide.

Moisture sources

A/C condensate lines, downspouts, leaking spigots and low spots draw termites. Put a station beside each, in soil, not on the pad or splash block.

Mulch and beds

Set the station in mineral soil, not in a mulch pile. Pull mulch back so the cap is visible and flush. Note mulch, fill or landscaping covering stations at every visit.

Utilities and septic

Florida's 811 law exempts pest control digging 18 in or less without mechanized equipment (s. 556.108). A powered auger loses that exemption: follow branch practice and call Sunshine 811 first. Keep off septic lids and drain fields.

Attached and nearby

Slab additions, attached garages and porches get stations too. A fence on the line or a neighbor's yard can block access: install what the label and property allow and map the limitation.

06 · Map, number, photograph

If it isn't on the map, it isn't protected

  1. 1Number every station from a fixed start point, in the same direction. Record any station barcode or ID with its number.
  2. 2Draw the map: outline, hardscape, irrigation, utilities, activity, added and above-ground stations, inaccessible areas.
  3. 3Record the count and perimeter footage. They drive the agreement, the renewal and the reports.
  4. 4Photograph each station flush with grade, and the perimeter as installed. Photos are the foundation of bond defense.
  5. 5Upload it to PestPac. The map is the reference for every future visit and any warranty or bond claim.

01 · The service cadence

Quarterly checks, an annual inspection, more when it's active

The label's floor: inspect and service at least once a year on an assigned date, no later than 30 days past it, and never more than 15 months between inspections. Clements' standard (quarterly plus annual, CW-05) is well above that floor.
02 · Run the visit

Every station, every time

  1. 1Start the order on arrival and open the station map and last visit's notes.
  2. 2Walk the map in order. Locate every station; a station you can't find is a gap.
  3. 3Open each one with the station tool and look: termites, feeding, mud, other insects, water, damage.
  4. 4Act and record each station's condition, bait status and anything you replaced.
  1. 5Fix obstructions: pull back mulch, reset raised stations flush, replace damaged or missing ones.
  2. 6Annual visit: also inspect the structure for subterranean evidence, as at install.
  3. 7Photograph activity, replacements and obstructions.
  4. 8Report to the customer: termites present or not, whether bait was replaced, product name. Required after every contract inspection (482.226(3)).
Two audiences. PestPac Mobile notes print for the customer: plain and factual. A station you had to move, a customer obstruction to follow up, or anything else the office must act on goes to technotes@clementspestcontrol.com.

04 · When you find termites

Activity is the system working. Handle it carefully.

  1. 1Disturb as little as possible. Confirm they're termites, not ants, and close what you don't need open.
  2. 2Replace bait more than one-third consumed. Per the label, gently move live termites from the old bait into the station with the new.
  3. 3Record and photograph the station number, activity and bait status.
  4. 4Shorten the cycle: return every 30–60 days until two consecutive visits show no activity, then back to the standard cadence.
  5. 5Tell the customer what you found, what you did and when you'll be back.
Activity in the structure (tubes on a wall, swarmers inside): photograph it, leave active tubes intact, tell your branch manager the same day. Where the label allows, a CSS places above-ground stations over active tubes or feeding sites.
Extra in-ground stations near the activity are supported by the label. Add them to the map and note them in PestPac.
Never spray into a station, pull bait that's being fed on, or tell the customer when the colony will be gone.

01 · Explaining Sentricon

Plain words, no promises

Instead of"It's totally safe for your kids and dog."
Say"The bait is sealed inside the station underground. I'm happy to give you the product name and label information."
Instead of"The termites will be gone in a month."
Say"It works through the whole colony, so it takes time. We'll check every 30 to 60 days until the activity stops."
Instead of"You're covered for anything, forever."
Say"Corteva's damage bond covers new subterranean termite damage as long as your annual monitoring is current. Your agreement has the details."
The customer's part: keep mulch, pavers, fill and planters off the caps; don't move or mow over stations; call before landscaping, additions or a pool; call if they see swarmers or tubes.
On request give the product name, active ingredient and label safety information (482.2265). Prices, extra stations and service changes go to the branch manager.
02 · The bond and who handles what

Know the coverage, and hand it to the right person

Corteva's $1,000,000 damage bond

The $1,000,000 damage bond is Corteva's, not a Clements warranty. It covers new subterranean damage after installation, only while annual monitoring is current (a lapse past the 30-day grace period voids it), never existing damage. It depends on Clements staying a Certified Sentricon Specialist in good standing.

Clements retreatment warranty

Separate from the bond: Clements retreats new subterranean activity at no charge for the term of the agreement while monitoring is current. TERM-07 covers renewals, lapses and transfers.
