01 · The non-negotiables

Six rules every Rodent Shield seal follows

Cement first

Every entry point 1/2 inch and larger is finished with cement, color-matched to the home. Rats chew foam. They don't chew cement.

The 1/2-inch threshold

A rat needs about 1/2 inch. Openings that size and up are documented and sealed as entry points. Smaller gaps are noted for the record.

Backing, then finish

Fill or backing goes in first (mesh, hardware cloth or metal), mechanically fastened. Cement seals every edge. Both layers, every time.

Vents breathe, water drains

Never seal dryer, exhaust or appliance vents, plumbing vent tops or weep holes. Attic vents get screened, never closed.

Photo before and after

Same angle, every entry point, uploaded to PestPac with the entry point PDF. The photos are the warranty record.

You can stop the job

Rot, damaged roof decking, unsafe access or contractor-level work: you stop, document and escalate. No seal is worth a fall.

02 · Roofline openings · roof rat territory

Where Florida rats get in most

Roof returns

The open corner where the roof and fascia return at the eave. Cut hardware cloth to the shape with at least 1 inch of overlap, screw it to solid framing, then seal every edge with cement. Back a large return with sheet metal.

Soffit and fascia gaps

Open seams, separated fascia, unfinished corners. Size it, then use the matching material from the size chart. Active gnawing on wood gets sheet metal. Soft or rotted wood is not a seal surface: stop and refer.

Gable vents

Torn screens, loose louvers, gaps at the corners. Cover with 1/4-inch galvanized hardware cloth, fastened and edge-sealed. The vent must still pass air: screen it, never fill it.

Roof stacks and cable entries: seal the gap around a plumbing or HVAC stack where it passes through. Never cap the open top of a plumbing vent; the drains need it. A cracked roof boot is a roofer's job.
Find the active one: dark grease rub marks, fresh gnawing, droppings below, no cobwebs. Seal every documented opening, but photograph the active ones closely.
03 · Ground-level openings · Norway rats and mice

Penetrations, chases, doors and weeps

Plumbing and conduit

Irregular gaps around pipes are the job Xcluder and copper mesh were made for. Pack tight with no soft spots, then cement over flush.

A/C line-set chase

Where refrigerant lines enter the wall. Pack mesh around the lines, finish with cement. Don't crush or nick the lines or their insulation. Included in Plus; a standalone add-on otherwise (RX-05).

Garage doors

Check the bottom seal and both corners. Gnawed or gapped seals get a sweep or brush seal that meets the slab fully with no corner gaps. Aluminum-reinforced preferred.

Weep holes

They let water out of the wall. Never fill them with cement, foam or mesh packing. Use a weep hole cover that keeps rodents out and lets water drain.

Every method follows the same order: measure, clean and prep the surface, install the fill or backing and fasten it, seal every edge with color-matched cement, tool it smooth, photograph.
04 · Never block these

Some openings exist on purpose. Leave them working.

  • Dryer vents. Lint builds up behind a screen or seal and can catch fire. Building code bans screens on dryer exhaust terminations.
  • Bath, kitchen and HVAC exhausts. Blocking them pushes moisture, mold and combustion gases back inside.
  • Plumbing vent tops. Seal around the stack, never the opening.
  • Weep holes and condensate lines. Water has to get out.
  • Attic ventilation. Soffit, gable and ridge vents are screened, never sealed solid.
No material, screen or cover on a dryer or exhaust vent, even if the homeowner asks. Note it, photograph it and tell them why. It's in the Rodent Shield terms.

01 · What finished looks like

If you wouldn't want it on your own house, it isn't done

Solid

Backing fastened with screws, flush heads, no soft spots you can push through.

Sealed

Cement on every edge. No gap at the overlap, no mesh corners poking out.

Weatherproof

Tooled to shed water, not hold it. Sun and rain won't crack or lift it.

Color-matched

Blends with the stucco or trim. Texture matched where you can.

Clean

No smears, drips or debris left on walls, roof, plants or driveway.

Florida weather: in summer, do exterior and roofline work in the morning. Don't apply cement in rain or on a soaked surface, and give it time to set before anyone touches it.
It gets checked again. Every follow-up and warranty visit includes a look at your seals. Cracked, lifted or gnawed work is redone and noted.

02 · Roofline and ladder safety

Most exclusion work is overhead. FC-06 rules apply on every job.

  • Extension ladder at 4 to 1, on firm footing, secured, 3 feet above the roof edge
  • Three points of contact. Materials go up in a pouch or by line, not in your hand
  • Never above the 4th rung from the top of an extension ladder or the top two steps of a stepladder
  • Roof access cleared with your supervisor. No wet, dewy, tile or metal roofs without approval and fall protection
  • OSHA requires fall protection near an unprotected edge 4 feet or more above a lower level
  • 10 feet from overhead power lines, ladder included

Heat

Roofline and attic work in the morning in summer. Water about every 15 to 20 minutes, shade breaks, and never work a hot attic alone without a check-in (RX-08).

Droppings at the opening

Wet them with disinfectant first. Never sweep or vacuum dry. Gloves and respirator per FC-03.

Hurt or close call?

Tell your supervisor immediately and file the written report the same day (CW-09).
