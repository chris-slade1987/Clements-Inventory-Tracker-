Why this course matters

The right product on the wrong grass kills the lawn.

Every herbicide label lists the turf it may be used on. A product that cleans up zoysia can burn St. Augustine to the ground. Misidentify the grass and you've broken the label, damaged the property and handed a competitor the account.

Applying a product to a site the label doesn't list is a violation of federal and Florida law. The turf species is part of the site. FC-02 covers the rules.

Some products injure some grasses

UF/IFAS rates quinclorac damaging to St. Augustine, centipede and bahia, and warns atrazine and metsulfuron damage bahia. Your ID decides which jug comes off the truck.

Customers chose their grass

A zoysia or CitraBlue customer paid for premium sod and notices every off-color patch. Running one playbook on every yard loses those accounts first.

Naming the grass builds trust

"Your Floratam needs a taller cut in this shade" tells a customer you know their lawn. "Your grass" tells them nothing.

02 · Four clues that do most of the work

Tip, fold, runner, seedhead

Tip. Blunt and rounded: St. Augustine, centipede. Pointed: everything else.
Fold. Roll the newest leaf. Folded (V): St. Augustine, centipede, bermuda, paspalum. Rolled: zoysia, bahia.
Runner. Stolons only: St. Augustine, centipede. Woody rhizomes: bahia. Both: zoysia, bermuda, paspalum.
Seedhead. Two-branched V: bahia. Two-branched fork: paspalum. 3–5 fingers: bermuda.
03 · The field ID routine

Standing, kneeling, in your hand

Run the same routine on every new account and any time the lawn doesn't match the account. It takes a minute and it's the minute that protects the lawn.

Can't name it? Don't spray a selective herbicide. Photograph the blade, tip and runner and confirm with your supervisor first. Guessing is how lawns get burned.
  1. 1Standing. Color, texture, density. Seedheads? Sun or shade? Ocean or freshwater nearby?
  2. 2Kneeling. Look at a single blade: width, tip shape, hairs. Run your hand across it: soft, coarse or stiff and prickly.
  3. 3In your hand. Pull a runner. On top of the soil or under it? Thick or wiry? Even or uneven gaps between nodes?
  4. 4Confirm. Check the account, ask the customer or install records for the cultivar, and look for a second grass moving in.
  5. 5Record it. Grass and cultivar in your notes, every time. If the account lists the wrong grass, get it corrected.

03 · Zoysia: a different playbook

Zoysia is a category, not one grass

Confirm the cultivar on every zoysia account. Coarse Z. japonica types and fine Z. matrella types are cut and managed differently, and the customer who chose zoysia is grading us against the lawn they paid for.

Don't run a St. Augustine program on zoysia. Extra nitrogen and frequent watering build thatch and feed large patch. If zoysia looks off, check water, then mowing, before more fertilizer. LAWN-05 covers the fertilizer program.
Coarse to medium · Z. japonica

Empire · JaMur · El Toro · Palisades

Empire is the most common zoysia in Florida. Mow 1.75–2.5 in with a rotary mower. All four are susceptible to large patch. Palisades has excellent drought tolerance.

Fine · Z. matrella

Zeon · Zorro · Cashmere · Diamond · Trinity

Fine, dense and very shade tolerant. Cut lower, often with a reel mower; below 1 in needs frequent mowing. UF notes Zeon is less well adapted to Florida.

Hybrids

Emerald · CitraZoy · Icon

Emerald: very fine, prone to thatch and dollar spot. CitraZoy (UF, 2019): no large patch observed, susceptible to leaf spot. Icon: very high salt tolerance.

01 · Mowing height and the one-third rule

Cut too low and every other problem gets worse

Inches, UF/IFAS ENH04 and species guides. Light band: the full range. Dark band: St. Augustine dwarf (2–2.5) and standard cultivars (3.5–4); Empire zoysia with a rotary mower (1.75–2.5).

Never remove more than one-third of the blade in one mowing, on any grass.
St. Augustine varies by cultivar. Standard types 3.5–4 in; Palmetto 3–4; CitraBlue 2–3; dwarf Seville 2–2.5.
Shade or drought stress? Mow at the top of the range. Scalping thins turf and lets weeds, insects and disease in.
We advise, the customer mows. If you see scalping or dull-blade tearing, note it and tell the customer what height their grass needs.

02 · Reading the turf section of a label

Four things to find before you mix

  1. 1Tolerant turf. Is this grass listed? Not listed means not allowed.
  2. 2Do-not-use list. Grasses, and sometimes cultivars, the product injures.
  3. 3Restrictions. Separate rates, temperature limits, new sod, stressed turf.
  4. 4Everything else. Rate, REI, rainfast and mowing intervals. Mix from the current label and the Clements approved mix rate sheet.
Example label · written for training, not a real product

BROADLEAF HERBICIDE · Directions for use on turfgrass

Tolerant turfgrasses: bahiagrass, bermudagrass, centipedegrass, zoysiagrass.

St. Augustinegrass: use only the St. Augustinegrass rate shown in the rate table. Do not apply when air temperatures exceed 90°F.

Do not apply to seashore paspalum, carpetgrass, dichondra, or newly sodded or sprigged turf until well established.

Do not apply to turf under heat or drought stress. Do not mow 24 hours before or after application. Rainfast in 6 hours.

04 · Mixed lawns and uncertain IDs

Treat to the most sensitive grass present

Many lawns are not one grass. Bermuda creeps into St. Augustine, bahia invades from the road edge, a back yard was resodded with something different. A broadcast product has to be labeled for every grass it lands on.

Account lists the wrong grass? Your PestPac Mobile notes print on the customer's report, so keep them about the visit. Send the correction to technotes@clementspestcontrol.com so the office can fix the account.
  1. 1Map it. Walk the yard and note where each grass is. Front and back can differ.
  2. 2Find the most sensitive grass in the area you'll broadcast. That grass sets the product.
  3. 3Spot-treat or split the yard when a product is only labeled for part of it, and keep it off the rest.
  4. 4Still unsure? Skip the selective herbicide, photograph it, and confirm with your supervisor before the next visit.
  5. 5Document the grasses, what you applied where, and anything you held back.
05 · Naming the grass for the customer

Plain answers, real diagnoses, no guessing

Instead of"Your grass is having a tough time."
Say"This is Floratam. It needs about six hours of sun, and under this oak it gets less. That's why it's thinning here."
Instead of"I'll spray the weeds with what I've got."
Say"That product isn't labeled for St. Augustine, so I'm not using it on your lawn. I'll treat it with one that is."
Instead of"Sure, we'll give it more fertilizer."
Say"Your Empire zoysia does better on less. The blue-gray color and footprints mean it wants a deep watering, not more feed."
Instead of"It's safe, don't worry about it."
Say"Here's the product name and active ingredient, and the label's re-entry directions. I'll leave the sign at the edge of the lawn."

Never call a pesticide safe or harmless. Questions about pricing or extra services go to the branch manager.
