Why this course matters

Fertilizer is the one product we apply that feeds the water too.

The right nitrogen at the right time gives a thick, green lawn that resists weeds and pests. The wrong amount, the wrong week or the wrong spot feeds algae instead.

Nitrogen and phosphorus fuel algae

Runoff and leaching carry them into the lagoon, rivers and lakes our branches sit beside. Blooms block sunlight, kill seagrass and use up the oxygen fish need.

Every county writes its own rules

Summer bans, slow-release minimums, setbacks and weather limits differ by county and city. Ordinance violations carry fines, and local governments enforce them.

The state licenses the applicator

Since 2014, every person who applies fertilizer for hire in Florida needs their own state certification (s. 482.1562). Customers and code officers can ask to see it.

01 · The three numbers

16-4-8 means 16% N, 4% phosphate, 8% potash. Select each one.

Always in that order: N-P-K. A 50 lb bag of 16-4-8 holds 8 lb of nitrogen, 2 lb of phosphate and 4 lb of potash. The rest is carrier and other nutrients.
The middle number should be zero on almost every lawn we feed. The ordinances on our routes ban phosphorus unless a soil test shows a deficiency.
02 · Secondary nutrients, iron and micronutrients

Small amounts, big color: what else is on the tag

Secondary nutrients

Calcium, magnesium, sulfur. Needed in moderate amounts for cell walls, chlorophyll and protein. Magnesium matters most for palms.

Micronutrients

Iron, manganese, zinc, copper, boron, molybdenum. Needed in tiny amounts. Manganese is a micronutrient, not one of the big three.

Iron

Yellowing between the veins of new growth is often iron chlorosis, common in centipedegrass and St. Augustine on alkaline soil. Iron greens up the leaf without a growth surge.

Iron is temporary. UF/IFAS puts the green-up at roughly 2 to 4 weeks.
Iron is not a substitute for nitrogen. Diagnose first: pale lawns can be pests, disease, water or pH (LAWN-08).
Iron stains concrete orange-brown. One more reason to keep every granule off driveways, pool decks and pavers.
03 · Quick release vs slow release

Same nitrogen, very different ride

Quick release (water soluble): ammonium sulfate, urea, ammonium nitrate, potassium nitrate. Fast green-up, but the excess the grass can't take in washes through sandy soil.
Slow release: polymer- or sulfur-coated urea, methylene urea, IBDU and natural organics. Nitrogen comes out over weeks, so less is lost and the lawn grows evenly.
The law cares which kind. Every county we serve sets a minimum share of slow-release nitrogen: 50% in most, 65% in Orange County.

01 · Pounds of nitrogen per 1,000 sq ft

Everyone in turf talks in lb N per 1,000 sq ft, not bags

lb of product per 1,000 sq ft=lb N wanted ÷ (% N ÷ 100)
Example

0.5 lb N from a 16-0-8: 0.5 ÷ 0.16 = 3.1 lb of product per 1,000 sq ft. A 6,000 sq ft lawn takes 6 × 3.1 = about 19 lb.

This is the concept. The product label sets the rate, and the most restrictive of label, state guidance and county ordinance always wins. Calibration is LAWN-09.
Per-application limits · UF/IFAS SL21 and local ordinances
No more than 0.7 lb of quick-release N per 1,000 sq ft in any one application.
Up to 2 lb N per application in spring, or summer where feeding is allowed, and only with at least 65% slow-release N.
No more than 1 lb N per application in the fall.
Orange County and the City of Naples cap every application at 1 lb N per 1,000 sq ft, any season.

04 · Shrubs and palms

Feed the root zone, not the stems

Spread evenly over the whole bed and the root zone, which reaches past the drip line. Label rate, by bed area.
Keep granules off leaves, crowns and stems. Brush off foliage; it burns.
Palms are different. UF/IFAS recommends a palm fertilizer such as 8-2-12-4Mg with micronutrients and slow-release N, K and Mg. High-N turf fertilizer can worsen palm potassium deficiency.
The ordinances cover beds too. Bans, setbacks and slow-release rules apply to landscape plants too.
05 · Spreading it right

The spreader decides where every granule lands

  1. 1Fill over turf, never on pavement. A spill on the driveway is a spill in the storm drain.
  2. 2Deflector shield down along driveways, sidewalks, streets, beds and water. Most of our counties require it on rotary and broadcast spreaders.
  3. 3Steady pace, slight overlap per the spread pattern. Shut the gate on sharp turns and when you stop.
  4. 4No spreading in wind that carries granules off target.
  5. 5Sweep or blow every stray granule back onto the turf before you leave (FC-04).

03 · Weather and soil

Check the forecast and the ground first

Heavy rain coming

Indian River, Port St. Lucie: 2 in. or more in 24 hours likely. Naples: 60% chance of 2 in.

Watch or warning

Flood, tropical storm or hurricane. Orange County adds severe thunderstorm advisories.

Saturated soil

Puddles or squishy turf: the soil can't hold more, so nutrients leach or run off.

Wind

Granules blowing off target means stop. Same weather sense as FC-04.

Can't fertilize today? Do the rest of the visit, note why in the office notes (technotes@clementspestcontrol.com) so the stop gets rescheduled, and tell the customer plainly. Never "make it up" with a heavier application next time.

01 · Limited Urban Commercial Fertilizer Applicator · s. 482.1562, administered by FDACS

One certificate per person. Every person who spreads it.

GI-BMP training

UF/IFAS Green Industries Best Management Practices course. Pass the test (75% or better) for a training certificate.

Apply to FDACS

Online, choosing the Limited Urban Commercial Fertilizer Applicator (LF) license type, with the GI-BMP certificate number.

Carry it

On you whenever you apply fertilizer. Ordinances in our area require it, and customers may ask.

Renew every 4 years

Four CEUs first: 2 in 482 Core, 2 in the LF category. Grace period is 30 days; after that you start over.

Who needs it: anyone applying fertilizer for hire to lawns or landscapes. The statute has no exemption for pest control licenses or ID cards. The only exemption is a yard worker using the homeowner's own fertilizer and equipment.
Supervision does not count. A certified person may not have uncertified staff apply fertilizer under their supervision. No certificate in your pocket, no spreader in your hands.
02 · What the fertilizer certificate does and doesn't cover

Fertilizer only. Anything with a pesticide in it is a pest control job.

Covered by the LF certificate

  • Fertilizer to turf and landscape plants for hire
  • Nutrient-only products: N-P-K, iron, micronutrient blends
  • Following every county rule while you do it

Not covered

  • Any pesticide on turf or ornamentals, including weed-and-feed and insecticide-fertilizer granules
  • Operating a pest control business
  • Letting anyone uncertified apply under you
Combination products need both: the fertilizer certificate for the nutrients, and pest control credentials under Chapter 482 for the pesticide (your FDACS ID card under the company's lawn and ornamental license). Your supervisor confirms you are cleared for each product before you use it.
