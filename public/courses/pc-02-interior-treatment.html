Interior Treatment

Inside the home is where families eat, sleep and play. This course is how we treat there: precisely, by the label, and in a way the customer can see is careful.

40–55 min5 checkpoints20-question check · 80% to passGHP technicians
Course objectives

By the end of this course you will be able to:

Know when an interior treatment is due: every initial service, and follow-ups on request or when pests are active inside
Run a two-to-three-minute interior inspection and let the evidence decide where you treat
Apply crack-and-crevice and spot treatments with an aerosol and a B&G, with no broadcast and no residue
Place gel bait where pests feed and keep it clean of sprays, flushing agents and repellents; use dusts and monitors correctly
Protect kitchens, nurseries, pets, aquariums and the people at home, following every label direction
Prepare the customer, explain re-entry and aftercare honestly, and document it all in PestPac Mobile
Why this course matters

Inside, every drop is personal.

Outside, a mistake lands in a flower bed. Inside, it lands on a kitchen counter, a toy or a pet bowl. The same product can be correct along a baseboard crack and illegal on the open floor a few inches away.

The label is federal law

Using a product in a way its label doesn't allow is a violation of FIFRA, the federal pesticide law, and Florida's Chapter 482 (FC-02). Most indoor labels allow only crack-and-crevice and spot treatment in homes.

Wrong placement means failure

A spray over gel bait ruins it. A broadcast floor spray doesn't reach the roaches living in the hinge. Precise work is what actually controls pests.

It's where trust is won

Many companies skip the interior. Shoe covers, a careful inspection and a clear explanation are what customers remember, and why they renew.

01 · Crack-and-crevice technique

Put the tip in the crack, not the product on the surface

  1. 1Fit the extension tube that comes with the aerosol.
  2. 2Insert the tip into the crack, gap or void. Don't spray from a distance.
  3. 3Short bursts, about one second, moving the tip along the crack.
  4. 4Nothing visible on the surface when you're done. Wipe any drip.

Targets: under sinks and around plumbing, behind the refrigerator and oven, vanity bases, door frames, baseboard gaps and utility penetrations. Skip any crack you plan to bait.

Correction to older Clements guides: they said to hold the can 12 inches away and also insert the tube. You can't do both. The tip goes into the crack.
02 · The B&G sprayer

Pin stream into cracks. Small spots only where the label allows.

  • Labeled rate, mixed at the truck. More product is never better, and it's off-label (PC-04).
  • Low pressure and a controlled trigger. No runoff under cabinets or onto floors.
  • Pin stream into the joint where the baseboard meets the floor, door frames, window sills, gaps under appliances.
  • Coarse fan, spot only: a small, localized area where pests rest, if the label lists spot treatment.
  • No broadcast across floors or carpets, and no visible residue on baseboards.
  • Interior liquid is not a barrier. It targets harborage and travel routes. The barrier is outside (PC-03).
03 · Where yes, where never

If people, food, children or pets touch it, it isn't a treatment site

Treat, as the label allows

  • Cracks and voids under sinks and around pipes
  • Behind and under the refrigerator, oven and dishwasher
  • Baseboard–floor joints and corners, as crack and crevice
  • Door frames, window sills, thresholds
  • Cabinet hinges and the gap behind the kick plate
  • Utility penetrations, water heater and laundry areas

Never

  • Broadcast across floors, carpets or rugs
  • Countertops, tables, dishes, utensils or any food surface
  • Cribs, bedding, toys or where infants crawl
  • Pet bowls, beds, litter areas; never on pets
  • Into or near aquariums and terrariums
  • Over gel bait, or anywhere the label doesn't list
Products differ. One label may allow spot treatment on floors along baseboards; another may allow crack and crevice only. Read the label of the product in your B&G, not the one you used last year.

Example label · Indoor directions

Indoor use. Spray directly into cracks and crevices or voids where pests are present. Do not apply broadcast treatments to floors or floor coverings. Spot applications may be made to floor surfaces along baseboards.

In the home, all food processing surfaces and utensils should be covered before treatment. Do not allow spray to contact food or feed surfaces.

Keep people and pets away from sites of application until the treated area has dried. Remove or tightly cover fish tanks and disconnect aerators during application.

Adapted from the current Suspend SC label (deltamethrin), a product Clements uses indoors. Your product's wording may differ. Read it.

05 · Reading an indoor label

Five lines to find before you spray inside

  1. 1Sites: is "residential interior" listed, and which methods: crack and crevice, spot, void?
  2. 2Rate for indoor use, and the mix you put in the B&G.
  3. 3Food areas: what must be covered or removed first.
  4. 4People, pets, aquariums: who leaves, what gets covered, re-entry.
  5. 5PPE for the applicator (FC-03).
Customer asks? Give the product name, active ingredient and label safety information. Florida law requires it (s. 482.2265).

01 · Gel bait rules

Small, hidden, clean and close to where they live

Size and count

  • Pea-size or smaller. The Advion cockroach label sets a spot at about 1/4 inch (0.5 g).
  • Spots per 10 feet by activity, from the label: Advion is 1–3 for light, 3–5 for heavy.
  • "Don't use bait like caulk" (UF/IFAS).

Where

  • Where the inspection found activity, near harborage
  • Cabinet hinges, behind the refrigerator and dishwasher, under sinks and vanities, plumbing gaps
  • Out of reach of children and pets; never on food surfaces
  • Not where it's routinely washed, or on surfaces above 120–130 °F

Keep it clean

  • Never spray a residual over bait, and never bait a surface you just sprayed (Advion label)
  • No flushing aerosols or repellents in baited cracks (UF/IFAS)
  • Right bait for the pest: cockroach gel for roaches, ant bait for ants
  • Store bait away from other products and out of a hot cab
Flushing aerosols and bait don't mix. A pyrethrin flushing aerosol like CB-80 drives roaches out and contaminates nearby bait. Where you bait for roaches, don't use it in the same cracks. UF/IFAS (ENY989, 2026) advises avoiding insecticide flushing agents with bait; canned compressed air flushes without contaminating.

04 · Dusts

Dust goes in enclosed voids, never where anyone can touch it

  • Only sites the label lists, and only with a product your supervisor has issued.
  • Enclosed voids: behind plumbing escutcheons, cabinet kick-plate voids, hollow wall spaces where pests travel.
  • A light puff from a bulb duster. Visible dust on any surface means too much: clean it up.
  • Never in open living areas, on floors, near food or air vents, or near bait placements.
  • UF/IFAS (2026) notes dusts are rarely needed with good baiting and sanitation, and drift is a real risk.
Monitors · read the sticky trap

Place along walls and corners near harborage, in dry spots out of reach of children and pets. Date and number each one, note its location in PestPac, and replace it when it's full, wet or dusty. Select a trap.

03 · Aftercare and the knock back

Tell them what you did, and what to do now

"I treated the cracks under the sink and behind the fridge and stove, and put small bait placements in the cabinet hinges. Please keep the kids and the dog off the kitchen baseboards until they're completely dry. Don't wipe away the bait or use store-bought sprays near it; that keeps them feeding. You may see a few more roaches over the next several days as they find it. If you're still seeing them after that, call us and we'll come back."A KNOCK BACK THAT COVERS IT

Aftercare to cover
  • Re-entry: people and pets off treated areas until dry. The label sets it; never guess a number of hours you can't back up.
  • Leave bait alone: no wiping, no household sprays or cleaners on it.
  • Sanitation: seal food, clean grease, fix the leak, remove cardboard.
  • Honest timing and when to call. No promises.
Never call a product "safe," "harmless" or "non-toxic."

04 · Document the interior

Write it so the customer and the next tech can use it

  • Chemical record: each product, amount, target pest and areas treated (FC-10).
  • Customer notes (on the inspection report): what you treated, what you found, what they can do.
  • Monitors and bait: where they are, and what they showed.
  • Interior declined: note it, so nobody argues about it later.
  • First interior visit: fill out the Clements service sticker and place it at the electrical panel, or another inconspicuous spot.
  • Office items (a lead, a leak the landlord must fix) go to technotes@clementspestcontrol.com.
