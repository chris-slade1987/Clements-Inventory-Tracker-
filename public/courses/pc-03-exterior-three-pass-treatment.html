Why this course matters

The outside is most of what we do.

After the initial service, most recurring GHP visits are exterior. Many customers are exterior-only. For them, the outside of the house is the entire Clements experience, every other month.

Customers judge what they can see

Webs on the eaves, granules on the driveway, a dirty bait station: these are what customers notice, and why they cancel. "Customers judge us on consistency, professionalism and thoroughness, not just product."

The label is federal law

Under FIFRA and Florida law (s. 487.031), applying against the label is a violation. Outdoor pyrethroid labels limit where the spray can go. An FDACS investigation starts with one complaint.

Runoff starts at the foundation

Spray on a driveway or granules on a walk wash to the storm drain and the lagoon with the next rain. A tight band protects the house and the water.

03 · Before the first pass

Two minutes that decide the whole stop

  1. 1Read the account. Start the order on arrival and check PestPac notes: no-spray areas, pets, gate codes, past issues.
  2. 2Check the sky. No spray in rain. Wind, inversions, storms on radar: FC-04 rules apply.
  3. 3Knock and greet. "Are you seeing any activity I should know about? Anything inside you'd like me to treat?"
  4. 4Clear the area. Pets and kids inside, bowls and toys moved, windows closed on the side you're treating.

Look the part

Shirt tucked, clean uniform, hat straight. The customer meets you before they see any work.

PPE the label lists

Read the label for each product on today's stop. Gloves and eye protection when mixing; FC-03 covers the rest.

Park and mix right

Away from storm drains and slopes to the curb. Mix over turf or soil with an air gap when you fill.

01 · The band

Up to 3 ft up, 3 ft out on soil, turf and mulch, if the label allows

What the label allows

EPA's outdoor residential pyrethroid language permits: building foundations up to 3 ft above grade, and soil, turf and vegetation around the structure. Everything else outside, including patios, driveways, walks, porches, windows, doors and eaves, is spot and crack-and-crevice only. A product label can be narrower.

What Clements does

Up to 3 ft up and 3 ft out on soil, turf and mulch, where the product label allows. The label band governs: Taurus SC allows only 1 ft up and 1 ft out (PC-04). Coarse fan, low pressure; pin stream for cracks.

Corrected: older guides said "6–10 ft out." That band runs onto pavement at most homes. The standard is now up to 3 ft out where the label allows, never across a hard surface.

03 · Rain, drains and runoff

The four label rules that stop the spray

Not during rain

"Do not make applications during rain." Not sure? The paper test from FC-04. Hold off when a downpour is due before the spray dries.

Not into drains

Never let product enter or run off into storm drains, ditches, gutters or water. Ants nesting at a drain get a bait, not a spray.

No runoff

Coarse, low-pressure spray. Wet the target, don't flood it. Don't water the area to the point of runoff.

Not on blooms

Keep spray and drift off flowering plants with bees on them. Never spray toward ponds or fish.

Corrected: an older guide said only to "adjust timing" if rain is expected in a few hours. The label is stricter: no applications during rain. Do the inspection, webs and any interior, then reschedule the outside. FC-04 covers wind, drift and water in full.

Pass 2 · Webs, nests and a second look

Why it matters: spider webs are the most visible sign of pests a customer can see. Leave them and the customer assumes nothing was done, even if everything else was perfect.

  1. 01The wipe-down standard
  2. 02Stinging insects and active spiders
01 · The wipe-down standard

Every eave, every arch, every reachable corner

Pass 2 is part of standard service on every GHP account, paid add-on or not. Walk the whole house again with the extendable spider brush once the band is down.

  • Eaves, soffits and corners, all the way around
  • Archways, entryways and reachable areas of enclosures
  • Light fixtures, window frames, garage corners
  • Inactive mud dauber and wasp nests within reach
You're inspecting as you go. Eyes on the roofline: gaps at soffits, rub marks, roof rat entry, wood damage. Note what you find for the customer.

Standard: a technician 5'5" to 6'0" should reach about 17 ft with the brush. Not sure an area is in scope? Ask your supervisor or the salesperson on the account.

02 · Stinging insects and active spiders

Look before you reach. Treat only what the label allows.

Check every high spot first

Before you push the brush into a corner, soffit or light fixture, look for wasp and hornet traffic. A surprised nest at 15 ft is how techs get hurt.

Active wasp nest?

Treat it with a product labeled for stinging insects, following the label, then knock it down once it's inactive. Honey bees or a swarm: don't treat. Call your supervisor.

Active spiders on the eaves?

Brush them down, then use a pin stream or spot treatment into the cracks and corners where they harbor. Eaves are a structural surface: spot and crack-and-crevice only.

Correction to older Clements material: earlier guides said to "soak down" the eaves with the backpack sprayer. Current pyrethroid labels limit eaves, windows and doors to spot and crack-and-crevice treatment, with a narrow exception on some labels for surfaces permanently protected from rain. Don't broadcast or soak structural surfaces.

01 · Pass 3 · the granular band

A 2–3 ft band on turf, soil and mulch

  1. 1Set the spreader to the label rate for the product and the area. PC-04 covers calibration.
  2. 2Walk a steady pace for even coverage. Focus on mulch beds, shade and moisture-prone zones.
  3. 3Keep it off hard surfaces. Use the deflector along edges. Sweep or blow every stray granule back onto turf.
  4. 4Water in only as the label says, and tell the customer: granules keep working after watering.
Granule or bait? Older guides call the granular "bait." Some perimeter granules are baits, many are contact insecticides. Read the label to know which you're carrying and how it's applied.
A granular that also contains fertilizer is fertilizer under county summer bans (FC-04). Keep granules off flowering plants with bees on them.
02 · Bait stations, if on the account

Open every station, every visit

  1. 1Gloves on, in sight of the customer if they're watching.
  2. 2Unlock and inspect. Gnawing, partial or full consumption, droppings, moisture, dead rodents, other pests.
  3. 3Clean it. Debris and moisture out, drain hole clear, lid wiped, vegetation cut back from the entry holes.
  4. 4Rebait by activity. Fresh, in-date blocks secured on the rod. Rotate stale bait even with no feeding.
  5. 5Relock, anchored, and record activity for that station. Replace any cracked or broken station on the spot.
Seal first, then bait. Activity inside the home means openings. Baiting before they're sealed can mean a rodent eats, gets in and dies in the wall. Rodent Shield (CW-05) handles exclusion.

Customer hears scratching in the attic?

That's an inside problem. Note it for the customer, send it to technotes@clementspestcontrol.com, and the branch manager follows up. Don't quote prices or promise results.

Old bait

Bag removed bait and handle it as the label's Storage and Disposal section directs. Never leave loose blocks on the property. PC-07 covers placement and species in depth.

04 · Wrap-up

The walk-back, then the customer

Customer home?

Knock back and tell them what you did and found: "Removed the webs from the eaves, treated the foundation and entry points, and laid granules in the beds. There's a gap at the A/C line worth sealing."

No one home?

Leave a completed door hanger: your name, date and time, services checked, and a specific note on the back. Skip it only if the customer is out of town or asked for none.

Record it

Customer-facing notes in PestPac Mobile print on the report. Anything the office must act on goes to technotes@. Complete the order before you leave.
