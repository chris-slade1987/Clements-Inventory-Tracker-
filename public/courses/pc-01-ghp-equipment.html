Why this course matters

Most callbacks start with poor application.

The product only works if the equipment puts it exactly where the label says, in the right amount. A leaking valve, a clogged tip or the wrong pressure turns good product into a stain, a drift complaint or a callback.

The label is the law

Federal law (FIFRA) makes any use inconsistent with the label a violation. Spraying the wrong place, at the wrong rate or with drift onto non-target surfaces is misuse, whatever the equipment did.

FDACS checks your equipment

Inspectors look at the truck: concentrates locked, containers identified, and an air gap or anti-siphon device on anything you fill from a hose (Rule 5E-14.106).

Customers notice your tools

A dripping wand on hardwood floors, a cracked bait station by the pool or webs left on the eaves tells the customer exactly how much care we take.

02 · Exterior and interior tools

Outside, the barrier. Inside, the hiding places.

Exterior service

  • Electric backpack sprayer: perimeter liquid band on the foundation and ground, entry points, spot treatments
  • Hand spreader: granular band around the perimeter. Granules release slowly into mulch, soil and turf, extending the barrier
  • Spider brush: webs and nests off eaves and soffits on every visit
  • EVO bait stations and key: rodent stations on the account, opened and serviced every visit

Interior service

  • Shoe covers: on as you step inside, every home, every visit
  • Flashlight: inspection under sinks, behind appliances, attics and voids
  • B&G handheld sprayer: baseboards, door frames, under appliances, spot treatments
  • Aerosol with crack-and-crevice tip, gel bait, duster: the hiding places a spray can't reach
  • Monitors (glue boards): show where activity is and whether the treatment is working

04 · Patterns and tips

Match the pattern to the surface

Pin stream

A narrow stream. Into cracks, along an ant trail into a gap, or a spot treatment into eave corners from the ground with the backpack.

Fan

A flat band. A light fan on baseboards and toe-kicks without runoff; a wider fan for the exterior perimeter band.

Crack-and-crevice tip

A straw or tip that goes into the gap so product lands inside it, where the pests hide, not on the surface around it.

Duster or bait tip

Dust puffed into dry voids; gel placed in small spots. Neither goes where a spray belongs, and spray never goes over bait.

The B&G's adjustable nozzle turns from a broad fan to a pin stream. Many B&Gs also take an interchangeable crack-and-crevice tip. Change the setting before you start a surface, not halfway across it.
Check the label for the application type. Many labels allow only spot or crack-and-crevice treatment indoors, and some don't allow broadcast spraying at all. The tip you pick has to match what the label allows.

05 · The electric backpack sprayer

The perimeter tool. Charged, strained, strapped and steady.

Before the route

Charge and check

Battery charged overnight and connected. Lid seal clean, strainer basket in place, straps and hose undamaged. A quick test spray at the branch into the tank or a labeled site shows the pump, valve and tip all work.

At the stop

Fill and put it on

Fill through the strainer with an air gap. Lid tight. Keep it at the tailgate and slip into the straps from the ledge: no reaching over the bed wall to lift a full tank. Adjust straps so it rides high and snug.

While you spray

Steady pressure, steady pace

Use the pressure or flow setting the tip and label call for, and keep it there. Walk at an even pace with the wand at a steady height. Let go of the valve when you stop, turn a corner or talk to someone.

The Clements band: up to 3 ft up and 3 ft out on soil, turf and mulch where the label allows (some are narrower), plus thresholds, windows, plant contact points and trash areas. Routine in PC-03.
Watch the wind and the surface. Coarse spray, low to the ground, aimed away from people, pets, pools, open windows and storm drains. Stop if it's drifting.

01 · Aerosols and gel bait applicators

Put the product inside the crack, not around it

Aerosol with crack-and-crevice tip

  • Seat the straw firmly so it can't blow off. Insert the tip into the crack, void or hinge gap, then use short bursts while you move it along
  • Keep the can upright for steady pressure. Check the label for where indoor use is allowed
  • Aerosols are pressurized and usually flammable: no pilot lights or sparks nearby, never on a hot surface
  • Store upright, capped, in the locked toolbox, out of heat. Straws back on the can so they don't get lost or contaminated

Gel bait applicators

  • Bait gun or pre-filled syringe with a fine tip. Small spot placements where roaches and ants travel and harbor, out of reach of children and pets, in the amount the label directs
  • Never spray insecticide or cleaner over a bait placement: contaminated bait gets avoided
  • Rotate baits as directed to prevent bait aversion and resistance
  • Wipe the tip, cap it and keep the plunger clean. Heat thins gel, so store it cool and sealed, away from solvent-based products
Updated from the old handbook: it said to "hold the can 12 inches away" and insert the tube. With a crack-and-crevice tip the tip goes into the gap. Where to treat inside the home is covered in PC-02.
02 · Dusters and hand spreaders

Dry products need dry equipment

Duster

  • For dry voids: wall voids, behind switch plates, weep holes and other places the label allows
  • Fill only about half full so there's air to carry the dust. Tap or shake it before each use
  • Puff lightly: a thin, barely visible film works; a pile repels and blows back. Wear the label's PPE, including eye protection
  • Keep it dry: water clumps dust. Never rinse it. Cap the tip and store it in a sealed bag in the toolbox
  • Electrical outlets and switches: follow PC-02 and the label exactly. Never push a metal tip into live wiring

Hand spreader

  • For the granular perimeter band, usually two to three feet wide
  • Gate closed while you fill and whenever you stop walking. Set the opening and walking pace for the product (PC-04 covers calibration)
  • Keep granules off driveways, patios and walks, and out of storm drains. Sweep or blow back onto the turf anything that lands on a hard surface
  • Empty leftovers back into the labeled bag at the end of the day. Granules left in the hopper cake and corrode it
  • Clean, dry and check the gate and spinner. Cross-contamination ruins the next product
03 · Inspection and finishing tools

The small tools customers judge us by

Spider brush

Webs off eaves on every visit, even if the account doesn't list it. Look for wasps first. Keep the pole at least 10 feet from overhead power lines (OSHA 1910.333). Clear the bristles away from doors.

Flashlight

Inspection is the foundation of every service. Charge it nightly and carry backup batteries. An inspection mirror and moisture meter help under appliances and with wet areas.

Monitors

Glue boards along walls, behind appliances, under sinks. Date and number them, record them, and keep them out of reach of kids and pets. Replace dusty or full ones.

Shoe covers

On as you step inside, off as you step out, every visit, even if the customer says not to worry. Torn cover? New pair. Never wear them outside and back in.

Why it matters: customers rarely see the product work. They see webs gone from the eaves, covers on your boots and a flashlight checking under their sink. That's the evidence of a careful service.
04 · EVO rodent bait stations and keys

The station is the proof. Treat it like the customer is watching.

  1. 1Gloves on before you touch a station, every time.
  2. 2Unlock with the key and open every station, every visit. Look for activity, moisture, mold, dead rodents and damage.
  3. 3Clean it out, wipe the lid, clear vegetation from the entry holes and check the drainage hole.
  4. 4Fresh, in-date bait secured on the rod. Old bait goes in a sealed bag. Never a loose block on the floor of the station.
  5. 5Close, lock, tug-test. Check that it's still anchored, and record activity for that station.
Keys stay on you. Clipped to your belt or key ring, never left in a station or on a ledge. A lost key means a station anyone could open.
Carry spare stations. A cracked lid or broken latch is replaced on the spot, then recorded. Placement and rebaiting decisions are covered in PC-07.

01 · The care routine

Small checks, on a schedule

Depressurize before you open anything. Lid, nozzle, valve or hose: release the pressure first and point it away from your face.
Never blow out a nozzle with your mouth, and never push a pin or wire through it. That's an exposure, and a reamed tip sprays wrong.
Rinse water is rinsate. Spray it onto a site the label allows. Never down a sink, storm drain or onto pavement (FC-04).
