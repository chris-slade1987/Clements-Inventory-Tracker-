Why this course matters

The lawn shows every mistake your equipment makes.

"Keeping equipment clean and in good working order to prevent malfunctions and ensure consistent application."CLEMENTS L&O TRAINING · PROPER SPRAY APPLICATION

Hundreds of gallons on board

A rig carries far more mix than any other Clements truck. One failed fitting or overfilled tank is a spill on a customer's driveway or in a storm drain.

The state checks it

FDACS rules require an air gap when you fill and locked concentrates on the truck (5E-14.106). Drift off target is illegal under Florida law (s. 487.031).

Customers see the stripes

A clogged tip leaves streaks; a spreader left open on a turn leaves a burned arc. The customer looks at that lawn every day until it grows out.

It can hurt you

Pressurized hoses, hot engines, spinning reels and a heavy ride-on machine on a ramp. Habits keep you safe.

02 · The tank and agitation

A tank of mix is only as good as it is evenly mixed

The main tank holds the day's liquid: water plus the products for the visit. Some products dissolve; others are particles suspended in water that settle to the bottom the moment the water stops moving. If the mix settles, the first lawn gets a dose that's too strong and the last one gets mostly water.

What goes in the tank, how much, and in what order is set by the label and your supervisor. Mix rates and calibration are covered in LAWN-09 L&O Calibration & Mix Rates. Never state or guess a rate.

Hydraulic agitation

Part of the pump's flow returns to the tank through the bypass line or a jet agitator, keeping the mix moving whenever the pump runs.

Mechanical agitation

Some tanks have paddles on a shaft. If yours does, it runs whenever there is mix in the tank, per the manufacturer's directions.

Tank habits
  • Agitate before you spray and keep it running while you spray
  • Mix sat overnight or over lunch? Circulate it well before the first trigger pull
  • Lid sealed before you drive; never overfill. Overflow is a spill
  • Know exactly what's in the tank. A mix log or label travels with it (FC-05)
03 · Pump, valves and plumbing

From the tank to the gun, and back again

Suction valve

Opens the tank to the pump. Open before the pump starts. A closed valve means the pump runs dry and wears out fast.

Strainer

Catches debris before it reaches the pump and tips. Most "weak spray" problems start with a dirty strainer. Check it daily.

Pump

Pushes mix to the gun. Know which type your rig has from your trainer. Never run it dry, and shut it off between stops.

Regulator and bypass

Sets working pressure and sends extra flow back to the tank. The gauge tells you what's happening; a sudden change means something's wrong.

04 · Practice · trace the flow

Follow a drop of mix from the tank to the lawn

Select the parts in the order the mix passes through them. When you troubleshoot, you'll walk this same path, starting at the tank.

Knowing the path is how you find a problem fast: no flow at the gun means checking the tank, the valve and the strainer before anything else.

01 · Guns, nozzles and pressure

Pattern decides where it goes. Pressure decides how far it drifts.

Lawn spray guns have an adjustable or changeable tip that turns from a wide fan to a cone to a narrow stream. Higher pressure makes smaller droplets, and small droplets hang in the air and travel. For lawn work you want the largest droplet that still covers.

Some labels name a droplet size, nozzle type or pressure limit. The label wins. Working pressure for your rig is set by your trainer from the nozzle chart.

Wide fan

A flat sheet for broadcast turf spraying. Sweep side to side at a steady walk and a steady height, slightly overlapping each pass.

Cone

A hollow or full cone that wraps foliage, for shrubs and ornamentals. Work from the base up to cover leaf undersides.

Narrow stream

Puts a heavy load in one spot. Rarely the right answer on a lawn: it causes runoff and uneven doses. Never use it to reach farther.

Change the setting before you start a section, never mid-pass. Release the trigger when you stop, turn or talk.

03 · Hoses and reels

A hose under pressure deserves respect

The hose carries mix at working pressure across the customer's property. A kink, a crushed section or a worn cover can split without warning. How you pull it out and wind it back decides how long it lasts.

The Clements checklist: while wearing gloves, properly wind up the hose to avoid kinks and damage. The outside of the hose carries residue.
Pulling out
  • Pull only what you need, and pull it straight off the reel
  • Route it around beds, decorations and personal property, never over plants or across a pool deck if you can avoid it
  • Don't drag it over sharp edges or let a car drive over it
  • Watch for kids, pets and tripping hazards across walkways
Winding in
  • Release pressure at the gun first if your rig calls for it
  • Guide it on in level, even layers with a gloved hand: no piles at one end
  • Keep fingers clear of the reel and the guide rollers, powered or hand-crank
  • Gun back in its holder, trigger locked, before the truck moves

Check the whole length weekly: blisters, cracks, soft spots, worn cover and weeping fittings mean it comes out of service.

04 · The SG52 sprayer spreader

A ride-on zero-turn that sprays and spreads

The Steel Green SG52 covers big lawns fast. It carries its own spray tanks (60 gallons, as a pair of 30-gallon tanks), spray booms, a hose reel for tight spots, a granular hopper and a control panel for speed, spray pressure and spreader settings.

Read the operator's manual for your unit. Specs, settings and procedures in it outrank anything general in this course.
Pre-operation check
  • Tires properly inflated
  • Hoses and nozzles secure, undamaged
  • Engine oil level and fuel
  • Air filter clean
  • Hopper and tanks loaded correctly, lids closed
  • Booms fold and lock
Safe operation
  • Label PPE on (FC-03), plus hearing protection whenever the engine runs
  • Brake engaged before you start; choke if needed, then let it idle a few minutes
  • Safe speed on slopes and rough ground; no sharp turns at speed. It can tip
  • Stay off steep embankments and away from the water's edge
  • Never leave it running unattended
04 · The SG52 sprayer spreader

Steady speed, slight overlap, off at the turns

Boom spraying

  • Mix agitated thoroughly before you start
  • Set pressure at the control panel
  • Lower the booms before moving forward
  • Consistent speed: speed changes the dose
  • Overlap slightly to avoid untreated gaps
  • Watch wind speed and direction

On-board hose reel

  • For obstacles and tight spaces the booms can't reach
  • Nozzle securely attached; pressure set for spot work
  • Keep the nozzle moving to avoid over-applying one spot
  • Retract carefully to prevent tangles and damage

Spreading granules

  • Hopper free of debris and clumps
  • Rate and width settings from the product label
  • Engage the spreader once moving at a steady pace
  • Spreader off for sharp turns so you don't double-dose
  • No granules into water, drains or the street (FC-04)
  • Don't spread on windy days
Spraying and spreading on the same pass doubles what you must watch. Your trainer decides when you're ready to run both at once.
05 · Backpack and handheld sprayers

The small-tank tools for the jobs the rig mix can't do

The treatment checklist: if the tank mix calls for it, prepare a backpack sprayer or small tank mix to treat shrubs, weeds or specific problem areas. A separate tank means you never put herbicide on a shrub just because it was in the main tank.

Electric backpack

Battery-driven, steady pressure. Charge it overnight; check the lid seal, strainer basket, straps and wand before the route. Fill at the tailgate and slip into the straps from there.

Manual backpack

Pump the handle to keep pressure steady. Uneven pumping means uneven spray. Same checks: lid, strainer, straps, wand and shut-off valve.

Handheld compression

For a few spots. Pump to working pressure, don't over-pump, and release pressure before opening the lid. A pressurized lid can spray you in the face.

Every small tank gets labeled with what's in it, every time (FC-02, FC-05). Rinse it at the end of the day; the rinsate goes onto a labeled site.
06 · Spreaders

Rotary throws wide. Drop puts it right below.

Rotary (broadcast)

Fast on open turf. Use the deflector shield along driveways, sidewalks, beds and water; several of our counties require it. Overlap passes as the bag or spreader chart directs.

Drop

Exact band between the wheels, for narrow strips, side yards and next to water. Overlap the wheel tracks slightly or you'll leave stripes.

Every spreader, every time
  • Setting from the product label; calibration in LAWN-09
  • Fill on the turf or truck, never on pavement; close the gate first
  • Walk first, then open; close the gate before you stop or turn
  • Header strips at the ends, then back and forth between them
  • Sweep or blow strays back onto the turf before you leave

01 · Filling with an air gap

The hose never goes into the tank

FDACS Rule 5E-14.106(5), F.A.C.

Spray tanks may not be filled through a hose or pipe protruding into the tank. The fill hose must end at least 2 inches above the tank's fill opening, or have an effective anti-siphoning device, so mix can't be pulled back into the water supply.

  • Park and fill away from storm drains, wells and water (FC-04)
  • Stay with the tank the whole time it fills. Overflow is a spill
  • Fill water first per the mixing order; products go in as the label directs
  • Hose stays clean: never lay the end in the mix or on the ground in a puddle

Correct. Water falls through open air into the tank. If the supply pressure drops, nothing in the tank can be pulled back up the hose.

03 · End-of-day cleaning

Clean it today so it works tomorrow

Truck rig

  • Pump off, pressure released, valves closed
  • Clean the strainer and any clogged tips with water and a soft brush
  • Hose wound level; gun in its holder, trigger locked
  • Look over hoses and fittings for leaks or wear
  • Lid sealed; contents of the tank known and recorded

SG52

  • Rinse the whole machine: booms, nozzles, hopper, wheels
  • Blow off moisture with compressed air to prevent rust. Eye protection on
  • Check nozzles, hoses, fittings and moving parts
  • Check oil; look at the air filter
  • Weekly or per manual: grease fittings, flush tanks and hoses

Spreaders and small tanks

  • Empty the hopper back into the labeled bag; fertilizer pulls moisture and corrodes metal
  • Rinse, dry and lightly lubricate per the manual
  • Backpacks and handhelds rinsed, pressure released, batteries on charge
  • Tanks labeled, lids on, secured
Never blow out a tip with your mouth or poke it with wire or a knife. Never wash equipment near a storm drain, well or water. Rinse water goes into the spray tank and onto a labeled site (FC-04).
04 · Cleaning and storage, Florida-style

We don't winterize for months of snow. We do plan for sun, rain and the odd freeze.

Heat and sun

UV cracks hoses and seals: keep the hose on the reel, not lying in the bed. Clear grass from engine cooling fins and the air filter. Keep concentrates shaded and locked (FC-05). Hydrate.

Rainy season

Never apply in rain. Dry equipment after every rinse to fight rust. Keep fertilizer bags sealed and dry. Most of our counties ban nitrogen and phosphorus June 1 to Sept 30, which changes what goes in the spreader (FC-04).

Cold snaps

Central Florida gets freezes, Orlando most often. Water left in a pump, gun or line can freeze and crack it. When a freeze is forecast, follow your branch plan: drain per the manual or park the rig indoors.

Slow weeks are for maintenance: inspect every hose length, replace worn tips and strainer screens, grease what the manual lists, and book repairs with your supervisor.
05 · Loading the truck safely

The ramp is where the SG52 gets dangerous

  1. 1Level ground, truck in park, parking brake set, engine off.
  2. 2Ramp seated and locked to the truck as designed; no gap, no wobble, clean of mud and wet grass.
  3. 3Everyone clear of the ramp, the sides and behind the machine.
  4. 4Straight line, slow and steady, in the direction the operator's manual specifies. Never turn on the ramp.
  5. 5Brake set, engine off, strapped down at the tie-down points before the truck moves.
Everything else on the truck
  • Concentrates locked whenever the truck is unattended (5E-14.106(3))
  • Backpacks, spreaders and containers upright and strapped so nothing shifts, tips or leaks
  • Florida law requires a secured load (s. 316.520)
  • Gas can approved and secured; refuel engines cold, never running
  • Spill kit on board before you leave the branch
Storage, securing and spills: FC-05. Load securing and the daily walk-around: FC-07.
Don't load or unload the SG52 on your own until your supervisor has signed you off on it. If anything about the ramp or the machine doesn't look right, stop and call your supervisor.
