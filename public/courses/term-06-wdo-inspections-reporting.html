Why this course matters

A WDO report is a legal document people buy houses on.

Buyers, sellers, lenders and agents make six-figure decisions on what one inspector writes on one state form. Every word has to be true, complete and defensible.

This course does not authorize you to perform WDO inspections. Only a WDO-category certified operator, or a special WDO ID cardholder working under one, may (s. 482.091(9)).

Regulated by statute and rule

Section 482.226, F.S. and Rule 5E-14.142, F.A.C. set the form, its contents, the notice, the standard and the 3-year retention. FDACS can discipline the company and the cardholder.

Liability that outlives the closing

The law requires WDO inspecting companies to carry errors and omissions insurance or a bond (or show qualifying net worth) because a missed or misstated finding can become a claim long after the sale.

Our name is on it

Agents remember which company writes clear, honest reports on time. One slanted report costs more trust than a hundred good ones earn.

01 · The six WDO groups

"All wood-destroying organisms" means more than termites

Florida defines a WDO as arthropod or plant life that damages and can reinfest seasoned wood in a structure: termites, powderpost beetles, old house borers and wood-decaying fungi (s. 482.021(30)). The inspection covers all of them. TERM-01 covers identification in depth.

01 · The state standard · Rule 5E-14.142(5)(c)

Four rules define every WDO inspection

Accessible by normal means

All areas you can reach normally. Not enclosed or concealed areas: behind wall and floor coverings, furniture, equipment, stored items, insulation.

Visual first

The inspection is visual. Probe and sound wood only when you have a visual finding of possible infestation or damage.

Nothing defaced

You never remove or deface any part of the structure to look. If seeing it would require that, it is not inspected, and the report says so.

All WDOs, fungi too

Includes wood-decaying fungi (brown, white and cubical rot). Not surface molds that don't damage sound wood.

The form itself warns the reader: the report covers visible, readily accessible areas only, is not a guarantee that WDOs or damage are absent, and is not a structural damage report.
No disclaimers. The rule bars adding disclaimers or extra language to FDACS-13645. Limits go in the sections built for them.

03 · Obstructed and inaccessible areas

What you couldn't see is a finding too

Obstructed

A visible, accessible area you could not fully inspect because something is in the way: stored boxes, furniture, appliances, insulation, wall or floor coverings, dense plants, a locked room. The law requires the area and the reason (s. 482.226(2)(a)4).

Inaccessible

An area with no normal access: an attic section with no hatch, a crawl area with too little clearance, enclosed wall voids, a sealed soffit. The law requires the areas that were inaccessible (s. 482.226(2)(a)5).

Be specific. "Attic: north half above the bedrooms, blown-in insulation over the ceiling joists" beats "attic: partial".
Section 3 of the form has lines for attic, interior, exterior, crawlspace and other, each with specific areas and a reason.
Never leave Section 3 blank to make a report look cleaner. An omitted limit reads as "inspected and clear."

01 · Four kinds of finding

Each has its own place on the form

Section 2 · B1

Live WDOs

Living termites, beetles or larvae seen at the time of inspection. Record the common name and location.

Workers in a freshly broken shelter tube; drywood termites in an opened gallery.

Section 2 · B2

Evidence

Signs the organism is or was present: dead WDOs or parts, frass, shelter tubes, exit holes, wings. Common name, description, location.

Empty tubes on the slab edge; pellets below trim; shed wings on a sill.

Section 2 · B3

Damage

Visible harm to wood caused by a WDO. Common name, description, location. Describe what you see, not how bad the structure is.

Galleries in a sill; honeycombed baseboard; cubical rot in a fascia.

Section 4

Previous treatment

Visible evidence a structure was treated before. Yes or no, and what you saw. You give no assurance about another company's work.

Patched drill holes in a slab; bait stations; an old treatment notice.

Nothing visible? Check Section 2 A: "No visible signs of WDO(s) (live, evidence or damage) observed." Never write "no termites" or "termite-free."
One spot, several boxes. Live drywood termites in a damaged sill with pellets below go in B1, B2 and B3.

Name, description, location. Observed, not guessed.

04 · Deliver, retain, stay honest

After the inspection, the report has three more jobs

Deliver

Findings go to the person who requested the inspection, and to the others named in Section 1. Real estate deals run on deadlines: the closing date and report-needed-by date are captured at intake.

Retain

Clements keeps a copy at least 3 years (s. 482.226(1)). V5 stores the FDACS-13645 copy in PestPac under the T-WDO service code. Re-inspections use T-WDO REINSPECT.

Stay independent

Section 5 certifies that neither Clements nor the inspector has a financial interest in the property or any role in the deal beyond inspecting (s. 482.226(2)(d)). Tell your supervisor about any conflict before you go.

Honesty is not negotiable. No one (agent, seller, buyer or coworker) can change what you observed. A false or incomplete report exposes the cardholder and the company to FDACS discipline and civil liability.
