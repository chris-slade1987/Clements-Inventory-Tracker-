Turf Pests

Find the insect before you blame it. How to diagnose chinch bugs, caterpillars, mole crickets, grubs, billbugs and the sap feeders on ornamentals, prove it with the right inspection, and treat it at the right time without harming pollinators or the customer's trust.

By the end of this course you will be able to:

Read a damaged lawn and tell chinch bug injury from drought stress and disease before any product comes off the truck
Choose and perform the right inspection: part the turf, flotation can, soap flush or cut the sod

Why this course matters

A brown patch is a question, not a diagnosis.

Chinch bugs, a dry sprinkler zone, brown patch and grubs can all leave a tired, off-color spot. Each one needs a different fix, and only one of them is fixed by an insecticide.

"Half of 'fungus' calls in Florida are not fungus at all. Diagnose before you spray."CLEMENTS FUNGUS IDENTIFICATION & CONTROL · 2026

The lawn is the customer's scoreboard

They see it every day. The southern chinch bug is the most damaging insect of St. Augustinegrass, Florida's most planted lawn grass. Miss it and a yard can be lost in a few hot weeks.

Guessing breeds resistance

UF/IFAS reports chinch bug populations resistant to pyrethroids, neonicotinoids and older classes after repeated use of the same chemistry. Every unneeded spray speeds that up.

The label and the law

Product only on labeled sites and pests, at label timing, off blooming plants and out of water (FIFRA, Chapter 482). Lawn treatments are posted, and customers can ask what was used.

01 · The diagnostic walk

Pattern, plant, conditions, then the pest itself

1

Read the pattern

Walk the lawn first. Patches by the driveway in full sun? Crescents between sprinkler heads? Circles in shade? Where it is tells you what it could be.

2

Pull the plant

Work at the edge, where green meets damaged. Chewed blades, a rotted sheath, roots gone, stems that snap at the crown, or a plant firmly anchored?

3

Check the conditions

Season, heat, rain, irrigation, mowing height, recent fertilizer, grass type. The weather and the symptoms have to agree.

4

Find the pest

No pest, no insecticide. Confirm with the right inspection, count against the threshold, and record what you found.

Open-ended can for the flotation test, plus water.
Dish soap and a watering can for the soap flush.
Hand lens and soil knife to part turf and cut a sod flap.

Part two

The southern chinch bug

Why it matters: it is the number one insect pest of St. Augustinegrass, it breeds 7 to 10 generations a year in south Florida, and it has beaten pyrethroids, neonicotinoids and even a resistant grass variety. Treat it carelessly and you help it win.

01 · Life stages and lookalikes

Small, fast, and living in the thatch

Life cycle: eggs hatch in about 8–25 days, faster in summer heat; nymphs molt through several stages; 3–4 generations a year in north Florida, 7–10 in south Florida. All stages can be present year-round.

03 · Practice · read the can

Count only the chinch bugs

You pushed a 6-inch can 2–3 inches into the soil at the edge of a yellowing patch, filled it and kept it topped up for five minutes. Select every chinch bug that floated up. Leave the beneficial insect and the debris alone.

Tip: chinch bugs are narrow with white wings or a pale band. The wide-headed bug with big eyes is something else.
UF/IFAS action level: 20–25 chinch bugs per square foot generally justifies treatment. A 6-inch can samples about one-fifth of a square foot. Check 3–4 spots around the patch, not one.
04 · Resistance and rotation

How one chemistry stops working

Rotate by IRAC group, not by brand

Most insecticide labels show an IRAC group number; if yours doesn't, ask your supervisor. Different brand names can be the same chemistry: several bifenthrin products in our older decks are all group 3A. Switching between them is not rotation.

IRAC groupClass · example active
3APyrethroids · bifenthrin, lambda-cyhalothrin
4ANeonicotinoids · imidacloprid, clothianidin
28Diamides · chlorantraniliprole · not for chinch bugs
Grass can lose too. Floratam was released as chinch-resistant in 1973; Florida chinch bugs overcame it within about 15 years. Cultural care still matters: mow standard St. Augustine at 3.5–4 inches (LAWN-04), water only at wilt, no heavy nitrogen, manage thatch.
05 · Treating chinch bugs the right way

Confirmed, counted, targeted, followed up

Confirm

Bugs found at the patch margin, counted in 3–4 spots. Record the counts and where they were.

Target

Treat the damaged area plus a border of the green turf around it, where the bugs are moving next, as the label allows.

Reach the thatch

Chinch bugs live in the thatch and top inch of soil. Follow the label on spray volume and any watering-in so product gets there.

Follow up

Recheck the edges next visit. Live bugs after a labeled application means rotate the group and tell your supervisor.

Rates come from the label, never from memory or this course. Product choice follows the current Clements L&O product list; mixing and calibration are in LAWN-09.
Spare the helpers. Big-eyed bugs, earwigs and a tiny egg wasp all eat chinch bugs. Targeted treatment leaves them working in the rest of the lawn.

05 · Talking to the customer

Name the pest, show the proof, set the timeline

What you say
  • Use the real name and show them: the bugs in the can, the larva from the flush, the grub under the flap.
  • "Dead St. Augustine won't green back up. It has to grow back in from the healthy edges." Resod or repair questions go to the branch manager.
  • "Seeing moths doesn't mean the lawn is in trouble. We check for the caterpillars before we treat."
  • "The black coating is sooty mold growing on honeydew. Once the insects are controlled it weathers away."
  • Never promise results or quote prices, and never call a product "safe" or "harmless."
What you leave
  • Lawn and exterior foliage posting as required (482.2267), and a fully completed Notice of Application sign.
  • Area of Concern flags with a short note on what was found and treated.
  • PestPac notes: pest, counts, where, what was treated, the watering or mowing advice. Customers can read them.
  • Anything the office must act on goes to technotes@clementspestcontrol.com.
  • People and pets off treated areas until dry, or as the label says. Product details on request (482.2265).
