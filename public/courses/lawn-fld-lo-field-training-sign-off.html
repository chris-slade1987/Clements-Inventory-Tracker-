Course objectives

By the end of L&O field training you will be able to:

Explain what Florida law requires before you work alone in L&O: five days and 40 documented hours in this category, separate from GHP
Describe the five-day progression from ride-along observation to running the L&O route with your trainer watching
Set up, run and clean the L&O rig: tank, pump, hose reel, ride-behind spreader-sprayer and backpack sprayer
Walk the yard and diagnose before treating: turf pests, fungus, drought stress and weeds
Follow fertilizer rules, post the notice of application, flag concerns and document every stop
Log L&O hours accurately, know who signs each record, and judge honestly when you're ready to go solo
Why this course matters

The whole street sees your work.

A lawn application happens outdoors, near storm drains, ponds, pets and neighbors. Mistakes show up as dead shrubs, burned turf or product in the water. Field training is where you prove you won't make them.

It's the law, per category

Fla. Stat. 482.091(3) bars you from performing, soliciting, inspecting or applying L&O pest control on your own until you finish at least 5 days of field training in that category. GHP days don't count.

Florida's water is protected

Fertilizer and pesticide runoff feeds algae blooms. State rules and local ordinances limit what goes down, when and where, with fines for violations.

Customers judge what they see

They can't read a label, but they can see a posted notice, a flagged weed, a clear door hanger and a lawn that improves. That's what keeps them on the program.

01 · What Florida requires

Two rules, two minimums, both must be met

Statute · Fla. Stat. 482.091(3)

At least 5 days of field training

  • Before you perform, solicit, inspect or apply on your own
  • Under the direct supervision, direction and control of a certified operator
  • In the appropriate category. Lawn & Ornamental is its own category: GHP days don't count toward L&O
Rule · FAC 5E-14.1421

40 hours, documented

  • The initial five-day training is 40 hours
  • Given by a certified operator, or a trainer the certified operator in charge designates in writing
  • Verified on form FDACS-13665 (Verification Record of Initial Employee Training) or an equivalent attendance record, a separate one for L&O
  • Kept by Clements for at least 2 years
5L&O days minimum. Four long days don't add up to five, even with 40 hours.
40L&O hours minimum. Five short days don't add up to 40. Days are added until both are met.

02 · Practice

Does it count toward your 40 L&O hours?

Supervised lawn and ornamental training counts. Breaks, paperwork, solo work and other categories don't. Clements makes the final call on edge cases, so ask if you're unsure.

04 · Your L&O credentials

Three things you carry, or confirm, before you work alone

FDACS ID card

Clements applies within 30 days of hire; HR starts it on Day 1. Carry it and show it on request (482.091).

L&O training record

Daily ride-along reviews and the L&O FDACS-13665: 5 days and 40 hours in this category, kept 2 years.

Fertilizer certificate

Everyone who applies fertilizer for hire needs their own FDACS Limited Urban Commercial Fertilizer Applicator certificate, which starts with UF/IFAS GI-BMP training (s. 482.1562). See LAWN-05.

No certificate, no spreader. Supervision doesn't count: you may not apply fertilizer, even with your trainer beside you, until your own certificate is issued. Your branch manager confirms your status and timing.
On request at any stop, give the business name, your ID card number, the product name, its active ingredient and the label's safety information (482.2265). See FC-02.

02 · Fertilizer, posting and water rules

The rules your trainer checks at every stop

Rainy-season restrictions

Many Florida counties and cities, including much of our service area, restrict nitrogen and phosphorus fertilizer June 1 to September 30. Rules vary by county and city: follow your branch's list and LAWN-05.

Rate comes from the bag

Urban turf fertilizer labels set the nitrogen per application, and quick-release nitrogen is capped lower than slow-release. Apply the label rate with a calibrated spreader. Never "a little extra."

Keep it out of the water

No granules or spray into ponds, canals, storm drains or swales. Sweep granules off driveways and sidewalks back onto the turf. Local ordinances can add buffer zones near water.

Post the notice

A notice of application, at least 4 by 5 inches with our business name, goes up in a conspicuous spot at the time of a lawn or exterior foliage application (rule 5E-14.147). Fill in the product information.

The sensitive registry

Neighbors on the FDACS pesticide-sensitive registry get notice at least 24 hours ahead of a lawn application nearby (482.2267). The office tracks the list; check your order notes.

Drift and herbicide

Check the wind before you spray and lower pressure near beds. Herbicide goes on turf only: never spray shrubs or plants with an herbicide mix.

01 · How L&O hours are logged

From the truck to the file

1

Log real times

Write the actual start and end time on the daily review. Subtract lunch and other breaks to get total hours trained.

2

Mark it L&O

Check Lawn & Ornamental Care and list treatments, equipment and issues seen, so the hours count in the right category.

3

Both sign, same day

You and your trainer sign and date it that day. It goes to HR, and the hours go in the CanopyOS field-hour log.

4

L&O FDACS-13665

Each session's date, topic, sign-in and sign-out, and trainer ID. Completed at 5 days and 40 hours, kept 2 years.

Clements' L&O record lists five topics: pest ID, pesticide selection and application, SDS and chemical handling, PPE, and environmental protection. Your days cover all five.
Times on the form must be the real ones, not a pre-printed 8:00 to 4:00. If the day ran short, the record should say so.
Keep your own running L&O total, separate from any GHP hours. You should always know where you stand.
