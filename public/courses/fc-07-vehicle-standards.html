01 · The everyday standard

Clean and orderly every day, not just on Friday

The handbook is plain: the company vehicle must be kept clean inside and out, at all times, with all materials well organized, properly labeled and in good repair. The outside picks up road dirt during the week and the Friday wash resets it. There's no excuse for a messy inside.

Once a truck is assigned to you, keeping it at standard is your job. Your supervisor inspects it monthly, and Graham Foster, Director of Field Operations, reviews vehicles and equipment on branch audits.
What "at standard" looks like
  • Nothing on the dashboard or tucked in the visors. Paperwork lives in folders or the storage area.
  • At the end of every day: used gloves, towels, dirty clothing, empty containers and trash come out.
  • No hats or cloth on the dash. Florida sun fades them and the dash.
  • Equipment organized, labeled and stowed in the same place every day.
  • Toolbox closed and locked. The truck locked whenever you walk away, for any reason.
  • Glass, mirrors and lights clean enough to see and be seen.

03 · The weekly protocol

Every Friday afternoon, back at your local office

Wash and clean your truck when you return Friday afternoon. Off on Friday? Do it Thursday afternoon. Leaving for the week without cleaning it is a write-up.

Exterior

  • Rinse off loose dirt, then soap and water on every painted area: hood, roof, sides, bumpers
  • Glass and mirrors, including the backs where bugs collect
  • Grease and brake dust off hubcaps, tires and fender-well lips
  • Rinse all soap; no bug splatter left on headlights or bumper
  • Towel dry, including the roof line over the windows

Interior

  • All trash out; nothing on the dash or in the visors
  • Vacuum the floor with seats all the way forward, then all the way back; check behind the seat
  • Damp-wipe dash, doors, handles, trim, steering wheel and buttons
  • Cup holders, console and white door jambs spotless
  • Floor mats shaken and wiped; floor wiped under the seats

Toolbox and bed

  • Aerosols organized; B&G clean, no leaks in the box bottom
  • Lock closes and locks properly
  • Every label present and legible; water containers marked "H2O"
  • SDS and spill kit stored and complete
  • Empty cans, containers and waste removed
  • Final lap around the truck; open both doors to check

03 · Maintenance you own

You schedule it. Your supervisor coordinates it.

  1. 1Know what's due. Oil changes, tire rotations and brake inspections are on your maintenance log, with last and next dates.
  2. 2Book it two to three weeks ahead with your supervisor so it fits the route.
  3. 3Plan around PTO so the truck is ready when you are, and isn't out of service the week you're back.
  4. 4Stay current. The monthly inspection checks that the next oil change is done or scheduled within 500 miles of when it's due.
  5. 5Basic checks are yours too: tire pressure, tire wear, lights and oil level, on a regular basis.

Report to your supervisor immediately

  • A warning light on the dash
  • Unusual sounds, vibrations or pulling
  • Brakes that feel soft, grab or grind
  • Fluid leaking under the truck
  • A seat belt missing or not working: supervisor and management, right away
  • Any damage or needed repair, however small
Do not start your route with a safety problem. Report it, and wait until it's fixed or your supervisor gives you another vehicle or clear direction.

Ignoring warning signs turns a cheap repair into an expensive one, and can make the truck unsafe on the road.

01 · Rules of the vehicle

Company vehicle, company rules

Work use only

The vehicle is provided for your assigned service work. It is not for personal use or personal errands.

Employees only

Only Clements employees ride in a company vehicle: no family, friends or customers. Everyone on board wears a seat belt.

Never in the truck

Alcohol or illegal drugs, even sealed, and smoking: grounds for immediate dismissal. No weapons; a small pocket knife is fine.

Locked, always

Never leave the vehicle unlocked, at any time, for any reason. That includes a two-minute stop at a customer's door.

Fuel card

Only for your company vehicle, only on days you have scheduled work, and only at an authorized station.

Parking and paperwork

Park safely, backing in when you can. Carry your license. Moving violations are yours; turn any parking ticket in to HR right away.

04 · Florida's marking rule

The business name, on both sides, in plain view

FDACS Rule 5E-14.103, F.A.C.
  • Every vehicle and trailer used for pest control carries the licensee's registered or trade name
  • On both sides, in bold letters at least 1½ inches high, in a contrasting color, in plain view of the public
  • Any vehicle carrying pesticides or application equipment is marked permanently: paint or decals on the body
  • Magnetic signs are allowed only on sales vehicles that carry no pesticides or equipment

The rule requires the name. It does not require a license number on the vehicle.

Your part: keep the lettering clean and uncovered. Don't let ladders, hose, mud or a magnet hide it, and report peeling or damaged lettering to your supervisor.

01 · Accidents in a company vehicle

Stop. Make it safe. Call. Document.

  1. 1Stop at once. Check for injuries. Anyone hurt: call 911.
  2. 2Make it safe. Hazard lights on. If no one is hurt and the vehicle drives safely but blocks traffic, Florida law (s. 316.061) expects you to move it out of the travel lanes. Otherwise leave it where it is until police direct you.
  3. 3Call the police for every accident, no matter how small. That's Clements policy. Stay until they're done.
  4. 4Call your supervisor and HR right away.
  1. 5Photos and information: damage, scene, the other driver's name, address, license, plate and insurance, and witness names and phone numbers.
  2. 6Talk about fault only with the police. Don't admit or assign blame to anyone else.
  3. 7Contain any spilled product if it's safe to do so (FC-05).
  4. 8Complete the vehicle accident form the same day, and report for post-accident drug screening when told.
Pull over safely before you use your phone, even to call 911. Driving behavior, phone rules and GPS are covered next, in FC-08 Driver Safety & GPS.

03 · The same-day report

Supervisor right away. Written report the same day.

The vehicle accident form is in the incident packet in your truck. Fill it out before the day ends, even if the damage looks minor and no one was hurt. Facts written down the same day hold up; memories a week later don't.

Send your photos to your supervisor with the form. Keep your insurance card where you can reach it at the scene.
What goes on the form
  • Date, time and exact location
  • What happened, in plain facts: speeds, directions, what you saw
  • Other driver: full correct name, address, license number, plate and insurance company
  • Witnesses: full names, addresses and phone numbers
  • The responding agency and any report or case number the officer gives you
  • Damage to every vehicle and property, with photos
  • Injuries, and whether anyone was taken for care

Leave out guesses about fault. That's for the police and the insurance companies.
