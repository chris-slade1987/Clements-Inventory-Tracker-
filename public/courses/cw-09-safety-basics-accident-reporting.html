Injuries are rarely dramatic

Most workplace injuries come from ordinary things: a wet floor, a cord across a walkway, a box lifted the wrong way. Office or field, nobody is exempt.

It's the law, and it's policy

OSHA requires a safe workplace, and Florida law sets strict reporting rules. Clements policy says to report unsafe conditions right away. Ignoring a hazard can lead to discipline.

Late reports hurt you

A quick report gets you care sooner and protects your workers' comp claim. A late one is harder to tie to work, and the hazard stays there for the next person.

01 · Rights and responsibilities

Safety is part of everyone's job description

What Clements expects of you

  • Follow safety rules and wear the protective equipment your job calls for
  • Use caution in all work: no shortcuts, no horseplay
  • Report any unsafe condition right away to your supervisor or manager
  • Keep your work area clean and orderly. Clutter causes accidents and fires
  • Report every injury and near miss, however small

What you can expect from Clements

  • A workplace kept free of known, serious hazards (OSHA)
  • Training on the hazards of your job, including chemical hazards and Safety Data Sheets
  • The right protective equipment for the task
  • Workers' compensation coverage for job injuries
  • No retaliation for reporting an injury, a hazard or a safety concern
Under the Employee Handbook, violating a safety rule, creating a hazard, or failing to report or fix one can lead to discipline, up to and including termination.
02 · Slips, trips and falls

The most common injury, and the easiest to prevent

Office

Cords across walkways, open file drawers, wet floors by the sink or cooler, boxes in hallways. Never stand on a rolling chair: use a step stool.

Warehouse

Spilled product or water, hoses on the floor, pallets jutting into aisles, poor lighting. Clean up or block off spills right away. Keep aisles and exits clear.

Field and truck

Wet grass, uneven yards, hidden holes, hoses, pool decks. Use three points of contact getting in and out of the truck or bed. Don't jump down.

Footwear matters. Closed-toe, slip-resistant shoes for anyone working in the warehouse or field. CW-07 covers the dress code.
See it, own it. If you can fix a hazard safely (pick up the cord, wipe the spill), fix it. If you can't, block it off and report it.
03 · Lifting and carrying

Lift with your legs. Keep it close. Don't twist.

  1. 1Size it up. Test the weight, clear your path. Heavy, awkward or over about 50 lb? Get a cart, a hand truck or a second person.
  2. 2Get close. Feet shoulder-width apart, load between your feet.
  3. 3Bend your knees, not your back. Back straight, chest up, tighten your core.
  4. 4Lift smoothly with your legs, holding the load close to your belly. No jerking.
  5. 5Turn your feet, not your waist. Twisting while loaded is how backs get hurt.
  6. 6Set it down the same way, bending at the knees.
Field: product jugs, bait cases and equipment out of a truck bed. Office: copy-paper cases and file boxes. Same rules.
04 · Heat basics · everyone in Florida

Know the difference. One of these is a 911 call.

Heat exhaustion

Heavy sweating, cool clammy skin, headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness, cramps. Stop working. Move to shade or AC, loosen clothing, sip water. Tell your supervisor. No better within an hour, or getting worse? Get medical care.

Heat stroke · emergency

Confusion, slurred speech, fainting, seizures, hot skin, very high body temperature. Call 911. Cool the person right away with water, ice and shade, and stay with them until help arrives.

Drink water about every 15 to 20 minutes on hot days, before you feel thirsty.
Take breaks in shade or AC. New employees and anyone back from time off need a week or two to acclimate.
Working heat in full PPE is covered in depth in FC-03.

01 · Warehouse and storage

A safe warehouse is an orderly warehouse

Exits and aisles

Exit doors, exit routes and the path to every extinguisher, eyewash and electrical panel stay clear at all times. Never block or lock an exit from the inside.

Pallets and shelving

Heaviest items low, lighter items high. Stack square and stable, nothing hanging over the edge. Don't climb shelving. Keep pallets out of walkways.

Ladders and equipment

Stored ladders are strapped to the wall so they can't fall. Never repair or clear a powered machine unless it's off and can't restart. Report broken equipment.

Right to know. Every chemical we store has a Safety Data Sheet (SDS), and you have the right to see it. Ask your supervisor where your branch keeps them.
Pesticide storage and spills: FC-05. Ladder use: FC-06. PPE and chemical exposure: FC-03.
02 · Fire and evacuation

When the alarm sounds, you leave. Every time.

  1. 1Stop what you're doing. Hang up the call, leave the truck. Don't stop for belongings.
  2. 2Alert others on your way out. Pull the alarm if it isn't sounding.
  3. 3Leave by the nearest safe exit. Close doors behind you. Smoke? Stay low.
  4. 4Go to the assembly point and check in, so no one goes back looking for you.
  5. 5Call 911. Tell firefighters the building stores pesticides. Never go back in until they say so.

Know before you need it

On your first day, learn two ways out of your work area, where the assembly point is, and where the nearest extinguisher and pull station are.

Prevent it

No daisy-chained power strips or extension cords as permanent wiring. Keep space heaters away from paper and switch them off when you leave. Smoking only where it's allowed.

03 · Using an extinguisher

Fight it only if it's small, and only with a way out

Use an extinguisher only on a fire smaller than a wastebasket, with your back to a clear exit, after the alarm is pulled. If it doesn't go out fast, leave. Select each step.

Spreading, smoky, or involving chemical containers? Don't fight it. Get out and call 911. Your life is worth more than the building.
04 · First aid and emergencies

First aid is for small things. 911 is for everything else.

The first aid kit

Your office, warehouse and service vehicle should each have one. Know where yours is. Typical contents: bandages, gauze, tape, antiseptic, gloves, cold pack, eyewash. Using it? Tell your supervisor so it gets restocked and the injury gets reported.

Helping someone who is bleeding? Put on gloves first. Avoid contact with blood, and wash your hands afterward.
Call 911 right away for
  • Trouble breathing, chest pain, or unconsciousness
  • Heavy bleeding that won't stop
  • A possible broken bone, head, neck or back injury
  • Heat stroke signs: confusion or very high temperature
  • A serious chemical exposure (FC-03)

01 · Why we report everything

Every serious injury sits on top of a pile of warnings

Near misses and small injuries far outnumber serious ones. Each is a free lesson. Report the bottom of the pyramid and the top stays empty.

Injury: anyone hurt, even a scratch or a sore back
Near miss: it almost happened, and nobody got hurt
Unsafe condition: a hazard waiting for someone
Vehicle accident: any collision or damage
02 · The Clements reporting steps

Supervisor right away. Written report the same day.

  1. 1Get care first. First aid, or 911 for anything serious. Make the area safe so no one else gets hurt.
  2. 2Tell your direct supervisor immediately, however minor. No answer? Leave a message and keep trying.
  3. 3Your supervisor brings in the branch manager and HR. HR handles workers' comp and tells you where to get care.
  4. 4Document the scene: photos of equipment or hazards, and witness names, while it's fresh.
  5. 5Complete the Workplace Accident Report the same day, even if you feel fine. Follow your call with an email.
  6. 6Post-accident drug screening when instructed, within the time you're given.
What the report covers
  • Date, time and exact location
  • What you were doing, and what happened, step by step
  • Who was hurt, what part of the body, and how
  • Equipment, materials or vehicles involved
  • What was done right after: first aid, 911, area secured
  • Witnesses' names and contact information
  • Photos attached

Pesticide exposure follows the same steps. See FC-03 for the exposure first aid.

05 · Vehicle accidents · the short version

In a company vehicle: stop, call, document, say only the facts

  1. 1Stop at once. Check for injuries. Anyone hurt: call 911.
  2. 2Make it safe. Hazard lights on. If no one is hurt and the vehicle drives safely but blocks traffic, Florida law expects you to move it out of the travel lanes. Otherwise leave it where it is.
  3. 3Call the police for every accident, no matter how small. That's Clements policy. Stay until they're done.
  4. 4Call your supervisor and HR right away.
  1. 5Photos and information: damage, scene, other driver's name, license, plate and insurance, witnesses.
  2. 6Talk about fault only with the police. Don't admit or assign blame to anyone else.
  3. 7Contain any spilled product if it's safe (FC-05).
  4. 8Fill out the vehicle accident form and report for post-accident drug screening when told.
Field employees go deeper in FC-07 Vehicle Standards and FC-08 Driver Safety & GPS. The same rule for everyone: no phone in your hand while driving. Pull over safely first, even to call 911.

Part four

Workers' compensation in Florida

If you're hurt on the job, workers' comp pays for your care and part of your lost wages. It works best when you report quickly, use the right doctors, and tell the truth.

  1. 01What it covers
  2. 02The deadlines

01 · What it covers

Insurance Clements carries on every employee

Medical care

Authorized treatment for a job injury or illness: doctor visits, tests, prescriptions and therapy, at no cost to you.

Lost wages

If the doctor keeps you off work or limits what you can do beyond the first week, you may receive a portion of your lost wages.

No-fault

Benefits don't depend on who caused the accident. In return, workers' comp is generally how job injuries are resolved, instead of lawsuits.

Questions about a claim? April Williford, Director of HR & Administrative Affairs. HR files the claim and gives you the information you need.
Florida law prohibits retaliation against anyone who files or tries to file a valid workers' comp claim.

03 · Authorized care and drug-free workplace

Use the right doctor, take the test, tell the truth

Authorized providers

In Florida, the employer and its insurance carrier choose the treating doctor. HR will tell you where to go. Emergencies don't need approval: go to the ER. Unapproved non-emergency care may not be paid. You can request a one-time change of doctor.

Drug-free workplace

Clements tests after workplace accidents, vehicle or otherwise. Under Florida law, a positive test, or refusing the test, creates a presumption that the injury was caused by drugs or alcohol, and benefits can be denied.

Safety rules count

Knowingly refusing required safety equipment or ignoring a safety rule can cut benefits by 25%. A false or misleading statement to get benefits is a crime and ends the claim.

Bottom line: report it right away, follow HR's direction on where to get care, and tell the truth. That protects your health and your benefits.
