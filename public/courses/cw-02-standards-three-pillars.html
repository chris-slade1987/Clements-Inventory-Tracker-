01 · The five standards

Five standards every Clements employee is held to

1

Punctuality & professionalism

On time, prepared, presentable and courteous.

2

Knowledge

Know your work, our services and our systems, and keep learning.

3

Respect for property

Homes, company equipment and customer information.

4

Honesty & transparency

Clear about services and costs. Realistic, never over-promising.

5

Environmental consciousness

The label, the least product that works, and no waste.

The original standards were written for technicians. They apply to all of us: a CSR's "arrival" is phones on at 9:00, a sales rep's "property" includes the customer's personal information, and an office "treatment" is an accurate invoice.
02 · Standard one

Punctuality and professionalism

  • On time is respect for the customer's schedule and your teammates' work. It reflects on the whole company.
  • Running late? Call before you're late, not after. Falling behind on the day? Tell the office or your manager right away.
  • Respond the same business day. Return office calls and emails during the day. Nothing waits past end of business.
  • Look the part. Clean, neat and dressed to policy. CW-07 covers uniforms and dress code.
  • Stay courteous on every call, email and visit, including with coworkers and in disagreements.
03 · Standard two

Knowledge

  • Know your job, our services and our systems well enough to answer common questions and teach prevention.
  • "I don't know yet, I'll find out" is a professional answer. Then follow up. Guessing is not.
  • Some questions need care. "I'm pregnant, is the spray dangerous?" If you're not sure, get the branch manager or licensed staff involved. Never improvise a health answer.
  • Product advice belongs to licensed and certified staff. Florida law ties pesticide application to licensing (Chapter 482).
  • Keep learning. Each certification you earn adds to what you can do.
04 · Standard three

Respect for property

  • The customer's home: handle belongings with care and leave it as clean as you found it, or cleaner. Extra mile: bring the empty trash cans up from the road.
  • Company property: vehicles, equipment, phones and the office are ours to keep in good order.
  • Customer information is property too. Names, accounts and photos of homes are confidential. Never post or share them.
  • Damage something? Tell the customer and your supervisor right away so we can make it right.
In-home habits like shoe covers and moving furniture are covered in CW-03.

01 · Standard four

Honesty and transparency

  • Be upfront about the service, the agreement and the cost. Approved pricing only.
  • Don't over-promise. Tell the customer what to expect and what they can do to help.
  • Report what really happened, including what you couldn't get to or got wrong.
  • Records must be true. Times, notes, agreements and invoices must match the work. Customers with an email on file get the inspection report.
  • Recommend only what's needed. Extra service is good business only when the customer needs it.
Florida's pest control law lets FDACS discipline a licensee for fraud or misrepresentation. Being honest protects the license.

03 · Standard five

Environmental consciousness

  • The label is the law (federal FIFRA). Right product, right rate, right place, every time.
  • Prevention first. Inspection, exclusion and sanitation before product: integrated pest management (FC-01).
  • Never call a product "safe," "harmless" or "non-toxic." Federal pesticide rules treat these as false or misleading claims. Say how it's used and what the customer should do.
  • Everyone cuts waste: emailed reports over paper, efficient routes, recycling, no idling.
  • Spills and leaks get reported right away (CW-09).
04 · The 50% rule

Grade the visit

"Providing effective pest control service counts for 50% (or less) of how you are graded by customers."CLEMENTS TECHNICIAN HANDBOOK

The service is done right. Now turn on what the customer also notices and watch the grade. For a CSR the "service" is the right answer or booking; for sales, the right recommendation.

02 · When you fall short

Everyone misses sometimes. Own it fast.

  1. 1Tell the person affected, customer or coworker, plainly and right away.
  2. 2Tell your supervisor before they hear it from someone else.
  3. 3Make it right and note it on the account or record.
  4. 4Fix the cause so it doesn't happen twice.
03 · How standards are checked

Observation

Supervisor observation, branch audits and day-to-day coaching.

Customer feedback

Reviews, compliments, complaints and cancellations.

Records

Job times, notes, agreements and account history in PestPac.

Certifications

Sign-offs like the one at the end of this course.

Repeated or serious failures lead to coaching and, under the Employee Policy Handbook, discipline. Hiding a mistake is always worse than the mistake.
