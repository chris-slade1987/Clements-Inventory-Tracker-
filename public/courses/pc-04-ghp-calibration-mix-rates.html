GHP Calibration & Mix Rates

How to read the rate off the label, calibrate your B&G, backpack and spreader, and do the mixing math so every tank you carry is right.

Course objectives

By the end of this course you will be able to:

Find the mix rate, application volume and maximums on a label, and match them to the approved mix rate sheet
Run a catch test and calculate your sprayer's output in fluid ounces per minute
Time a measured test area and work out gallons of finished spray per 1,000 sq ft
Calculate how much concentrate goes in any tank for a target percent, and convert percent to ppm
Calibrate a hand spreader in pounds per 1,000 sq ft
Measure with the right tools and mix a tank in the order the label requires
Why this course matters

The right product at the wrong rate is still wrong.

The label rate is federal law. The only way to know you're applying it is to measure what goes in the tank and what comes out of the nozzle.

Over-application

A FIFRA and Chapter 482 violation. More residue where kids and pets live, runoff toward canals and ponds, stains on stucco and paint, and wasted product.

Under-application

Pests survive, the customer calls back, and the survivors are the ones least affected by the product. A weak mix costs a second trip and the customer's trust.

The certified operator's name is on it

Florida holds the certified operator in charge responsible for correct concentrations and formulations (482.152). Your math is their license.

01 · Three numbers on every label

Concentrate, finished spray, treated area

Mix rate

How strong the tank is

Fl oz of product per gallon, or a percent dilution. "0.33 to 1.0 fl oz per gallon of water."

Application volume

How much spray hits the ground

Gallons of finished spray per 1,000 sq ft or per linear foot. "About 1.5 gallons per 1,000 sq ft."

Maximums

The lines you can't cross

Product per 1,000 sq ft, times per year, days between treatments, band size. "Not more than 1 oz per 1,000 sq ft."

Product on the ground = mix rate × application volume. Mixing the tank right is only half the job. If your sprayer puts out too much, a perfect mix still goes over the label. That's why Part two is calibration.

Calibrating your sprayers

Two technicians can carry the same tank mix and put down very different amounts of product. Calibration tells you what your sprayer, your nozzle and your walking pace actually deliver.

01 · Same mix, different dose

The mix was perfect. The dose wasn't.

Both technicians mixed CrossCheck Plus at 1.0 fl oz per gallon, the top of the label range, and treated the same 1,000 sq ft band. The label maximum is 1 fl oz of product per 1,000 sq ft.

Technician B has two legal fixes: lower the output (nozzle, pressure, pace) until it's 1 gallon per 1,000 sq ft, or lower the mix to 0.66 fl oz per gallon, which puts down 0.99 fl oz at 1.5 gallons.
Technician A1.0 gal per 1,000 sq ft
1.0 fl oz
Technician B1.5 gal per 1,000 sq ft
1.5 fl oz
Label maximum: 1 fl oz of product per 1,000 sq ft

Product on the ground = 1.0 fl oz per gallon × gallons applied. Technician B is 50% over the label, without changing the mix.

02 · The calibration method

Output per minute, then gallons per 1,000 sq ft

  1. 1Prep. Clean water only in the tank. Check the screen, nozzle and gaskets. Charge the battery or pump to working pressure.
  2. 2Catch test. Spray into a graduated container for 30 seconds. Double it for fl oz per minute. Do it three times and average.
  3. 3Mark a test area like the real job, for example a 5 ft × 100 ft band (500 sq ft).
  4. 4Time yourself spraying it at your normal pace, nozzle setting and band.
  5. 5Calculate gallons per 1,000 sq ft and compare it to the label.
  6. 6Adjust and repeat (pace, nozzle or pressure) until you hit it. Write the numbers down.
The math
fl oz used = fl oz per min × minutes
gal per 1,000 sq ft = fl oz used ÷ sq ft × 1,000 ÷ 128
Worked example

Catch test: 40 fl oz in 30 s = 80 fl oz per min.
500 sq ft took 48 s = 0.8 min.
80 × 0.8 = 64 fl oz on 500 sq ft.
64 ÷ 500 × 1,000 = 128 fl oz = 1.0 gal per 1,000 sq ft.

03 · Practice · the calibration timer

Run the catch test, then do the math

Pick a sprayer and start the timer. When it stops, read the container. Each mark is 2 fl oz.

04 · B&G vs. backpack

Know what changes your output

B&G handheld

Compressed air. Pressure drops as you spray and as the tank empties, so output drops too. Pump back up to the same pressure you calibrated at. Never over-pump; it stresses the seals.

Electric backpack

The pump holds steady pressure, but a low battery or a different pressure setting changes output. Calibrate on a full charge at the setting you use on the route.

Nozzle and pace

A fan tip and a pin stream put out very different amounts. Labels call for a low-pressure, coarse spray outdoors. Walk the same pace every time.

Recalibrate when you change a nozzle or tip, after any repair, when you change pressure settings, and any time the spray pattern looks off.
Daily care keeps calibration honest. Rinse and depressurize at the end of the day. A clogged screen or worn tip quietly changes your output.

01 · Measuring tools and units

Measure it. Never eyeball it.

Graduated measuring cup marked in fl oz and mL, used only for pesticide. Read it on a level surface, at eye level.
Syringe or small cylinder for amounts under an ounce. A 0.4 fl oz dose is too small to read on a big cup.
Scale for granules. Weigh, don't scoop.
Never use kitchen spoons or cups, or take tools home. Rinse the measuring cup into the tank so all the product goes where it belongs.

03 · The mix formula

When the label gives a percent

fl oz of concentrate = target % × gallons × 128 ÷ product %
Example 1

CrossCheck Plus at 0.06%, 1 gallon

0.06 × 1 × 128 ÷ 7.9 = 0.97 fl oz. The label rounds this to 1.0 fl oz.

Example 2

CrossCheck Plus at 0.02%, 4-gallon backpack

0.02 × 4 × 128 ÷ 7.9 = 1.30 fl oz. The label's 0.33 per gallon × 4 = 1.32 fl oz.

Example 3

Taurus SC at 0.06%, 1 gallon

The formula gives 0.84 fl oz. The label says 0.8 fl oz, because its percent is by weight. Use the label's number.

When the label gives fl oz per gallon, use it, multiplied by your tank size. The formula is for checking, and for labels that only give a percent.
"Per gallon of water" or "of finished dilution"? CrossCheck Plus goes into a gallon of water. Taurus SC is topped up to make one gallon. Follow the wording.

06 · Mixing order · select the steps in order

Mix a backpack load in order

The Taurus SC label: fill the tank 1/4 to 1/3 with water, add the product, then add water while agitating to the final volume.
Tank mixes only when both labels allow it and your supervisor approves. CrossCheck Plus lists the order: wettable powders, then liquids and flowables, then emulsifiable concentrates, agitating after each.
Mix only what you'll use today. CrossCheck Plus says not to let a tank mix stand overnight.
Mix away from storm drains, wells and water. Wear the label PPE, with goggles when pouring concentrate (FC-03). Spill: the 3 Cs and your supervisor (FC-05).

Granules and the daily routine

01 · Calibrating a hand spreader

Pounds per 1,000 sq ft

  1. 1Weigh the spreader with product in it, or weigh the product you load.
  2. 2Set the gate to the starting setting from the label or spreader chart.
  3. 3Spread a measured strip, for example 5 ft × 50 ft (250 sq ft), at your normal pace and crank speed.
  4. 4Weigh again. The difference is what you applied.
  5. 5Calculate: lb used ÷ sq ft × 1,000. Open or close the gate and repeat until you're in the label range.
Worked example

Before 5.00 lb, after 4.55 lb = 0.45 lb used.
0.45 ÷ 250 × 1,000 = 1.8 lb per 1,000 sq ft.
Job: a 3 ft band around 180 ft of foundation = 540 sq ft. At 2 lb per 1,000, that's 1.08 lb.

Keep granules off driveways, patios and walks. Blow them back into the bed, and keep them away from storm drains.

03 · Calibration on the route

The habits that keep every tank legal

Read every new jug

Labels change. Check the rate on each new container against the approved sheet before you mix from it.

Plan the tank

Mix for the stops you have. Leftover spray goes on a labeled site at or below the label rate. Never in a drain or on the ground.

Record the real amount

Florida requires the product and amount on the service record (5E-14.142). Enter what you actually used in PestPac Mobile.

Know your numbers

Your fl oz per minute, your gallons per 1,000 sq ft, your spreader setting. Keep them with your equipment.

Count the year

Taurus SC at 0.06% is limited to 2 applications per calendar year. Check the service history before a callback.

Unsure? Ask

Product choice, rates and formulations belong to the certified operator. Call your supervisor before you guess.
