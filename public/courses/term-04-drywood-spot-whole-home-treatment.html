01 · Drywood in sixty seconds

A colony that lives and eats inside the wood

No soil contact. Drywood termites get their water from the wood. No mud tubes, and Sentricon never treats them.
Small, slow, scattered. Small colonies that grow slowly; one structure can hold several. Swarmers start new ones nearby.
Where we see them: attic framing, eaves, window and door frames, trim, cabinets and furniture. The West Indian drywood termite is the common structural species in South Florida.

04 · Inspection technique

Look, listen, probe, ask

Raking light

Hold your flashlight low and almost flat to the wood. Kick-out holes (about 1 to 2 mm), blistering and pellet piles cast shadows and jump out.

Sounding

Tap along trim and framing with a screwdriver handle. Galleried wood sounds hollow or papery next to solid wood.

Probing

A careful probe where wood sounds hollow shows galleries. Probe only where needed, and never on finished surfaces without the customer's OK.

Moisture meter

Drywood termites need no outside moisture. High readings and soft, wet wood point to subterranean, dampwood or decay instead. Separate the evidence.

Clean-board test

Old pellets stay put for years. Fresh pellets on a clean glue board or paper towel under the spot show current activity.

Ask about history

"Have you ever had the house tented? Any prior treatment? When did you first notice the pellets?" History separates fresh activity from old debris.

Not sure? Uncertain identification goes to the branch certified operator before anything is sold.

02 · Spot treatment, step by step

Drill, inject to refusal, treat the surface, plug, monitor

Before you drill, say it plainly: "This treats what we can see in this spot today. It isn't whole-structure protection."
  1. 1Inspect and photograph the area; confirm it meets the spot limit
  2. 2Set expectations with the customer
  3. 3Write every treatment location on the agreement and the treatment notice
  4. 4Drill into galleries with the smallest practical bit (face frames, trim, jambs)
  5. 5Inject Termidor Foam or Fuse Foam into exit holes, galleries and ports until refusal
  6. 6Apply Bora-Care to exposed, unfinished surfaces of the member where the label permits
  7. 7Plug drill points and wipe off excess product
  8. 8Place a glue board (or paper towel) directly under the spot as a frass monitor
  9. 9Photograph the finished work and the monitor in PestPac

04 · What Whole-Home means

Whole-Home is not tenting. Here's what it is.

Program definition (V5): Whole-Home Treatment applies liquid Bora-Care to all accessible wood in the structure and is sold two ways: Preventative (no active activity) and Corrective (active activity, adding Termidor Foam or Fuse Foam at every active site and a 60-day reinspection). The warranty covers the entire structure.
Limits disclosed at sale: scope is accessible framing. Ductwork, blown-in insulation, low clearance and finished spaces are inherent limits. Every board can't be reached; the goal is broad protective coverage.
No attic (townhouse, condo): drill-and-foam at exterior door and window frames and accessible wood penetrations, sold as Whole-Home. Confirm HOA responsibility for exteriors first.
05 · Running a Whole-Home job

Coat the wood, not the house

Before

Whole-structure inspection, signed graph, baseline photos. Expectations in writing: Preventative or Corrective, and that treatment can't reach colonies in inaccessible wood. Walk the hose path; photograph anything fragile or already damaged.

During

Drop cloths at the attic access, hose connections and under every hose run. Stage equipment outside or in the garage. Mix Bora-Care to the current label. Smallest practical tip, fine controlled spray: trusses, rafters, joists, sheathing edges, exposed sills, headers, garage framing and beams. Foam every active site.

After

Photograph the work; log the area treated; mark excluded areas on the graph. Flush every line with clean water right away. Sweep a small radius at the hatch for a clean baseline. Corrective: book the 60-day reinspection before you leave.

Never broadcast spray onto drywall ceilings or finished surfaces. Long hoses leak, drip, kink and burst, and every one of those is a damage claim.
Attic safety: work early, limit your time, hydrate, walk only on joists, and wear the label PPE (FC-03, FC-06). The branch manager audits every Whole-Home job.
06 · Products and the label

Three products in the V5 program. The label is the law.

Wood treatment

Bora-Care

A borate (disodium octaborate tetrahydrate) applied to bare, unfinished wood. It soaks into the wood and protects what it reaches. It can't penetrate paint, stain or sealers, which is why finished wood is a limitation.

Foam injection

Termidor Foam

Fipronil foam injected into kick-out holes, galleries, wall and cabinet voids and drilled ports until refusal. The foam carries the active ingredient through the gallery system.

Foam injection

Fuse Foam

Fipronil plus imidacloprid foam, used the same way: direct injection into galleries, voids and ports. Either foam is used at every active site.

Every application: products labeled for the pest, site and substrate; current labels on the truck; record brand, EPA registration number, quantity, equipment and locations. Mix ratios come from the current label, never memory.
When the customer asks: give the product name, active ingredient and label safety information (s. 482.2265). Never call a product "safe," "harmless" or "non-toxic."

01 · What treatment can and can't do

Promise the process. Never the result.

Can
  • Eliminate activity in the galleries the foam reaches
  • Protect accessible, unfinished wood that Bora-Care soaks into
  • Give a documented baseline so new activity can be recognized
  • Retreat verified new activity under the warranty terms
Can't
  • Reach colonies hidden in wood we can't access or see
  • Guarantee no other colonies already exist in the structure
  • Clean up old frass. We don't offer it: removing frass from hidden cavities isn't feasible
  • Repair damage. All drywood work is retreatment only
Old pellets fall for months. Vibration shakes old frass loose. Fresh pellets on a clean monitor mean new activity; pellets only in the photographed spots are old debris.
Baseline photos settle it. Warranty calls are judged against the original photo set from the same angles.
Never say "they'll be gone for good," "this kills every termite in the house," or quote a price. Pricing questions go to your branch manager.

03 · When the answer is fumigation

Some jobs are beyond spot and Whole-Home

Activity above the Corrective limit, spread across the structure, in inaccessible wood, or back after a prior corrective treatment usually needs a whole-structure fumigation. Clements does not currently perform, sell or refer tent fumigation.

  1. 1Stop. Don't sell Spot or Whole-Home for it, even if the customer asks
  2. 2Document the findings on the graph and in PestPac with photos
  3. 3Tell the customer honestly the activity is beyond what our services can resolve
  4. 4Tell your branch manager the same day, and email technotes@ so the account is noted
How to say it

"Drywood termites live inside the wood itself. We treat drywood where we can reach it: a single spot, or the whole home with Bora-Care. What I'm seeing has spread into wood we can't reach, and that usually means the house needs tenting, which we don't currently offer. I don't want to sell you something that won't solve this."

Don't name, recommend or hand off to a fumigation company. Referral isn't company policy today. If the customer asks who to call, your branch manager handles it.
Future service: V5 records a planned subcontracted fumigation model. None of it is in effect until Clements approves it.

03 · Follow-up and warranty timeline

What happens after you leave

Florida deadlines (Rule 5E-14.105(9)(a)): inspect within 30 calendar days of the owner's written notice; retreat within 90 days of discovery (180 for commercial or multi-unit).
Records: treatment and reinspection reports kept at least 3 years; disclosures signed and stored in PestPac; the branch manager reviews the graph, photos and records before the job closes.
