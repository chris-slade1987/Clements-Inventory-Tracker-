Why this course matters

The right add-on finishes the job. The wrong promise ends the trust.

An add-on should solve something you can point to. Offered that way, customers thank you for it. Offered as a sales pitch, or promised beyond what we do, it costs us the customer and the renewal.

Real health exposure

Droppings, urine and dead rodents carry leptospirosis, salmonella and rat-bite fever. One dry sweep in an attic puts that dust in your lungs and the customer's air.

The label and the law

Bait in exterior boxes and disinfectants are pesticides under FIFRA. The label is the law (FC-02). Tamper-resistant, anchored stations are required where children, pets or wildlife could reach bait.

Renewals depend on it

Exterior rodent boxes are required on every Plus warranty renewal and transfer. Customers who hear that from day one renew. Customers who hear it at renewal feel surprised.

The three add-ons

01 · Exterior rodent boxes

Always offered. Required at every Plus renewal.

  • What it is: tamper-resistant bait stations installed around the outside of the home, then refilled on a set schedule, every two months or quarterly.
  • Quantity: set at the inspection, based on what you find: activity, harborage, fruit trees, palms, neighbors.
  • Required: on every Plus warranty renewal (1-year or 3-year) and on every warranty transfer after a home sale.
  • Paired with Plus: the program recommends a discount. Whether and how much is the service advisor's call, not yours.
Boxes don't fix activity inside. Seal first, then bait. A rodent that feeds outside and gets back in through an open gap can die in a wall (CW-05).
Every stationAnchored, locked, labeled, keys on you (PC-01).
Every serviceOpen, clean, fresh bait on the rod, relock, record activity (PC-03).
02 · A/C chase sealing

A common Florida entry point, closed the Clements way

What it is: sealing the gap where the refrigerant line set passes through the wall. Rodents follow the lines straight in.
Already in Plus. On a Plus job the chase is an entry point inside the standard scope. As a standalone add-on, it's for customers outside the standard program.
Materials by size (RX-04): 1/2 to 1 inch, Xcluder or copper mesh packed tight, sealed with color-matched cement. Larger, hardware cloth plus cement. Foam is never the seal.
Protect the system. Don't kink, cut or crush the lines or their insulation, and leave the condensate drain line open. Damage means an HVAC call for the customer.
03 · Deodorization

For the smell the rodents leave behind, once they're gone

What it is

An enzyme-based product applied per its label to visible urine and feces. It reduces the odor that comes with it. Offered on Basic and Plus.

When

Note the odor at the inspection. Present it at the final follow-up, when the customer can see the activity has stopped. While rodents are active, it's wasted work.

What you report

Location, size and how hard it is to reach, with photos. The advisor prices it from what you report.

What it isn't

Not a full attic sanitation, not insulation removal, and it can't reach inside a closed wall. Never promise the smell will be "gone." Say it reduces odor.

Disinfect first, then deodorize. Droppings and urine get wetted with disinfectant and picked up the CDC way (Part three) before the enzyme product goes down. Never mix bleach with another product.
Follow both labels: the disinfectant's contact time and the deodorizer's directions, PPE and re-entry. Use only the product your supervisor issues.

01 · Scope

Handle it, report it, or stop

Within the service
  • Collecting rodents from traps at every follow-up
  • Minor dropping cleanup in a small, easy-to-reach area, about 2 ft by 2 ft or less
  • Small "while I'm here" repairs, noted in the service notes
  • The three add-ons, when the customer agrees
Not offered: document and escalate
  • Attic sanitation or decontamination beyond a small area
  • Insulation removal and replacement
  • Opening walls or ceilings to reach a dead rodent
  • Structural or roof repair: rot past about 4 to 6 inches, bad decking
  • Other wildlife: a separate service
Stop work: don't enter or continue
  • Mold covering large areas of sheathing or framing
  • Suspected asbestos: crumbling gray pipe wrap or board, older homes
  • Gas or sewer odor, unknown odor, poor air
  • Extreme heat, unsafe footing or access
  • PPE not right for what you see
Escalate means: photos, a clear customer note, technotes@clementspestcontrol.com for anything the office must act on, and a same-day call to your branch manager. Never quote or promise the work.
02 · Insulation: the right order

New insulation over an open entry point gets ruined again

1 · Remove

Trapping takes out the rodents already inside. Follow-ups confirm activity has stopped.

2 · Exclude

Entry points sealed with approved materials. Nothing new gets in.

3 · Clean and deodorize

Small cleanup in scope; deodorization if it fits. Larger contamination escalated.

4 · Insulation

Only after 1 to 3. Sales decides how it's handled and priced, by us or a contractor.

Photograph: depth and coverage, compressed or matted areas, urine staining, droppings, nesting and gaps. Then the service advisor can talk to the customer from real evidence.
What you can say: "Your insulation is matted and stained over the garage. I've photographed it, and I'll have the branch manager follow up with your options." No prices, no energy-savings promises.

Part three

Safe cleanup

Why it matters: the dust in a rodent attic is dried urine and droppings. Sweep it or vacuum it and you breathe it. The CDC method takes a few extra minutes and keeps it out of your lungs, and out of the customer's home.

  3. 03The CDC cleanup method
  4. 04Practice: put it in order

01 · What you're exposed to

Treat every dropping, nest and carcass as contaminated

Leptospirosis

Bacteria in rat urine. Gets in through cuts, eyes, nose and mouth, or contaminated water and soil. Gloves on, hands off your face.

Salmonella

Spread by droppings on food and surfaces. Never carry debris through the kitchen, and wash before you eat or drink.

Rat-bite fever

From bites, scratches, or handling rats and their carcasses without gloves. Any bite or scratch is reported the same day.

Hantavirus

Breathed in from stirred-up dried droppings and urine. In Florida, cases are linked to cotton rats and rice rats, not roof rats, Norway rats or house mice.

Why the same rules apply anyway: you rarely know every animal that has used an attic or crawlspace, and the other diseases come from the household rodents themselves. Every job gets the same PPE and the same wet cleanup.

Sources: CDC, How to Clean Up After Rodents; CDC, Hantavirus; Florida Department of Health, Hantavirus.

03 · The CDC cleanup method

Wet it, wait, wipe it up. Never sweep or vacuum it dry.

Droppings and urine
  1. 1Ventilate the space where you can. The CDC opens rooms for 30 minutes and stays out while it airs.
  2. 2Gloves, eye protection and respirator on. Seal check.
  3. 3Spray until very wet with 1 part bleach to 9 parts water (about 1½ cups per gallon) or an EPA-registered disinfectant. Mix bleach fresh.
  4. 4Let it soak 5 minutes, or the label's contact time.
  5. 5Wipe up with paper towels, bag them, then disinfect the surface.
Dead rodents, nests and used traps
  1. 1Spray the rodent, nest and the area around it with disinfectant. Wait 5 minutes.
  2. 2Into a plastic bag with any disposable trap, tied shut, then into a second bag, tied.
  3. 3Bagged before it goes through the living area, then into a covered trash can.
  4. 4Wash gloved hands, take the gloves off, then wash with soap and warm water.
Brooms and vacuums put the dust in the air. Neither one touches droppings, urine or nesting.
04 · Practice · cleanup order

A dead roof rat in a snap trap, with droppings around it on the attic plywood

Select the steps in the order you'd do them.

Emergency · bite or scratch

Wash it now. Report it today.

  1. 1Wash the wound right away with soap and water. It's the single most effective step.
  2. 2Apply an antiseptic if you have one.
  3. 3Tell your supervisor immediately. Written report the same day.
  4. 4Get a medical evaluation. Rodent bites can transmit rat-bite fever.
After a heavy exposure

Know the warning signs

Fever, muscle aches, fatigue or chills in the weeks after working in a contaminated space (the CDC puts hantavirus onset at 1 to 8 weeks): see a doctor and tell them about the rodent exposure. Shortness of breath: get care right away.

Exposures and spills follow the same rule: supervisor immediately, written report the same day (CW-09). A spilled disinfectant or deodorizer gets the 3 Cs: Control, Contain, Clean up (FC-05).
Attic heat is an exposure too. Short entries, water before and during, and get out at the first sign of dizziness or nausea (RX-08).

01 · Show, explain, recommend, route

Four steps, every time

Show

The photo or the spot itself: the open chase, the stained plywood, the palm over the roof.

Explain

What it means in plain words. "Rats follow these lines in." No jargon, no scare talk.

Recommend

The one add-on that fits what you showed them, and what it does and doesn't do.

Route the price

"Your service advisor or branch manager will give you the price." You never quote.

It's their decision. "No" is a fine answer. Thank them, note it, and move on.
Never promise results: no "guaranteed," "100%," or "smell gone for good."
Basic has no warranty, and an add-on doesn't create one. Warranty talk is RX-06.

03 · Documenting add-ons

Offered, accepted, declined or escalated: it goes on the record

PestPac Mobile notes print for the customer (FC-10). Write what you found, what you did and what you recommended, in plain words.
Photos to PestPac: the chase before and after, odor areas, box locations, anything you escalate.
Pricing questions, accepted add-ons to schedule, escalations go to technotes@clementspestcontrol.com and your branch manager. Not in the customer note.
Declined items are recorded too, and courtesy repairs go in the service notes even when there's no charge.
