Why this course matters

The customer buys a year, not a visit.

A lawn program only works when the right treatment lands in the right month. Every visit sets up the next one, and the customer judges all of it by what they see out the window.

Summer fertilizer rules are law

Every county and city where our branches work has its own fertilizer ordinance. Nitrogen or phosphorus on a lawn in the wrong month can mean a fine for the company and a mark against the applicator's credential.

Wrong season, wasted visit

A weed spray in the wrong heat, a fungicide on chinch bug damage, a feeding that pushes tender growth before a cold snap: each one costs product, a callback and the customer's trust.

A customer who understands stays

"Why didn't you fertilize in July?" is a cancellation waiting to happen, or a chance to show we know Florida lawns. You decide which.

03 · Full program or a la carte

Most lawn customers are on the full program. Not all.

Full program

All fourteen treatments on the calendar, recurring year after year. Each visit is planned around the one before it: sprays every other month, weed control in three seasons, feedings in spring, summer and fall.

A la carte

Some customers choose individual treatments, a different frequency, or both: sprays only, for example. Accounts that joined Clements from other companies may also carry older frequencies such as quarterly.

The service order is the plan. Check what this account bought before you mix or load the spreader.
See a problem the customer didn't buy for? Note it on the inspection report and flag it. LAWN-03 covers when to go beyond the order.
Adding or dropping treatments is an office and branch manager change. Never quote a price or change a schedule yourself.

03 · Weed control timing

Three weed visits, three different weed crops

April · spring

Catch summer weeds young

Summer annuals germinate in spring. Young weeds are far easier to kill than ones that have gone to seed, and the turf is waking up to fill the gaps.

July · summer

Peak heat, peak pressure

Doveweed, sedges and dollarweed thrive in hot, wet weather. Use only products labeled for summer heat on that turf, and keep off St. Augustine at midday.

December · winter

Winter weeds, cool-season products

Winter annuals sprout in fall and grow through winter. Some broadleaf products are only usable in cool weather, so this is the window.

Turf only. Never spray herbicide on shrubs or plants. Spot or blanket is a judgment call (LAWN-06).
Systemic products take 7–14 days for a full kill. Tell the customer before they ask.
The label rules: turf type, temperature limits, rainfast time and mowing interval all change by product.
04 · Granular feeding timing

Feed the turf when it can use it

APRTurf and shrubs. Turf is greening up and actively growing; shrubs push new growth in early spring. UF/IFAS: first lawn feeding in early April in central Florida; south Florida may feed year-round.
JULTurf only, inside the summer rules. In every blackout jurisdiction this feeding carries no nitrogen and no phosphorus. Part three covers it.
OCTTurf and shrubs. The blackout ends September 30. This is the last feeding before the cool season: nitrogen after mid-October pushes tender growth that cold snaps damage, and potassium helps turf handle the cold.

Why granular, why slow-release

Slow-release granules feed through the soil and roots over several months, so less washes away with a storm. Several of our counties require a share of the nitrogen to be slow-release.

Read the grade: N-P-K. A bag that starts 0-0- carries no nitrogen and no phosphorus. LAWN-05 covers grades and county rates.
Phosphorus on shrubs, too, follows county rules: every jurisdiction on our table requires a soil test showing a deficiency first (LAWN-05).

02 · What changes in summer

Four visits fall inside the blackout. Here is what happens to each.

JUL
Liquid spray · pest

Goes ahead. The tank carries insecticide (and fungicide if diagnosed), but no liquid fertilizer with nitrogen or phosphorus.

JUL
Weed control

Goes ahead. Herbicide isn't fertilizer, but check the label: some combination products contain fertilizer, and those count.

JUL
Granular turf feeding

Changes. In a blackout jurisdiction, only a product with no nitrogen and no phosphorus that your branch manager has approved for that county. Never the spring or fall blend.

SEP
Liquid spray · pest

Goes ahead, still inside the blackout until September 30. Same rule as July: nothing with N or P in the tank.

Settled for this course: the "summer" turf feeding on our calendar (and in CW-05) is real, but in every blackout jurisdiction it is a no-N, no-P feeding. Normal nitrogen feedings resume October 1.

02 · What to tell customers about the calendar

Plain words, real names, honest timing

"When are you coming next, and for what?"

Name the next visit and its month: "Next is your fall feeding in October, then a fungus spray in November."

"Why no fertilizer this summer?"

"The county doesn't allow nitrogen or phosphorus from June through September, to protect local waters. Your summer feeding has neither. Full feeding resumes in October."

"The weeds are still there."

"What we sprayed moves into the roots. You'll see yellowing in a few days and the full kill in one to two weeks. I'll check it first thing next visit."

"Why spray for fungus in winter?"

"Brown patch shows up in cool, damp weather, usually November through March. That's why your November and January sprays target it."

Never quote a price, promise a result, or call a product "safe."
Keep people and pets off treated areas until dry, or as the label says.
Not home? Door hanger and notes say what was done and what comes next.
