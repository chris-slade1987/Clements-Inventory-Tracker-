Why this course matters

A termite contract is a promise in writing, renewed every year.

Homeowner's insurance generally does not cover termite damage. For many customers, our agreement is the only protection they have, and they keep paying for it year after year on trust.

Florida regulates the words

Chapter 482 and Rule 5E-14.105 control what a termite contract must say, how fast we respond to a claim, and what reports we leave. FDACS can fine or discipline a license for violations.

A missed renewal can end coverage

If monitoring lapses past the grace period, the Corteva bond and our retreatment warranty end, and coverage never reaches back to cover the gap.

What you say can become an obligation

An offhand "you're fully covered" can turn into a dispute the company has to answer for. The contract is the promise. Point to it.

02 · Corteva's bond vs our warranty

Sentricon customers have two protections from two different companies

Corteva $1,000,000 damage bond

  • Whose: Corteva's, the maker of Sentricon. Not a Clements warranty.
  • Covers: new subterranean termite damage after installation.
  • Never: damage that existed before install.
  • Requires: annual monitoring current (a lapse past the 30-day grace period voids it), and Clements in good standing as a Certified Sentricon Specialist.
  • Claims: the branch manager files with Corteva. Clements does not decide them.
+

Clements retreatment warranty

  • Whose: ours, written into the Sentricon agreement.
  • Covers: retreatment of any new subterranean activity at no charge, for the contract term.
  • Never: repair of damage. That's the bond's job, through Corteva.
  • Requires: monitoring fees current.
  • Ends: with the bond, if the account lapses past the 30-day grace period.

Corteva's terms control the bond. Describe it in the approved wording (pending Clements confirmation) and point to the customer's documents.

04 · What the contract must say

Two sets of Florida rules shape every termite agreement

Fla. Stat. 482.227 · warranty wording
  1. 1Page one, in bold: repair and retreatment, retreatment only, or no warranty or guarantee.
  2. 2Page one, in bold: whether any disclaimers, limitations, conditions or exclusions apply. Those sections get bold headings.
  3. 3No "full" or "unlimited" with "warranty" or "guarantee" if any limitation exists.
Rule 5E-14.105 · what the contract contains
  • Owner, property address and every structure covered
  • Licensee name and business address
  • Contract date, term and renewal option
  • Termites covered: subterranean, drywood or both (page one); Formosan named if excluded
  • Control of a known infestation, or prevention
  • Whether reinspections are made, how often, and any fees
  • Conditions for retreatment, and for repairs if any
  • Maximum treatment and repair prices; exact renewal fee, if any
  • Spot jobs: every treated location listed
  • Signatures of both parties
Before anyone signs: the customer signs the FDACS Consumer Notice (FDACS-13692) and gets the contract before any work or payment. A second contract on the same structure needs the Consumer Consent Form (FDACS-13671).

02 · When a renewal lapses

A lapse ends coverage, and coverage never reaches back

Past the 30-day grace period, the Corteva bond and our retreatment warranty are void. Paying later restores protection only going forward. Damage that starts during the gap is not covered.

Be direct, and kind. If a lapsed customer asks about the bond, tell them plainly that it ended at the lapse and doesn't reinstate for the past. Then explain that the branch manager can set up reactivation to protect the home from here on.
What you do
  1. 1Check the status first. Renewal date and payment status are in PestPac. Never guess whether an account is current.
  2. 2Don't decide reinstatement. Reactivation terms, fees and whether stations are reinspected are the branch manager's call.
  3. 3No prices. Not the renewal, not a reactivation. The office or the branch manager quotes from the account.
  4. 4Note it for the office. Customer wants to renew or reactivate? Send it to technotes@clementspestcontrol.com the same day.
03 · Home sales and other renewals

Renewal rules differ by service

The home is sold

Sentricon's bond and contract can transfer to the new owner, who keeps the existing contract pricing. The transfer must be started within 30 days of the sale. Spot warranties never transfer.

Drywood Whole-Home

Renews annually; every renewal includes a reinspection and any needed retreatment of the covered structure. Renewal pricing, transfer and the late-renewal rule are still being finalized: ask the branch manager.

New construction

Florida requires at least one year from the treatment date on the contract, issued within 30 days of treatment, and the owner can extend it annually for at least four more years. For Sentricon jobs, that's the monitoring renewal.

Every renewal date lives in PestPac, set at install or treatment. Routing & Logistics (Jessica Sanderson) owns PestPac and Sentricon reporting. If a date looks wrong, flag it through technotes; don't fix it on the device.

01 · What the ATI is, and who does it

The inspection the contract promises

"ATI" is shorthand for the annual termite inspection on a termite agreement. On Sentricon accounts it comes on top of quarterly station monitoring, and it doubles as the inspection the Corteva label requires at least once a year.

Cadence: Sentricon, quarterly monitoring plus the annual inspection. Active accounts every 30 to 60 days until two clean visits in a row. Whole-Home Preventative, annually. Whole-Home Corrective, at 60 days, then annually.

A WDO ID card, or WDO certification

A contract inspection or retreatment may only be done by someone holding the FDACS WDO employee ID card, or a certified operator in the WDO category (482.226(3)(b), 482.091(9)).

Sentricon: a Certified Sentricon Specialist

Or a technician working under one. Corteva's bond depends on Clements keeping that certification in good standing.

Not a GHP add-on

On a GHP stop, you never inspect, diagnose or quote termite work. Photos and a same-day call to the branch manager.

    04 · Report and document

    If it isn't documented, we can't defend the warranty

    Photos and station logs are the foundation of bond defense and of every warranty decision. The branch manager audits termite records, and an incomplete file is corrected before it closes.

    Florida requires a signed report after every contract inspection or treatment: whether the covered termites are present or absent, whether treatment was made, and the common or brand name of any pesticide used. (482.226(3)). We keep a copy at least three years (Rule 5E-14.105(6)).
    Every ATI record includes
    • Who, where, when: date, address, technician, customer contact
    • Findings: locations, type and age of evidence, species if known
    • Station log: each station's bait status, integrity, obstructions
    • Product details if you treated: brand name, EPA reg. no., amount, locations
    • Photos: stations, evidence, conducive conditions, same angles as baseline
    • Limits: inaccessible areas and why, pre-existing conditions
    • Treatment notice: still posted at the attic or crawl access. Only the owner may remove it.
    • Next steps: follow-up, renewal and corrective-action dates in PestPac

    PestPac Mobile notes print on the customer's report. Anything the office must act on goes to technotes.

    05 · Conducive conditions, in writing

    An exclusion only holds up if we gave written notice

    Florida bars us from using a contract exclusion to deny retreatment or repair unless the condition primarily caused the problem and, if we knew about it, we told the owner in writing within 60 days of discovery and gave them 60 days to fix it.

    Use the talking points, don't lecture. "Here are the two things that will help your protection most..." Then put them in writing on the report.

    Moisture

    Gutters, downspouts, irrigation overspray, A/C condensate.

    Wood-to-ground

    Siding, trim, deck supports, fence posts, landscape timbers.

    Plants and mulch

    Shrubs against the wall; mulch piled to the foundation.

    Stored wood

    Firewood or lumber against the structure or on the ground.

    Blocked stations

    Pavers, planters or new landscaping over a station.

    Lost access

    Attic or crawl access blocked or sealed off.

    01 · Words to use and avoid

    Describe the contract. Don't improve on it.

    Say

    • "Your agreement covers retreatment of new activity. Let me show you where it says that."
    • "The damage bond is Corteva's. It covers new subterranean termite damage after installation while annual monitoring stays current."
    • "I'll document this with photos and your branch manager will call you today."
    • "Keeping your renewal current is what keeps the bond and the warranty in force."

    Never say

    • "You're fully covered" or "unlimited warranty." Banned when any limitation exists.
    • "Clements will pay for the damage." We don't repair; the bond is Corteva's and they decide claims.
    • "Don't worry, that's covered" or "that's not covered," before the branch manager reviews it.
    • Any price, renewal fee or reactivation cost. And never "you'll never see termites again."

    No employee can expand coverage by what they say or write. The written agreement controls.

    03 · Escalating a claim

    Every claim goes to the branch manager, the same day

    1. 1Document before anything else: photos of activity and damage, locations, station numbers, the date the customer first reported it.
    2. 2Don't disturb the evidence before it's photographed, and don't promise an outcome.
