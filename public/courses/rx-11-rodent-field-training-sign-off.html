Why this course matters

A patch that fails fails the warranty.

Rats test every repair, every night. Foam in the wrong place, a cloth patch with no overlap or a missed roofline gap shows up as a callback within months, and on a Plus account it lands on the warranty.

Customers are told you're certified

The Rodent Shield certification is a Clements standard, above state licensing, that sales and customer materials can point to. It has to mean something on every job.

Your photos are the warranty record

The entry point PDF and PestPac photos from the first visit define what's covered. Declined points, courtesy repairs and limits all have to be on file.

Attics, roofs and biohazards

Rodent jobs put you on ladders, in attics well over 120°F and around droppings and urine that carry disease. The safe way is the only way you'll be signed off.

01 · What Florida requires

Exclusion is pest control, even with no pesticide

Statute · Fla. Stat. 482.021

What counts as pest control

  • Any method or device used to prevent or control a pest in, on or under a structure: sealing entry points is a method
  • Any pesticide or mechanical device used against rodents: traps and bait stations
  • Inspecting for an infestation is pest control too
  • Structural rodent work falls in the general household pest (GHP) category; rodenticide baiting always needs it
Statute 482.091(3) · Rule 5E-14.1421

Field training before solo work

  • At least 5 days of field training in the category, under a certified operator, before you inspect or apply alone
  • 40 hours, recorded on FDACS-13665 and kept 2 years. For GHP, that's PC-FLD
  • A separate state limited wildlife certificate (482.157) allows nonchemical rodent control only. You work under our GHP license instead

02 · The exclusion rules

What your lead checks on every sealed opening

Cement first, color-matched

Cement is the primary material for every opening 1/2 inch and larger. Foam is a courtesy fix under 1/2 inch only: not billed, not warranted, never primary.

Overlap and fasten

1/4 inch galvanized hardware cloth overlaps at least 1 inch on every side; a sheet metal or wood patch at least 1.5 inches. Screws or staples, never adhesive. Edges sealed with cement.

Vents breathe

Never cover or screen a dryer vent, bath or kitchen exhaust, HVAC exhaust or attic ventilation: fire, carbon monoxide and moisture risk. Gable vents get hardware cloth, never solid sealant.

Document the scope

Plus includes a set number of entry points; extra points are priced by the service advisor, never by you. Declined points go on the agreement with the homeowner's acknowledgment. Courtesy repairs go in the notes.

1/2 inch is the line

Rats need 1/2 inch, mice 1/4 inch. Every opening 1/2 inch and up is documented and drives the exclusion scope. Measure it; don't eyeball it.

Know where it stops

Roof rot past about 4–6 inches, soft decking, structural or remodel-level repair, and large insulation jobs are referred out. Stop, photograph, escalate the same day (RX-09).

03 · Traps and follow-up visits

Place for the rodent, then prove the work holds

Placement

On the path, on the edge

  • Gloves on: human scent causes avoidance
  • On the wall, trigger end toward it
  • Attic first where possible, ideally above the garage
  • Out of reach of kids and pets; exterior stations tamper-resistant, anchored, baited per label
Patience

Neophobia sets the timing

  • Norway rats avoid new objects 5–10 days: pre-bait unset, set next visit
  • Mice usually accept a new trap within 24–48 hours
  • Don't move devices or switch baits without a reason
  • Disturbed but no catch: change the trap type or bait there
Follow-up

Every visit, every trap

  • Every other day in week one; five follow-ups in Basic and Plus
  • Check every trap, bag and remove carcasses, reset in active areas
  • Recheck every sealed point for new gnawing or a reopened gap
  • Tell the customer what's next; document it
Plus warranty: 1-year or 3-year, chosen at the original service (3-year can't be added later), 30-day grace period, transferable within 30 days of a sale. Bait stations required to renew or transfer.
Never quote prices or promise "they'll never come back." Warranty, renewal and pricing questions go to the branch manager or service advisor (RX-06).

05 · Staying safe on rodent jobs

Your lead stops the job for any of these

Droppings and nests

N95 or P100 and gloves before attic entry, fit test current (FC-03). Never dry-sweep or vacuum droppings. Wet them with 1 part bleach to 9 parts water (about 1½ cups per gallon) or an EPA-registered disinfectant, soak, then bag and seal.

Bites and disease

Leptospirosis, rat-bite fever and salmonella are real risks. In Florida, hantavirus is linked to cotton rats and rice rats, not our three house rodents, but every dropping is a biohazard. Bite or scratch: wash, report the same day, see a doctor.

Attic heat

Summer attics can pass 130°F. Exclusion in the morning, water about every 15 to 20 minutes, a check-in when you're up there, and you stop at the first cramp, dizziness or nausea.

Ladders and roofs (FC-06): 4-to-1, secured, 3 ft above the edge, 10 ft from power lines, three points of contact. No tile or metal roofs; wet, soft or steep roofs stop the job; fall protection near an edge at 4 ft or more.
Stop-work authority is yours. Heat, bad air, rot, unsafe access: stop, photograph, explain it to the customer and tell your supervisor the same day. Injuries get a same-day written report.

01 · How field hours are logged

From the job site to your training file

1

Log real times

Actual start and end times for each session with your lead. Subtract lunch and breaks.

2

Tag the phase

Every hour belongs to Phase 1, 2 or 3. The sign-off sheet asks for each phase total.

3

Both sign, same day

You and your lead sign the day's record. The hours go in the CanopyOS field-hour log.

4

Sign-off sheet

Dates, total and phase hours, assessment result and the lead's signature go to management with your RX-10 result.

The plan: Phase 1 about 10 hours, Phase 2 about 12, Phase 3 about 8. The certification program states 20–30 field hours in total; operations confirms your target.
Minimums, not targets. Not ready at the end of a phase? The phase is extended. Certification is never awarded on hours alone.
Rodent Shield hours are their own record. Your state GHP hours (PC-FLD) are logged separately on FDACS-13665.
