Why this check matters

The certificate is a promise to the customer.

Clements tells customers that its Rodent Shield technicians have been through rigorous, structured training beyond state licensing. This check is where that claim is proven, before you ever work a job alone.

Warranties stand on your knowledge

A Plus warranty is only as good as the inspection, the materials and the documentation behind it. A wrong material or a missing photo turns into a failed seal and a claim we can't defend.

Rodent work carries real hazards

Attics above 130°F, ladders and roofs, droppings that can carry disease, and bites. OSHA rules apply to all of it, and so does your stop-work authority.

Customers can tell who knows

A technician who names the species, points to the rub mark and explains the plan earns the job and the renewal. One who guesses loses both.

Recap · RX-01 · Rodent biology & behavior

Climber, digger, squeezer

Thigmotaxis

All three run walls and edges. Devices go on the edge, on the path.

Smell

Gloves on new devices. No repellents on the path. Don't switch baits mid-program.

Reproduction

Mice breed at 6 weeks, about 3-week gestation. "Five mice today, fifty by summer."

Florida seasons

Fall push-in (Sep–Nov): cooler nights, thinning food, attic activity spikes. Usually roof rats.

Recap · RX-02 · Inspection & entry point assessment

Seven steps, every property, in order

  1. 1Greet the customer. Their account of what they hear and see is your first evidence.
  2. 2Exterior perimeter. Foundation gaps, burrows, rub marks, droppings, gnawed sweeps.
  3. 3Vegetation and landscape. Palms and limbs on the roof, hedges, fruit, feeders, pet food.
  4. 4Roofline from the ground. Gable vents, soffit returns, fascia, ridge, pipe stacks.
  5. 5Garage and outbuildings. Door sweep corners, stored boxes and seed.
  6. 6Interior, kitchen first. Behind appliances, cabinet bases, plumbing penetrations.
  7. 7Attic or crawlspace. Respirator and gloves first. Trails, nests, entry from inside.

Gap sizes

Mouse ¼ in (a pencil). Rats ½ in. Plus documents every entry point ½ in or larger as the warranty record. Note smaller gaps for the customer record.

Active or old?

Active: pale fresh gnaws, dark tacky rub marks, fresh droppings, no cobwebs. Old: weathered, dusty, cobwebbed. Both go on the list.

Activity level

Light, moderate or heavy drives the plan. Heavy activity with gnawed wiring: escalate to your manager.

Keep the list internal

Talk about findings in general terms. The location-by-location list becomes the scope once a Plus agreement is signed.

Recap · RX-03 · Traps & devices

The right device, on the runway

Snap traps

Fast, clear evidence. Day one for mice; for rats, only after pre-baiting. Perpendicular to the wall, trigger at the edge.

Bait stations

Locked, tamper-resistant, anchored (the Clements standard). Required by rodenticide labels wherever children, pets or non-targets could reach.

Multi-catch

Mice only; rats don't fit. No bait. Check often so animals don't die inside.

Glue boards

Monitoring or tight spots only, never the primary kill tool, never where a child, pet or wildlife can reach.

Placement: on the path, not near it. Attic over the garage when possible. Hidden but reachable indoors. Kids and pets first.
Cadence: traps checked every other day in week one, five follow-up visits in both tiers. Don't move a device without a reason.
Bait shyness: a near miss teaches avoidance. Disturbed but no catch? Change the trap type or bait at that spot.

Recap · RX-04 · Exclusion, the Clements standard

Measure the opening, then pick the material

< ½ in½–1 in1–4 in4–8 in> 8 inVent
Cement first. Color-matched cement is the primary material for every entry point ½ in and larger.
Foam is not exclusion. Rodents chew through it. Courtesy use under ½ in only; not billed, not warranted.
Stacks yes, vents no. Never seal dryer vents, exhaust vents or attic ventilation: fire and moisture hazard. Gable vents get hardware cloth, not solid sealant.
Seal before you bait outside, and note any "while I'm here" courtesy repair in the service notes, even at no charge.
Recap · RX-05 · Add-on services

Three approved add-ons, and what we don't do

Deodorization

Enzyme-based treatment for urine, droppings and odor. Offered on Basic and Plus. Best presented at the final follow-up, when the customer can see the problem is solved.

A/C chase sealing

Where refrigerant lines enter the wall: a common Florida entry. Inside the Plus package scope; sold as a standalone add-on outside it.

Exterior rodent boxes

Always offered. Required at every Plus warranty renewal and on a warranty transfer. Refilled bi-monthly or quarterly; quantity set at inspection.

No sanitation add-on. Removed from the program for liability and time. Small, easy droppings cleanup only.
Insulation removal: refer to an insulation contractor. We don't pull and replace attic insulation.
Never quote. The service advisor prices add-ons. Price questions go to sales or your branch manager.

Recap · RX-08 · Rodent field safety

Every step, every time

PPE by task

  • Handling droppings or dead rodents: nitrile gloves
  • Attic entry on a rodent account: N95 or P100 plus gloves
  • Nest removal: P100, gloves, disposable coveralls on heavy jobs
  • Respirator: medical evaluation, annual fit test, seal check every use (FC-03)

Droppings and disease

  • Never sweep or vacuum dry. Wet with fresh bleach solution (1 part bleach to 9 parts water, about 1½ cups per gallon) or an EPA-registered disinfectant, soak 5 minutes (CDC), then remove
  • Bag and seal before crossing living areas
  • Leptospirosis, salmonella, rat-bite fever
  • Florida hantavirus is tied to cotton rats and rice rats, not our three household rodents

Heat, bites, heights

  • Lightheaded, nauseous, soaked: stop, get out, cool down. Confusion or hot dry skin: call 911
  • Water about every 15 to 20 minutes; exclusion work in the morning in summer
  • Bite or scratch: wash with soap and water, tell your supervisor immediately, written report the same day, get it checked
  • Ladders: 4-to-1, three points of contact (FC-06)
Attic stop-work: extreme heat, an unknown odor, poor air or a structural concern means you get out and call your supervisor. Summer attic work goes with a check-in, never alone in extreme heat.
Recap · RX-09 · Limits & escalation

Know where the job ends

You can
  • Inspect, identify, document
  • Place traps and stations
  • Seal with approved materials
  • Small droppings cleanup
  • Courtesy repairs in scope
Refer out
  • Remodel-level or structural repair
  • Roof rot beyond about 4–6 in
  • Compromised roof decking
  • Major insulation replacement
  • Anything needing a roofing or contractor license
Stops the job
  • Unsafe access or slippery roof
  • Excessive attic heat
  • Poor ventilation or air quality
  • Customer interference that creates risk
  • Unexpected live electrical
Escalate in 5 steps
  1. Stop safely
  2. Photograph and note why
  3. Explain it to the customer as protecting the work
  4. Tell your branch manager the same day
  5. Log it with technotes; plan the return or referral
Stop-work authority is yours. No exclusion job is worth an injury, and sealing through rot is a warranty failure waiting to happen.
