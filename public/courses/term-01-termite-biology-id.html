Why this course matters

Wrong termite, wrong service. And it's in writing.

A home is often the biggest thing a customer owns, and homeowner's insurance usually does not cover termite damage.

Sentricon never treats drywood termites, and a drywood spot treatment does nothing for a subterranean colony in the soil. The ID picks the service.

The law writes the ID into the contract

Every termite contract must say on page one whether it covers subterranean, drywood or both, and must name Formosan termites if they are excluded (Rule 5E-14.105). Telling a customer they have a pest without strong evidence is a prohibited act (5E-14.104).

Damage happens out of sight

Termites work inside wood and behind walls. A large Formosan colony can do serious structural damage in months. Early, accurate ID is what protects the structure.

Trust is won or lost on the first answer

A customer holding a jar of winged insects wants a straight answer. "Those are ant swarmers" or "this needs our termite team today" is what they remember.

03 · Three ways to live

Where the colony gets its water decides almost everything

Subterranean

Colony in the soil

Needs soil moisture. Travels to wood through soil and mud tubes. Large colonies. Includes Formosan and Asian subterranean.

Drywood

Colony inside the wood

Gets its water from the wood itself. No soil contact. Pushes six-sided pellets out of kick-out holes. Small colonies, often several per home.

Dampwood

Colony in wet wood

Needs wood with very high moisture: leaks, rot, stumps, palms. No soil foraging. Fix the water and you change the problem.

The treatment follows the water. Subterranean colonies are reached through the soil (Sentricon or a liquid treatment). Drywood colonies are treated in the wood. Dampwood starts with fixing the moisture. Part four explains each.

03 · Damage patterns

Break open the wood and the damage tells you who did it

Native subterranean

Eats the soft spring wood along the grain and leaves the harder rings. Galleries look layered, with soil and mud plastered inside. Wood may sound hollow or look blistered.

Formosan and Asian

Eat both soft and hard wood, hollowing timbers out. Voids get filled with carton: a brown, layered mix of chewed wood, soil, saliva and droppings. Many soldiers.

Drywood

Smooth, clean chambers cut across the grain, connected by tunnels. No soil. Loose pellets inside. Thin surface layers may blister or crack.

Dampwood

Large, wet galleries in soft, rotting wood near a water source. Pellets clump. Usually with decay fungi, a leak or wood touching soil.

Probe, don't demolish. On a termite inspection, sound and probe suspect wood with a screwdriver or probe. On a service visit, note and photograph what you see and leave the investigation to the termite team.

01 · Why ID changes treatment

One question first: where does this colony live?

Subterranean · incl. Formosan and Asian

Reach the colony through the soil

  • Sentricon Always Active is our default for existing homes and new construction. It eliminates the colony; stations are checked quarterly plus an annual inspection (TERM-02).
  • Full liquid treatment where activity is confirmed and a full liquid strategy is approved. Never a partial job (TERM-03).
Drywood

Treat the wood where they live

  • Spot treatment for one area: foam injected into galleries (TERM-04).
  • Whole-home borate treatment of accessible wood, preventative or corrective (TERM-04).
  • No tent fumigation. Clements does not perform, sell or refer it. Widespread or inaccessible drywood is documented and explained honestly.
Dampwood · darkwing · Cuban subterranean

Uncommon: send it up

  • Dampwood starts with fixing the moisture source and removing wet, decayed wood.
  • For these less common species, the branch certified operator decides the approach. Photograph, collect and escalate.
Sentricon never treats drywood. A drywood spot treatment never fixes subterranean. Selling the wrong one fails the customer and breaks the contract terms the law requires.
Who decides: termite services are sold and performed by certified, credentialed staff. If you're not on the termite team, you identify and escalate. You never diagnose, quote or promise a termite treatment.
02 · Other wood-destroying organisms

Termites aren't the only thing that eats a house

Chapter 482 defines wood-destroying organisms as termites, powderpost beetles, old house borers and wood-decaying fungi. A real estate WDO inspection reports all of them on state form FDACS-13645 (TERM-06).

Powderpost beetles

Signs: tiny round exit holes, about 1/32–1/8 in, and fine powdery frass that sifts out. Common in hardwood flooring, furniture and trim.

Lookalike: drywood pellets are hard and six-sided; beetle frass feels like flour or fine grit.

Old house borer

Signs: oval exit holes about 1/4–3/8 in in softwood framing; galleries with ripple marks, packed with powder and tiny pellets.

Note: uncommon in Florida, but it's on the form and you should recognize it.

Wood-decay fungi

Signs: soft, spongy, discolored wood; brown rot cracks into cubes and crumbles; white rot leaves wood stringy and pale. Always tied to moisture: leaks, condensation, wood touching soil.

Link: decay invites dampwood and subterranean termites.

WDO inspections and reports are done only by a certified operator in the termite and WDO category, or an employee holding the special WDO inspection ID card under one (482.091). WDO inspection policy and pricing are still being finalized.
03 · Specimens, escalation and the customer

Found something? Collect it, photograph it, send it up

1

Leave it intact

Don't break tubes, sweep pellets or spray. The evidence is what the termite team needs.

2

Photograph with scale

Close-up and wide shots, a coin or ruler beside it. Note the location and what you saw.

3

Collect a specimen

Soldiers or swarmers, ideally 10 or more, in a vial of rubbing alcohol (70%). Label date and address.

4

Branch manager, same day

Photos and notes to your branch manager the same day. Office follow-up to technotes@clementspestcontrol.com.

"I found what looks like a termite sign by your back door. I've photographed it and I'm sending it to our termite team today so a specialist can look at it properly. Please leave it as it is until they do."
Never say
  • "You have termites" before the termite team confirms it
  • A price, a treatment recommendation or a promise
  • "It's nothing," without identifying it
  • That any product is safe, harmless or non-toxic

PestPac Mobile notes print on the customer's report: record what you observed, not a diagnosis.
