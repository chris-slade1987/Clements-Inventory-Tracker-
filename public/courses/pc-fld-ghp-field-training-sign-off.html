Course objectives

By the end of field training you will be able to:

Explain what Florida law requires before you work alone: five days and 40 documented hours, in each category
Describe the five-day progression from ride-along observation to working solo with your trainer watching
Perform a complete GHP service to the Clements treatment checklist, interior and exterior

Know who signs each record, what you are certifying when you sign, and where the records go
Judge honestly whether you are ready to go solo, and what happens if you are not ready yet
Why this course matters

Nobody works alone until they're ready.

Reading about a treatment is not the same as doing one in a customer's kitchen. Field training is where you prove it, with someone experienced beside you.

It's the law

Florida Statute 482.091 bars you from performing, soliciting, inspecting or applying on your own until you finish at least 5 days of field training in that category. Rule 5E-14.1421 requires the 40 hours to be documented and kept for 2 years.

Our license is on the line

FDACS can inspect our training records. Missing or false records put the company license, our certified operator and your ID card at risk.

Customers trust the uniform

A customer can't tell a first-week tech from a ten-year tech. Every visit you run alone has to meet the same standard as theirs.

01 · What Florida requires

Two rules, two minimums, both must be met

Statute · Fla. Stat. 482.091(3)

At least 5 days of field training

  • Before you perform, solicit, inspect or apply on your own
  • Under the direct supervision, direction and control of a certified operator
  • In each category you'll work in. GHP days don't count toward Lawn, and Lawn days don't count toward GHP
Rule · FAC 5E-14.1421

40 hours, documented

  • The initial five-day training is 40 hours
  • Given by a certified operator, or a trainer the certified operator in charge designates in writing
  • Verified on form FDACS-13665 (Verification Record of Initial Employee Training) or an equivalent attendance record
  • Kept by Clements for at least 2 years
5days minimum. Four long days don't add up to five, even with 40 hours.
40hours minimum. Five short days don't add up to 40. Days are added until both are met.
30days from hire for Clements to apply for your FDACS ID card. HR starts it on Day 1.
02 · Practice

Does it count toward your 40 GHP hours?

Training time under your trainer's supervision counts. Breaks, paperwork and other categories don't. Clements makes the final call on edge cases, so ask if you're unsure.

03 · Who's who in your training

Five people have a part in your sign-off

You, the trainee

Show up ready, ask questions, practice every step, and sign an honest daily record.

Training technician

Rides with you, shows every step, fills out and signs the daily review. Designated in writing by the certified operator.

Certified operator in charge

Legally responsible for your training and supervision under Chapter 482. Verifies the 40 hours.

Branch manager & Director of Field Operations

Run the field rides and classroom reviews, and decide with your trainer when you're ready to go solo.

HR

Starts your FDACS ID card application, receives the signed daily forms and keeps the 40-hour training record on file.

Questions about your training plan go to your branch manager or Graham Foster, Director of Field Operations. Questions about forms, payroll or HR policy go to April Williford, Director of HR & Administrative Affairs.

01 · How hours are logged

From the truck to the file

1

Log start and end

Write the actual start and end time on the daily review. Subtract lunch and other breaks to get total hours trained.

2

Both sign the same day

You and your training technician sign and date the form at the end of that day, not later.

3

Forward to HR

Signed forms go to HR for your personnel file. Your hours are also entered in the CanopyOS field-hour log.

4

FDACS-13665

When you reach 5 days and 40 hours, the 40-hour verification record is completed and kept at least 2 years.

Record the real start and end times, not the scheduled ones. If the day ran short, the form should say so.
Write the service category (GHP) and the services you covered, so the hours count in the right category.
Keep your own running total. You should always know how many hours and days you have.

01 · What "ready" looks like

Six things your trainer must see you do without help

Work safely

Right PPE every time, product locked and labeled, ladder and vehicle safety, and you know the 3 Cs for a spill.

Mix and apply by the label

Mix from the current label and the approved mix rate sheet. Calibrated equipment. No guessing.

Inspect and identify

Find the conditions and the evidence, and name the common ants, roaches and rodents before you treat.

Treat to standard

Interior crack and crevice and baits, the exterior three-pass, initial services and cleanouts, to the checklist.

Handle the customer

Greet, listen, knock back with findings. Honest expectations, no promises, pricing to the branch manager.

Close it out

Customer-ready PestPac notes, office items to technotes@, door hanger when no one's home, order completed before you leave.

02 · If you're not ready yet

More training is a normal result, not a failure

Some techs need a sixth or seventh day on a skill. That's far better than a misapplication, a callback or a hurt customer.

Until you're signed off, you never run a stop alone. Not "just this once," not an easy exterior, not if your trainer calls in sick. Ask your branch manager for another trainer or other work.
What happens next
  1. 1Your trainer writes the gaps under Areas for improvement and Next steps on the daily review.
  2. 2Your branch manager and the Director of Field Operations agree on extra training days and what they'll focus on.
  3. 3Extra days are logged on the same form. Every hour counts in your record.
  4. 4Your supervisor re-observes only the items still open on the sign-off list.
  5. 5When everything is observed, you're signed off and your GHP certification can be awarded.
