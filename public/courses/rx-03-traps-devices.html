Traps & Devices

Snap traps, multi-catch traps, glue boards, stations and one-way doors: which device fits the rodent, where it goes, how often we check it, and how we keep children and pets safe around every one.

45–55 min5 checkpoints20-question check · 80% to pass
Course objectives

By the end of this course you will be able to:

Choose the right device for the species, the location and the household: snap, multi-catch, glue board, electronic, station or one-way door
Explain neophobia and run a pre-bait on a rat account instead of setting traps that rats will walk around
Set a snap trap safely and place it on the runway: against the wall, perpendicular, trigger end to the wall
Use exterior tamper-resistant bait stations the way the label and Clements require, and never bait before sealing
Run a Rodent Shield check visit: read every device, decide reset, move or switch, and record what you found
Remove and dispose of dead rodents safely, and keep every device out of reach of children, pets and non-target animals
Why this course matters

A trap in the wrong spot is a trap that never catches.

Rodent Shield Basic is a trapping program: an inspection, up to six traps or enclosed stations, and five follow-up visits. Plus puts traps at every entry point we seal. The devices are what the customer is paying for, and they sit in a home with children and pets.

Rodenticide is regulated

Federal law (FIFRA) makes any use against the label a violation. Rodenticide labels set where bait may go and when it must be in a tamper-resistant station.

Children and pets come first

A rat trap can break a small finger or a cat's paw. Bait in the open can poison a dog. Placement is a safety decision before it's a pest decision.

Customers judge the result

A rodent that dies inside a wall, a glue board found with a live mouse on it, or a trap the family wasn't told about ends the customer's trust fast.

02 · Placement rules and baits

On the edge, on the path, out of reach

On the runway

Against the wall, top plate, beam or pipe the rodent runs, at the rub marks and fresh droppings. Not near the path: on it.

In the attic when possible

Ideally above the garage access. Odor stays out of the living space and devices stay away from the family.

Hidden but reachable

Indoors: behind furniture, in cabinet voids, inside enclosed stations. Never in open traffic areas.

Leave it there

Don't move a device between visits without a reason. A device in place two weeks catches better than a fresh one.

Bait the trap
  • Pea-size, pressed onto the pedal so it must be worked at, not grabbed
  • Match what they're eating: nut spreads, fruit or seeds; soft nesting material like cotton works for mice
  • Don't switch baits mid-program at a spot that's taking bait
  • Gloves on to handle every trap. Human scent causes avoidance
  • No repellents or strong odors on the runway
Basic: up to six traps or enclosed stations in activity areas. Plus: targeted at or near each entry point. Quality of placement beats quantity.

01 · Exterior bait stations

Tamper-resistant, anchored, locked, recorded

The label decides

Where bait may go, how far from buildings, and when a tamper-resistant station is required (anywhere children, pets or non-target animals could reach it). Read it for every product.

EVO stations

Our tamper-resistant station. Bait secured on the rod, never loose. Anchoring every exterior station is the Clements standard (PC-01, PC-03).

Locked and keyed

Close, lock, tug-test. The key stays on you, never in the lock or on a ledge.

Mapped and recorded

Every station numbered, on the account, with activity recorded per station each service.

In Rodent Shield
  • Exterior rodent boxes are an add-on, always offered
  • Required at every Plus warranty renewal and on a warranty transfer at sale
  • Refilled bi-monthly or quarterly; quantity set at inspection
Rules are changing. EPA is still finalizing new rodenticide limits (carcass searches, station use, endangered species). They arrive on the label and in Bulletins Live! Two. The label in your hand wins.

03 · Glue boards

A monitoring tool, not the program

Clements guidance (Rodent Certification Training)
  • Use for monitoring, or as a supplement where a snap trap won't fit: under appliances, behind shelving
  • Never as the primary kill device in a rodent program
  • Never where a child, pet or non-target animal could touch it
  • Date, number and record every board; replace dusty or full ones
  • Follow the board's label for placement and how often to check it
Why the limit: a rodent on a glue board isn't killed. It can struggle for hours, and a customer may find it alive. That's a welfare problem and a trust problem.
Live rodent on a board: don't leave it and don't hand it to the customer. Remove it from view and euthanize it promptly and humanely, the way your supervisor showed you in field training. Then bag and dispose of it.
Lizard, bird, snake or pet caught? Stop and call your supervisor. Don't pull the animal off the glue. Then ask why a board was where a non-target could reach it.
04 · Multi-catch, electronic and one-way devices

Useful tools, each with a catch

Multi-catch mouse traps

Curiosity traps that catch several mice, no bait needed. Good for commercial accounts and no-kill requests. Mice only. A live-catch trap holds live animals: check it at least every 24 hours, so use it only where someone can.

Electronic traps

Battery-powered traps that kill quickly, often with an indicator showing a catch. Keep them dry, check the batteries each visit, and place them like a snap trap. Use only models Clements approves.

One-way doors and excluders

Let rodents out but not back in. Fit one only on the last active entry, after every other opening is sealed, with traps kept inside for any rodent that can't leave. Never on a dryer or exhaust vent.

The 24-hour rule (CW-05): Florida's wildlife rule (FWC 68A-9.010) requires live traps and snares to be checked at least every 24 hours. It covers live-capture wildlife trapping. It doesn't set the schedule for snap traps on Rodent Shield.
Bats are never trapped. Bats are excluded only under the wildlife rules in CW-05. If you find bats where you planned a one-way door, stop and call your branch manager.

01 · The Rodent Shield check schedule

Five follow-ups. The first week is the busy one.

Initial service

Inspection, devices placed and mapped. Tell the customer when you're coming back.

Next day, if possible

The first follow-up ideally happens the day after placement when the schedule allows.

Every other day, week one

The high-activity window: fresh placements, rodents testing new routes after sealing.

Remaining follow-ups

Per the program: five follow-ups in Basic and Plus. More visits are extra; pricing comes from the branch.

Every device, every visit

Check each one: catch, sprung, bait taken, moved, new sign nearby. Reset or replace, then record it.

What success looks like

Captures decline over time, noises drop off, no new sign, and the exclusion work is intact.

When it isn't working

Captures flat or climbing means a missed entry point. Re-inspect. On Basic, present Plus; on Plus, it's a warranty review (RX-06).

03 · Carcass removal and disposal

Every dead rodent is a biohazard

  1. 1PPE first: nitrile gloves always; a respirator in an attic or enclosed space with built-up droppings (FC-03, RX-08).
  2. 2Wet it down: spray the rodent, trap and nearby droppings with 1 part bleach to 9 parts water (about 1½ cups per gallon) or an EPA-registered disinfectant; let it soak about 5 minutes.
  3. 3Bag it: rodent (or trap and rodent) into a plastic bag, spray inside, seal it, then into a second bag. Never barehanded.
  4. 4Dispose: in the trash unless local rules require otherwise. Never leave it for the customer.
  5. 5Reset and record: disinfect or replace the trap, reset it on the same runway, record the catch on that device.
  6. 6Clean up: gloves off inside out, bagged; wash your hands before you touch your face, phone or food.
Never sweep or vacuum droppings dry. That puts particles in the air you breathe. Wet first, always (CDC guidance).
The real risks: leptospirosis (urine, through cuts or eyes), rat-bite fever, salmonella. In Florida, hantavirus is tied to cotton rats and rice rats, not house mice, roof rats or Norway rats, but we treat all rodent waste as hazardous.
Bitten, or ill within weeks of a heavy job? Tell your supervisor immediately and file a written report the same day. See a doctor and mention rodent exposure.
04 · Children, pets and emergencies

Assume a toddler and a dog will find every device

Prevention
  • Traps in the attic, in closed voids, or inside an enclosed station where kids or pets live
  • Rodenticide only inside locked, anchored, tamper-resistant stations outdoors
  • No glue boards where a child, pet or non-target animal can reach
  • Count your devices in and out; nothing left behind loose
  • Show the customer every device on the map, and ask about pets and children before you place anything
Child or adult got into bait? Call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 (911 if they're not breathing, seizing or unresponsive). Have the label or product name ready.
Pet got into bait? The owner calls their veterinarian or an animal poison control center right away. Give them the product name and active ingredient (482.2265). Anticoagulant poisoning can take days to show.
Finger or paw caught in a trap? Release the bar carefully and get medical or veterinary care if it's injured.
Any of these: tell your supervisor immediately and file a written report the same day.
