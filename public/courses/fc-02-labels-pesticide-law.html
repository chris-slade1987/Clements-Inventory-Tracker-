Why this course matters

The label is the law.

A misread label is not an honest mistake. It is a federal and state violation, and it can hurt a customer, a pet, a pond or you.

Federal and state penalties

EPA civil penalties under FIFRA run up to $24,885 per violation for a commercial applicator (2025 inflation adjustment). FDACS can fine up to $5,000 per violation and suspend or revoke a license or ID card.

The company's license to operate

Clements can only do pest control because the state licenses us. Every application you make is made under that license and our certified operator's name.

Customer trust

Customers let us into their homes around their kids and pets. Florida law gives them the right to ask what you applied. Your answer should be confident and correct.

03 · Signal words

One word tells you how acutely toxic the product is

Signal wordCategoryWhat it means
DANGER-POISONIHighly toxic if swallowed, inhaled or absorbed through skin. Red "POISON" and skull and crossbones.
DANGERICan cause severe, often permanent, eye or skin damage (corrosive).
WARNINGIIModerately toxic, or causes substantial but temporary eye injury or skin irritation.
CAUTIONIII · IVSlightly toxic, or mild to moderate irritation. Optional on Category IV.

The signal word reflects acute (short-term) toxicity of the product as packaged, based on its worst route of exposure. It says nothing about long-term effects or environmental risk. Every label must also say "Keep Out of Reach of Children."

04 · Directions for use

The most important section on the label

Where

The sites the product may be used on: indoor, outdoor, perimeter, lawn, crack and crevice. Not listed means not allowed.

What

The pests it is labeled for, and the application method: spray, bait, dust, granule, injection.

How much

Mix rate, application rate, and the maximum per application or per year. Never exceed it.

How often

Minimum days between treatments, weather limits, and when people and pets may return.

Every directions section opens with the same sentence: "It is a violation of Federal law to use this product in a manner inconsistent with its labeling."
Labels change. Mix from the current label and the Clements approved mix rate sheet, never from memory or an old handout. If they don't agree, stop and ask your supervisor.
05 · Label vs. Safety Data Sheet

Two documents, two different jobs

The label

  • Approved by EPA under FIFRA at registration
  • A legal document. Tells you how you may use the product
  • Directions, rates, sites, PPE, first aid, storage and disposal
  • Must be followed exactly. FDACS enforces it
vs

The SDS

  • Written by the manufacturer under OSHA Hazard Communication (29 CFR 1910.1200)
  • 16 standard sections: hazards, fire fighting, spills, chemical properties, emergency numbers
  • For safety and emergencies. It does not tell you how to apply
Keep both within reach for every product you carry. In an exposure, take the label to the doctor. On how to use the product, the label always governs.

01 · FIFRA and who enforces it

One federal law, enforced in Florida by FDACS

EPA

Registers

Reviews each product and use, approves the label and assigns the EPA Reg. No.

FDACS

Enforces in Florida

Under its agreement with EPA, FDACS inspects, investigates and penalizes misuse.

You

Follow the labeling

"Labeling" includes the container label, the attached booklet and any supplemental label.

FIFRA allows you to

  • Apply at a lower rate, concentration or frequency than the label
  • Treat a pest not named on the label, if the site and method are on the label
  • Use any equipment or method the label does not prohibit
  • Tank-mix products the labels don't prohibit, each at or below its label rate

FIFRA never allows you to

  • Exceed the label rate, or treat more often than the label allows
  • Apply to a site the label doesn't list
  • Mix, store or dispose of it other than as the label says
  • Ignore any precaution, PPE or restriction on the label

03 · Restricted-use pesticides

General use or restricted use

EPA classifies a product as restricted use (RUP) when it could harm people or the environment even when used as directed. Most products a GHP technician carries are general use. A general-use label has no classification statement.

RESTRICTED USE PESTICIDEDue to [reason, e.g. acute toxicity]. For retail sale to and use only by Certified Applicators, or persons under their direct supervision, and only for those uses covered by the Certified Applicator's certification.
Who may apply it: a certified applicator, or a noncertified person under their direct supervision. Under Chapter 482, that supervisor is the certified operator.
Before a noncertified applicator uses an RUP (40 CFR 171.201): at least 18 years old, trained within the last 12 months, given product-specific instructions, with the labeling available at all times.
Supervision: immediate communication with the certified applicator at all times, and the certified applicator on site whenever the label requires it.
Records: brand, amount, use, date and location of every RUP application, kept at the licensed location for at least 2 years (5E-14.142).
04 · Service containers and labels on file

If it leaves the original jug, it still needs a label

  • Brand or trade name
  • Common or chemical name and percentage of each active ingredient
  • EPA Reg. No. and EPA Est. No.

Florida requires a permanent, durable label or tag with the active ingredient and the information EPA requires or recommends (5E-14.106). The list above is the Clements standard.

Never put pesticide in a drink bottle, food container or anything unlabeled. Pesticide in a food or drink container is a classic cause of accidental poisoning.
Concentrates stay in leakproof, labeled containers and are locked whenever the vehicle is unattended.
A current specimen label for every product we use is kept at the licensed business location and shown to FDACS on request.
Keep the label and SDS for every product on your truck where you can reach them. The full requirements are in FC-05 and FC-07.

02 · Your ID card · 482.091

Required for everyone who performs pest control

  • Clements and the certified operator in charge apply for it within 30 days of your hire date.
  • Carry it whenever you perform or solicit pest control. Show it on demand to a customer or an FDACS inspector.
  • It must carry your current photo and signature.
  • It is renewed every year, and it expires the day you leave Clements. The company collects and destroys it.
  • You can hold a card for only one licensee at a time.
  • Office-only staff whose duties are clerical don't need one. Anyone who solicits or accepts pest control work in person or by phone does (5E-14.142).

04 · What you may and may not do

As an ID cardholder (not a certified operator)

You may

  • Perform pest control in the categories you're trained in, once you complete field training
  • Work under the supervision of your location's certified operator in charge
  • Use Clements products, equipment and materials on Clements customers
  • Solicit and accept pest control work for Clements
  • Apply a restricted-use product only under a certified applicator's direct supervision

You may not

  • Work alone before completing your 40-hour, five-day field training
  • Do side jobs, use your own products, or work as an independent contractor
  • Change the product, rate or formulation without consulting the certified operator
  • Perform WDO inspections without a WDO ID card endorsement
  • Perform fumigation without the required special ID card or endorsement, or supervise anyone

06 · Records and customers

Records and the customer's right to know

Records · 5E-14.142

Service records and contracts are kept at least 2 years, and the information is recorded within 2 working days of the application. Record the product, amount and where it went, accurately, every time.

Customer's right to know · 482.2265

On request, a customer gets the business name, your ID card number, the brand or common name of the product and its active ingredient, and the label's safety information.

Lawn notices and the registry

Lawn and exterior foliage applications need a posted notice, at least 4 by 5 inches, with our business name. Neighbors on the FDACS pesticide-sensitive registry get notice 24 hours ahead. The office tracks the registry list.

Honesty counts. False records or claims are grounds for discipline under Chapter 482. A false statement on a license or certificate application voids it, and violating the chapter is a misdemeanor (482.191).

02 · Storage, disposal and emergencies

The label covers the end of the job too

Leftover mix

Plan your mix so little is left. Apply what remains to a site the label lists, at or below the label rate. Never pour it on the ground, in a sink, or near a storm drain.

Empty containers

Triple rinse or pressure rinse as the label directs, and add the rinse water to the spray tank. Never reuse or refill a pesticide container. Dispose of it the way the label says.

Exposure or poisoning

Follow the label's First Aid section. Call Poison Control, 1-800-222-1222, with the label in hand. Tell your supervisor right away. Exposure response is covered in FC-03.

A spill is handled with the 3 Cs: Control, Contain, Clean up. Tell your supervisor right away and file a written report the same day. FC-05 covers spill response in full.
