Why this course matters

Every hole you miss is a callback you've already scheduled.

Trapping removes the rodents in the house today. The inspection decides whether more can get in tomorrow. It sets the tier, the scope of work, the price the branch quotes and the warranty Clements stands behind.

It's the warranty reference record

On Plus, every entry point 1/2 inch or larger is documented at the first visit. A missed entry point inside the original scope triggers warranty review later, and your photos are the proof of what was there.

Rodent control is regulated work

In Florida it falls under Chapter 482 and the General Household Pest and Rodent Control category. The customer is paying for a licensed company's judgment, not a guess.

It's how customers know we're different

Anyone can set traps. The technician who shows exactly how rats are getting in, and why, earns the Plus job, the renewal and the referral.

01 · The exterior route

Walk it the same way on every property

Before the truck door

Account history, prior rodent notes, the season. Gloves on; respirator ready; ladder checked.

Greet

Introduce yourself, explain the inspection, and let them show you where.

Perimeter

Slow, full circle: foundation, penetrations, burrows, rub marks, droppings, gnawing.

Landscape

Palms and limbs on the roof, dense hedges, fruit, feeders, pet food.

Roofline

From the ground first, with binoculars. Gable vents, soffits, returns, stacks.

Garage

Door seal corners, walls, stored boxes, seed and pet food.

Read the season. A September or October call about ceiling noise is the fall push-in, and it's usually roof rats: lead with the roofline. Canal-side properties lean Norway rat at ground level. RX-01 covers why.
Start near the reported spot, but never stop there. The noise is over the bedroom; the entry point may be on the other side of the roof.
02 · The roofline zone · roof rat primary

Look up first: most Florida attic jobs start here

Roof returns

Where the fascia turns the corner and meets the roof edge. Open, unfinished returns are the classic roof rat entry. Check every corner of the house.

Soffits and fascia

Separated seams, sagging panels, chewed corners, fascia pulling away. Grease staining along an edge means a regular route.

Gable vents

Torn or loose screens and gaps at the corners. Rub marks around a vent are a strong active sign.

Roof penetrations

Gaps around plumbing stacks and HVAC stacks through the roof or eave. Cracked or open sections of the ridge cap.

Utility entries up high

Cable, phone and electrical lines entering at the eave with open collars around them.

The highways

Palm fronds, oak limbs and vines touching or overhanging the roof. Not an entry point, but they lead straight to one.

Ground first, then ladder. Scan with binoculars, then go up only where you need a measurement or a closer look, following FC-06. Never step onto a roof without supervisor clearance; tile cracks and wet or metal roofs are slick.
03 · Walls, slab and garage · Norway rat and mouse primary

Low openings, and the ones you never close

  • A/C chases: the unfinished gap where the refrigerant line set enters the wall. One of the most common Florida entry paths.
  • Plumbing and utility penetrations: supply and drain lines, hose bibs, irrigation, electrical conduit, cable and internet lines.
  • Garage door seals: gaps at the corners of the bottom seal, chewed rubber, daylight under the door.
  • Foundation and stucco: cracks and gaps at the slab edge; burrows with clean edges and fresh soil (active) or leaves and cobwebs (old).
  • Doors and trim: gnawed sweeps, weatherstripping and trim corners.
Weep holes and weep screed: document, never seal shut. They let water drain out of the wall. Block one and you trap moisture in the wall, which leads to rot, mold and stucco damage. Mice can fit through some of them: photograph and record them, and flag them for the exclusion plan in RX-04.
Dryer, bath and kitchen exhaust vents: never apply any material. No screen, mesh, foam or cement under any Rodent Shield tier. Blocking them risks fire, carbon monoxide, moisture and appliance damage. Check that the damper closes, document it and recommend a repair if it doesn't.
Also never block: A/C condensate lines, gas lines, or anything a utility or inspector needs to reach. When in doubt, document and ask.

05 · Active or old?

Every entry point gets a status

Active points tell you where to target traps today. Old points are still weak spots that can open up again. Both go on the entry point list, both get photographed. Switch the view to compare.

    Field tip: with a gloved finger, a fresh rub mark smudges slightly; an old one doesn't. Not sure? Photograph it, date it, compare on the follow-up.

    01 · Before you go up

    An attic on a rodent job is a biohazard and a heat hazard

    PPE on before entry

    N95 or P100 respirator and nitrile gloves on an active or recent rodent account. Respirator use follows the fit and training rules in FC-03 (OSHA 1910.134).

    Never dry-sweep or vacuum

    Dry droppings and nesting stirred up go into the air you breathe. Wet first with disinfectant and let it soak, as the CDC advises. Inspect without disturbing.

    Heat

    Florida attics pass 120°F. Go early in the day, limit your time, drink water, climb down slowly. Dizzy or confused: get out and tell your supervisor.

    Access and footing

    Check the attic ladder or use your own (FC-06). Walk only on joists or decking. Wear a bump cap; watch for nails and low trusses.

    Stop-work signs

    Extreme heat, an unknown odor, sagging or damaged framing, exposed or chewed wiring. Don't go in, or come out. Note it and call your supervisor.

    Know the real risks

    Leptospirosis, salmonella and rat-bite fever come from urine, droppings and bites. In Florida, hantavirus is tied to cotton rats and rice rats, not our three house rodents, but the same precautions protect you.

    Bitten, scratched or exposed? Wash with soap and water, tell your supervisor immediately, written report the same day (RX-08).

    03 · Kitchen, utility and garage

    Inside, follow the droppings to the nest and the way in

    Kitchen first

    Under and behind the range, refrigerator and dishwasher (pull the kick plate with your light). Cabinet bases and the plumbing holes through them. Gnawed food packaging.

    Utility and laundry

    Water heater closet, air handler and its chase, behind the washer and dryer, wall voids and penetrations you can see.

    Garage

    Corners and wall bases, the attic hatch, stored cardboard, mulch or seed bags, and vehicles. Norway rats and mice favor ground level here.

    Mouse droppings inside? The nest is usually within 10 to 30 feet. Look for small caches of food nearby, then for the gap that lets them in.
    Droppings at a glance: mouse about 1/4 inch, pointed; roof rat about 1/2 inch, pointed and curved; Norway rat about 3/4 inch, blunt. RX-01 and PC-07 cover ID in depth.

    02 · Practice

    Seal, document, or refer?

    Seal = goes on the Plus scope of work. Document = record it, don't close it. Refer = someone else has to act first. Assume a Plus job unless the line says otherwise.

    03 · When it's not our job

    Exclusion can't hold on a broken structure

    Roofer

    Broken or missing tiles or shingles, failed flashing at a roof-to-wall joint, damaged roof decking, ridge vents that need replacing.

    Contractor or carpenter

    Rotted fascia or soffit wood, framing damage, large holes that need rebuilt wood rather than a patch over soft material.

    Electrician

    Chewed or exposed wiring. A fire hazard: tell the customer and your supervisor right away and recommend a licensed electrician.

    HVAC company

    Gnawed or crushed ductwork, a damaged air handler chase, a dryer vent damper that won't close.

    Branch manager

    Duplexes, quads and multi-unit buildings: partial exclusion doesn't work without a concrete firewall between units. Also heavy activity with wiring damage.

    Your supervisor

    Anything above your ladder limits, roof access, or an unsafe attic. Don't stretch the rules to finish the inspection.

    Refer means: photograph it, record it, and tell the homeowner it needs repair before or alongside our exclusion. Internal follow-ups go to technotes@clementspestcontrol.com and your branch manager.

    05 · The homeowner debrief

    Explain what you found, in general terms

    "You've got roof rats in the attic, and they're active. I found several entry points along the roofline and one at the A/C line, and your queen palm is giving them a bridge onto the roof. Here's how Rodent Shield handles that."
    Why general? Location-by-location repair instructions turn customers into do-it-yourselfers whose patches fail, and a detailed list in hand lets a competitor bid against our own inspection.
    Do and don't
    Doshow the evidence and the kind of entry points, and explain the biology behind it
    Dopresent Plus whenever you found entry points, including on a Basic job
    Dorecommend the conditions they can fix: trim palms and limbs, pick up fruit, move feeders
    Doname any repair a roofer, contractor or electrician must do first
    Don'tquote prices; the branch or a service advisor quotes after the inspection
    Don'tpromise results ("they'll never get back in")
    Don'tblame the home or the customer, or call any product "safe"
