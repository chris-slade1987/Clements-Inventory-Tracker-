Why this course matters

We are paid to solve the problem, not to spray.

Anyone can buy a can of bug spray. Customers trust Clements because our people find out why pests are there and make the fix last. IPM is how professionals think, and it is the foundation for every course that follows.

It's part of your license to work

Florida law (s. 482.091) requires every employee ID cardholder to complete classroom training in pesticide safety, IPM and pest control law, then continuing training every year.

Finding the source means fewer, better-placed applications: less exposure for families, pets, pollinators, waterways and you.

It keeps customers

Results that last mean fewer callbacks and customers who stay for years. A customer who understands what you found trusts you with the next problem.

01 · The role of pests in the ecosystem

Pests aren't "bad." They're in the wrong place, in the wrong numbers.

Outdoors, most of the animals we manage break down dead material, recycle nutrients and feed birds, lizards and fish. They become pests when their numbers or location conflict with people's health, property or comfort.

In nature · termitesRecycle fallen trees and dead wood back into soil
In a homeEat the framing, trim and cabinets of a house
In nature · cockroaches and antsScavenge dead insects and plant debris; food for wildlife
In a homeContaminate food and surfaces and trigger allergies and asthma
In nature · rodentsSpread seeds; prey for owls, hawks and snakes
In a homeSpread disease, chew wiring and nest in insulation
That's why our goal is to keep pests below the level where they cause harm, while protecting everything else: lady beetles, bees, butterflies and the animals that eat pests for us.

02 · What IPM is, and isn't

A decision-making process, not a product

"An effective and environmentally sensitive approach to pest management that relies on a combination of common-sense practices."U.S. EPA · INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES

EPA describes four tiers: set action thresholds, monitor and identify, prevent, and control, starting with the least risky methods that work.

Myth: "IPM means no pesticides." Pesticides are still a key tool. IPM uses them when and where they're needed, at label rates.
Myth: "IPM just means spraying less." It means thinking first. Sometimes the right call is more work: sealing, trimming, adding monitors.
Myth: "IPM is only for farms." It began in agriculture. Today it's the standard for homes, schools, restaurants and lawns.
The Clements version: inspect, identify, and solve the problem, not just the symptom.

04 · Inspection and identification

Wrong ID, wrong treatment. Every time.

German vs. American roach

German: small, breeds indoors in kitchens and bathrooms. Calls for sanitation, gel bait and monitors inside. American: large, lives outdoors, in sewers and damp areas. Calls for exterior work, moisture fixes and sealing.

Subterranean vs. drywood termite

Subterranean: mud tubes, nests in soil. Sentricon or liquid. Drywood: pellet frass and tiny exit holes, no soil contact. Spot or whole-home treatment. Sentricon never treats drywood.

Chinch bugs vs. drought vs. fungus

A yellow-brown patch in St. Augustine can be any of the three. Check the edge where brown meets green for chinch bugs, look for a fungal ring, and check irrigation coverage before you treat.

Inspect where pests live, not where they're seen: under sinks and appliances, behind outlets, attics, soffits, foundations, mulch beds, the edge of damaged turf.
Tools: flashlight, inspection mirror, glue-board monitors, a hand lens, and the Pest ID chart. Not sure what it is? Photograph it and ask your supervisor before you treat.

03 · Targeted chemical control

When you treat: right pest, product, place and amount

Right pest

Confirm the ID first. The pest decides the product and formulation.

Right product

Baits for insects that share food. Non-repellents where a repellent would scatter a colony.

Right place

Cracks, crevices, voids and harborage, where pests contact it and people, pets and pollinators don't.

Right amount

Label rate, never more. Extra product doesn't work better. It's illegal and adds risk.

Prevent resistance. Repeated use of one product, or sub-lethal doses, leaves only the toughest pests alive. Follow label rates and the product rotation your supervisor sets.
The label is the law. Every application follows the label's site, rate and directions. FC-02 covers labels and pesticide law in depth.

03 · Bring the customer in

IPM works best as a partnership

Half of the conditions you find are the customer's to fix: the leaky spigot, the pet food left out, the shrubs against the wall. Tell them what you found, what you did and what they can do, in plain language and without blame.

"I found ants trailing in from the shrub touching your back wall. I placed bait where they're feeding. If you can trim that branch back, it cuts off their highway into the house."
Do explain the conducive conditions you found and show the customer when you can.
Do say we use EPA-registered products applied according to the label.
Do pass questions about added services or pricing to your branch manager.
Don't call any product "completely safe," "natural so it's harmless" or "non-toxic." Claims must match the label.
Don't promise a pest-free home forever. Promise the process: inspect, treat, follow up.
