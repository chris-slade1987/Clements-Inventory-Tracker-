Why this course matters

Hundreds of gallons, under a family's home. Every drop has to be right.

A full liquid job is the largest pesticide application most technicians ever make. A gap lets termites through. A punctured duct or a treated well can hurt the people we came to protect.

Termites only need one gap

Subterranean termites forage through the soil around the whole slab. A skipped plumbing penetration or a short-volume segment is the way in, and the warranty call that follows.

The label is the law

Using a termiticide in a way that doesn't match its label violates FIFRA and Florida Rule 5E-14.106. Termite contracts, reports and notices are set by Chapter 482 and Rule 5E-14.105.

Customers trust what they can't see

Nobody watches what goes under the slab. Your diagram, records and photos are how the customer, your manager and FDACS know the job was done the way we said.

01 · Sentricon first, liquid when approved

Where full liquid fits in the termite program

Sentricon Always Active

The default for subterranean termites, post-construction and new construction. Bait stations eliminate the colony, with Corteva's damage bond while monitoring is current (CW-05).

Full liquid treatment

An existing structure with confirmed active subterranean activity, where a full liquid strategy is approved. Complete, label-compliant treated zones at every accessible soil, slab and foundation interface.

Liquid pretreat

New construction only when the builder specifies it (HUD, VA or contract language). A different job with its own rules; not covered here.

Never call a partial job complete. Spot treatments, skipped areas and construction limits are written up separately and never represented as a full treatment.
Pricing, payment terms and warranty for full liquid are not set yet. If a customer asks, the answer is "your branch manager will go over that with you."
02 · How a non-repellent works

Termites can't tell the soil is treated

Fipronil (Termidor) and imidacloprid (Premise) are common non-repellent active ingredients. Use only the termiticide Clements approves for the job, and its current label.

03 · Before the first hole

Know what's under the slab and in the ground

Utilities

Electrical lines and conduit, water and sewer lines. The label says don't apply until their location and type are known, and never puncture them.

Ducts in the slab

Heat or A/C ducts and vents in or under the slab. A drilled duct carries termiticide straight into the living space.

The slab itself

Post-tension cables, radiant heat, waterproofing, finished floors. If you can't confirm it's clear, you don't drill there; your supervisor decides.

Water

Wells, cisterns, sumps, French drains, storm drains and any surface water. Each has a label rule (Part three).

How to find them: ask the customer, look for floor registers and cleanouts, check the builder's plans or slab stamp, and get utility marking, permits or authorizations where they're required before invasive work.
Drilling concrete: eye and hearing protection, gloves per the label, and control the dust (concrete dust contains silica). FC-03 covers your PPE.

05 · Volume from the label

The label sets the volume. You do the math.

Calculate each zone from your site measurements and record it on the diagram. Deliver the label volume to every segment, and write down any segment that wouldn't take it (refusal, obstruction, saturation) and what was approved instead.

Worked example: Termidor SC (EPA Reg. No. 7969-210, 2024 accepted label). Trench, rod and sub-slab: 4 gallons of finished dilution per 10 linear feet per foot of depth. Hollow block voids: 2 gallons per 10 linear feet of footing. 0.06% = 0.8 fl oz per gallon (78 fl oz per 100 gallons).
Always use the label of the product actually on your truck. Rates differ between termiticides and change between label versions.

Label excerpt · example termiticide · EPA Reg. No. 7969-210

Termidor SC Termiticide/InsecticideCAUTION

Active ingredient: fipronil 9.1%

  • Labeled at 0.06%, 0.09% or 0.125% finished dilution
  • Apply 4 gallons of finished dilution per 10 linear feet per foot of depth
  • Trenches a minimum of 6 inches deep, no deeper than the bottom of the footing; need not be wider than 6 inches
  • Rod holes and slab drill holes no wider than 12 inches apart; rods do not extend below the footing
  • Footing more than 4 feet below grade: treat to a minimum depth of 4 feet
  • Do not apply within 5 feet of a well or cistern; 5 to 10 feet only by the backfill method
  • Do not treat water-saturated soil or during precipitation

02 · Trench and rod

A continuous zone from grade to the footing

  1. 1Trench along the foundation wall and around piers: at least 6 inches deep, no deeper than the footing
  2. 2Rod from the trench bottom toward the top of the footing, holes no more than 12 inches apart
  3. 3Apply the label volume for the depth, slowly and evenly; treat the trench soil and backfill as the label directs
  4. 4Replace soil and restore landscape within scope; don't leave treated soil exposed or piled on a hard surface
Never rod below the footing or through a vapor barrier or line. Hit something hard? Stop and find out what it is.
03 · Drill and inject

Under the slab, where the termites actually get in

Where to drill

Along expansion joints, cracks, and plumbing and utility penetrations through the slab, and through attached slabs (patios, porches, garage aprons) along the foundation. Holes no more than 12 inches apart.

How much

Sub-slab injection uses the per-linear-foot rate on the label. Bath trap openings and hollow block voids have their own rates. Watch for back-pressure and surfacing product, and record any refusal.

Plug and patch

Plug holes with non-cellulose material (no wood plugs) and patch to the company standard. Clean up dust and product before you move to the next room.

Inside the home: drilling through finished floors (tile, carpet, wood) is planned and agreed with the customer in advance. Pull carpet back, protect furnishings, and keep people and pets out of the room.
Bath traps: the opening in the slab under a tub drain is a termite highway. Access it through the plumbing access panel, or drill the slab beside it if there isn't one.
04 · Wells, cisterns and water

Groundwater in Florida is a few feet down

Sandy soil and a high water table mean termiticide can reach drinking water fast. These rules come from the example label and Rule 5E-14.106; check the label you're using.

  • Never apply into or let product reach a well, cistern, sump, sewer, storm drain, gutter or surface water
  • Within 5 feet of a sump pit and pump: no application
  • French drains: soil must be dry before you treat; no rodding through the slab within 24 inches of the drain
  • No treatment of saturated soil, during rain, or where runoff can carry product
  • Fill tanks with an air gap of at least 2 inches or an anti-siphon device (5E-14.106(5))
  • Concentrates locked up whenever the truck is unattended, even when you walk around back (5E-14.106(3))

06 · Leaks, cleanup and re-entry

Check for leaks while you work, and before you leave

Watch for it

Wet spots on floors or carpet, product surfacing at joints, wall bases or drains, or an odor indoors. Check inside during sub-slab work and again when you finish.

Stop and clean up

Stop the application. Control, contain, clean up (FC-05). The label requires every leak outside the treatment zones to be cleaned up before you leave the site.

Keep people out

Residents, children and pets stay away from contaminated areas until cleanup is complete. Tell your supervisor immediately and file a written report the same day.

Re-entry: people and pets stay out of the treatment area during application, and stay off treated soil until it has absorbed into the soil. People in the home are told to leave if they see signs of a leak. Follow the re-entry wording on your label.
Air systems: where the label calls for it (crawl spaces, plenums), turn off air circulation that moves air from the treatment area into living space before you treat.

05 · Follow-up and retreatment

If termites come back, the clock is set by rule

Written notice

The owner notifies us in writing of termite activity covered by the contract.

30 days

Inspect within 30 calendar days of the written notice (5E-14.105(9)).

90 or 180 days

Retreat within 90 days of discovery; 180 days for commercial or multi-unit, unless access is refused or the owner postpones in writing.

Document

Signed report, products, locations, photos; follow-up dates in PestPac.

Conducive conditions in writing. An exclusion clause can only be used if we gave the owner written notice of the condition within 60 days of finding it and 60 days to correct it. Note it every time.
Corrective treatment only after confirming the identification, access, label requirements and warranty coverage. Reinspection schedule and warranty terms for full liquid: your branch manager.
