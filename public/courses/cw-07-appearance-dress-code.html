Why this course matters

The customer decides about us before you say a word.

Trust at the door

A customer is letting a stranger onto their property, often into their home. A clean, complete uniform tells them you are who you say you are.

Safety in the field

Loose hair and jewelry can catch on hose reels, spreaders, ladders and attic framing. Clothing also stands between your skin and the products we use.

Professional, not the stereotype

What sets us apart from the stereotypical "bug guy" is professionalism. That starts with how we look, in a truck, at a gas station or at the front desk.

01 · Who wears what

Two dress standards, one company

Field uniform

Technicians and anyone required to wear a uniform. You are issued company shirts, pants, a belt, a jacket and a hat, and you wear them every minute you are on company time.

  • Company-issued shirt, tucked in
  • Company-issued pants and belt
  • Black work boots or black sneakers
  • Company-issued hat and jacket only

Office attire

Office and administrative employees, including customer service. You choose your own clothes within the business-casual standard, with denim allowed on Casual Fridays.

  • Khakis, slacks, skirts, dresses or capris
  • Dress shirts, blouses or polos
  • No athletic shoes or sneakers
  • Nothing too revealing or too tight
Not sure which applies to your role? Ask your supervisor on day one. This course covers both, because everyone at Clements should recognize a teammate who is in or out of policy.
02 · Grooming and hygiene · everyone

Clean, neat and odor-free

The handbook is direct: when you report to work, you and your clothing should be free of stains and of chemical, tobacco and body odors. Because of the nature of our business, frequent bathing and laundering are necessary.

Extremes of hairstyle and grooming can also project an unprofessional image. Management makes that call, so if you're unsure, ask first.

No stains

Change out of a uniform with product or grease on it. A spare in the truck helps.

No odors

Chemical, tobacco and body odor all reach the customer before you do.

Pressed, not wrinkled

Start the day in a clean, pressed uniform or neat office clothes.

Well groomed

Neat hair and grooming every day, in every role.

03 · How the policy is applied

Year-round, interpreted by management

In effect all year

The dress code stays in effect year-round unless management relaxes it for a season or a specific function. You'll be told when that happens. Don't assume.

Management interprets it

Management can interpret and apply the dress code to any situation the written policy doesn't spell out. Any deviation from company-issued attire needs your supervisor's approval.

Out of policy

An employee who doesn't follow the policy may be sent home to change or face other disciplinary action where appropriate.

The simple rule: ask before, not after. A quick question to your supervisor in the morning beats being sent home at noon.

03 · Footwear

Black work boots or black sneakers

Footwear is part of your uniform. It should be comfortable, sturdy and right for the job: black work boots or black sneakers, which give ankle support and are easy to keep clean.

Approval: footwear must be approved by the Director of Field Operations. Check with your supervisor before you buy.
Company support: Clements provides company-issued boots, or subsidizes the cost of approved shoes up to $75 per year. Your supervisor can tell you which option applies to you and how to request it.

Shoe covers inside every home

Put on shoe covers before you walk in. It protects the customer's floors and shows you respect their property.

When the label says more

Some product labels require chemical-resistant footwear plus socks. The label wins. FC-03 covers label PPE.

Not in the field

Brown or tan boots, colored sneakers, sandals, slides or any open-toe shoe.

04 · Hats, jackets and layers

The Clements logo stays visible

Hats

Optional, but if you wear one it's company-issued. Choose a moisture-wicking floppy brim hat for sun protection or a trucker-style cap with ventilated mesh.

Any other hat (a straw sun hat, a beanie on an unusually cold day) only with your supervisor's approval.

Company jacket

Issued for colder weather. Wear it over the uniform, zipped up, with the hood tucked away when you're not using it.

Keep it clean and in good condition, with the company name or logo clearly visible.

Your own layers

Anything extra you wear for warmth or any other reason goes under the company uniform, so the logo shows at all times.

Get your supervisor's permission first. Personal clothing should never be visible on the job.

05 · Safety rule · field positions

Hair secured. Jewelry off.

Clements has found that long hair and certain jewelry are a safety hazard in field work. This is a safety rule, not a style rule.

  1. 1Long hair must be styled or secured at or above your shoulders or collar.
  2. 2No jewelry in field positions, other than a wedding band.

Caught and pulled

Hose reels, spreaders, pump belts, ladders, bushes and attic nails can all grab loose hair, a necklace, a hoop earring or a bracelet.

Product gets trapped

Rings, bracelets and loose hair hold residue against your skin and get in the way of a proper glove fit.

When you apply a product, the label's PPE comes first. Most labels require a long-sleeved shirt and long pants. FC-03 covers what each label requires.
06 · Care and replacement

Keep it clean. Replace it on schedule.

Each branch has one designated ordering month a year to replace worn items. Ask your supervisor which month is yours.

Wash work clothes separately from household laundry, every time. It's standard pesticide safety practice and keeps residue away from your family.
Uniforms are company property. They are returned when you leave Clements, like keys, devices and other issued equipment.

02 · Your ID card · Florida law

Part of being dressed for the field

Under Florida law (Chapter 482.091, F.S.), anyone who performs or solicits pest control must carry a current FDACS employee identification card, with their photo and signature, on their person.

You may not perform pest control without it. Show it on request to the customer or a Department inspector. Keep your company identification visible, as your branch directs.
