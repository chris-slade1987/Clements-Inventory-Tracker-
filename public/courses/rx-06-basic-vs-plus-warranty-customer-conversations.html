Why this course matters

The job is half the work. The conversation is the other half.

A perfect seal still turns into a complaint if the customer expected silence on night one, or thought "warranty" meant "rodent-free forever." What you say at the kitchen table decides whether a Plus job ends in a renewal or a refund request.

Your words become the promise

The written service agreement controls what's covered. A verbal "you're guaranteed" that goes past it can be a deceptive claim under Florida's consumer protection law, and it lands on the company.

Renewals are built on trust

Rodent Shield is designed around Plus warranties that renew every year. Customers renew when what we told them on day one turned out to be true.

Calm customers stay customers

Rodents in the attic are stressful. A clear plan, "here's what happens next and when I'm back," prevents panicked calls to the office.

02 · Choosing the right tier

The inspection decides, not the budget alone

Recommend Basic when
  • There's active rodent activity but no confirmed structural entry points
  • The customer wants to start with elimination before committing to exclusion
  • Activity looks outdoor or perimeter-based, with no evidence of interior entry
Recommend Plus when
  • Entry points were found and the customer wants a lasting fix
  • The customer asks about a guarantee or warranty. Only Plus has one
  • The problem keeps coming back after other services
Basic inspection finds entry points? You must present the Plus upgrade before you leave the property. Same visit, every time.
Duplex, quad or any multi-unit home: flag it for manager review. Sealing one unit doesn't work without a concrete firewall between units.

04 · Explaining each tier

Plain language, no prices, no promises

Basic, in your words

"Basic is elimination. I inspect inside and out, set traps where the activity is, and come back five times to check and remove what we catch. You'll get a written summary of what I found. Basic doesn't seal any openings and doesn't carry a warranty."

Plus, in your words

"Plus is elimination and exclusion. I photograph and document every opening half an inch or larger, seal the ones in your agreement with cement-first materials, and trap right at those points. Five follow-up visits, a documentation package, and your choice of a 1-year or 3-year warranty on the sealed points."

Don't quote a price, even a ballpark. The service advisor quotes after the inspection.
Do explain the difference: Basic removes the rodents inside; Plus also closes the way in.
Pricing questions go to the service advisor or the branch manager.

01 · 1-year or 3-year

The choice is made once, at the original service

The 3-year can't be added later. Present both options at the original service and document the choice.
Both paths get a yearly inspection. The 3-year adds a booster service at the end of year 3.
Basic has no warranty and no renewal. No exceptions, no "partial" warranty.
02 · What keeps it in force

Six rules that keep a warranty alive

Renew on the anniversary

The office starts renewal outreach 30 days before each anniversary. Nobody waits for the customer to call.

30-day grace period

Coverage stays in force for 30 days past the anniversary. After that it lapses, and a returning customer starts over at new-customer pricing with no warranty.

Exterior bait boxes at renewal

Every Plus renewal, 1-year or 3-year, must include exterior rodent bait boxes. Present them as part of the package from day one.

Price locked

The warranty price is tied to the original contracted price. No year-over-year increases while it stays current.

Transfers with the home

If the home sells, the new owner calls within 30 days of the sale. No fee. Bait boxes are required, and the office opens a new account for them.

Documented at the start

The entry point PDF and photos from the original job are the warranty record. Undocumented work can't be backed. RX-07 covers this.

03 · What it covers, and what it doesn't

The warranty follows the documented scope

Triggers a warranty review

  • Activity continues in the areas we treated
  • A documented, sealed entry point has failed or been reopened
  • A missed entry point is found inside the original documented scope
  • New activity that matches the original service area

The branch manager decides, based on the documented scope, site conditions and whether the account is current.

Not covered

  • Entry points the homeowner declined, documented on the service agreement
  • Courtesy foam on gaps under ½ inch. Foam is not exclusion
  • Dryer, bath and kitchen exhaust vents. We never seal vents
  • Any Basic account, and any lapsed Plus account
  • Missed home-sale transfers (past 30 days)

Out-of-scope work found at a warranty or renewal visit is offered at a discount off standard pricing.

05 · The claims process

Document it. The branch manager decides it.

  1. 1Listen and record: what they hear or see, where, and since when. Don't say "covered" or "not covered."
  2. 2Inspect and photograph the area, and compare it with the original entry point PDF and photos.
  3. 3Call your branch manager the same day and send the details to technotes@clementspestcontrol.com so the office logs it.
  4. 4Tell the customer who calls next: "Your branch manager will review your agreement and call you."
  5. 5The branch manager decides warranty service or an out-of-scope offer, and the office schedules it.
Customer calls the office first? The CSR records the details and routes it to the branch manager. Recurring-customer callbacks are scheduled within 24 hours.
PestPac Mobile notes print on the customer's report. Write what you found, factually. Coverage opinions don't go in customer-facing notes.
Never change a date, a photo or a note to make a claim fit. Record exactly what you found.

03 · Words to use and never say

Describe the program. Don't improve on it.

Say

  • "Your warranty covers the entry points we documented and sealed. Here are the photos."
  • "You may hear them for a few nights. The ones inside are looking for the way out, and that's where the traps are."
  • "Activity should drop visit to visit. If it doesn't, I'll tell you and we'll adjust."
  • "Keeping the boxes outside and the renewal current is how we catch new pressure early."

Never say

  • "You'll be rodent-free forever," "guaranteed," or "they'll never come back"
  • "It'll be quiet tonight" or any fixed date for the last rodent
  • "Don't worry, that's covered," before the branch manager reviews it
  • Any price, starting price or renewal amount. And never call a bait "safe" or "non-toxic"
Why "never forever": rodent pressure outside the home never goes away. New damage, storms, renovations and landscaping can open new ways in. We close the ones we find and document, and we keep watching.

03 · Closing the job well

The walkthrough at the end of a Plus job

  1. 1Show the work. Walk through the entry point documentation and the before-and-after photos.
  2. 2Name what wasn't done. Declined points are on the service agreement and aren't covered. Vents are never sealed.
  3. 3Set the schedule. When you're back, what they may hear, and what to call about.
  4. 4Explain the warranty they chose and how renewal works, bait boxes included.
  5. 5Leave the summary and confirm the next visit date.
At the final follow-up, when the customer can see the problem is resolved, is the natural time to mention deodorization if there's odor. RX-05 covers add-ons.
Mention the transfer. If they sell, the new owner can take over the warranty within 30 days at no fee.
Don't know an answer? "Let me confirm that and get back to you." Then ask your branch manager. Never guess.
